# PARAMETRIC 2V GEODESIC DOME — RAW 1/8-LOG WEDGE PANEL + EDGE SPLINE SYSTEM

## 1. PROJECT PURPOSE

Build a complete interactive 3D application that models a 2-frequency geodesic hemispherical dome constructed using an unconventional panelized framing system.

The structural concept is:

* Start with round tree trunks.
* Split each trunk longitudinally into exactly 8 equal radial sectors.
* Do NOT plane the sector sides into rectangular lumber.
* Do NOT shave the 45° sector cross-section to conventional dome bevel angles.
* Preserve the natural 1/8-circle cross-section.
* Use the raw wedge members to create 40 independent triangular frames.
* Every triangular panel owns all three of its edge members.
* Therefore a geometric dome edge shared by two triangles contains TWO wooden members rather than one conventional shared strut.
* The two neighboring raw wedge members do not directly mate.
* A separate continuous spacer / spline / key / gasket occupies the space between them.
* The spacer determines or accommodates the required angle between adjacent triangle panels.
* Spacer implementations must include:

  1. rigid triangular spline,
  2. round rubber hose,
  3. compressible circular gasket,
  4. generic custom extruded profile.

The application must show the system as a real buildable assembly, not merely draw lines representing a geodesic dome.

---

# 2. PRIMARY GEOMETRY

Use a standard 2V icosahedral geodesic hemisphere.

Set the LONG geodesic edge length:

A = 72.000 inches

Use standard 2V chord factors:

A factor = 0.61803
B factor = 0.54653

Derive sphere radius from A:

sphere_radius = 72 / 0.61803

Expected:

sphere_radius ≈ 116.499 inches
sphere_diameter ≈ 232.998 inches
sphere_radius ≈ 9.708 feet
sphere_diameter ≈ 19.417 feet

Calculate short edge B from the ratio rather than storing an arbitrary value:

B = A × (0.54653 / 0.61803)

Expected:

B ≈ 63.670 inches

The application must expose A as a user-editable master dimension and recalculate the entire dome from it.

# 3. DOME TOPOLOGY

The final hemisphere must contain exactly:

40 triangular faces
26 vertices
65 unique topological edges

Face types:

10 × A-A-A equilateral triangles
30 × B-A-B isosceles triangles

Conventional unique edge counts:

35 × A edges
30 × B edges

The base perimeter consists of:

10 × A edges

Therefore interior shared seams consist of:

25 × A seams
30 × B seams

Total interior panel-to-panel seams:

55

These are the seams requiring spacers.

There are 10 exposed A edges around the base and these do not receive an adjacent-panel spacer unless an optional foundation interface is enabled.

# 4. PANELIZED WOOD MEMBER COUNT

Every triangle owns all three wooden edge members.

Do NOT deduplicate wooden members across neighboring triangles.

Total wooden wedge members:

40 × 3 = 120

By type:

A-A-A panels:
10 × 3 A members = 30 A members

B-A-B panels:
30 × 1 A member = 30 A members
30 × 2 B members = 60 B members

TOTAL:

60 × A members at 72.000 inches
60 × B members at approximately 63.670 inches

This duplication is intentional.

The topology has 65 abstract dome edges, but the physical wooden model contains 120 independent edge members.

# 5. RAW WOOD CROSS-SECTION

Default source trunk:

diameter = 8.000 inches
radius = 4.000 inches

Each trunk is split longitudinally through its center into 8 equal sectors.

Sector central angle:

360° / 8 = 45°

Each member is therefore an extrusion of a 45° circular sector.

Cross-sectional area:

A_sector = πR² / 8

For R = 4":

A_sector ≈ 6.283185 in²

Outer curved arc length:

arc = 2πR / 8

Expected:

arc ≈ 3.141593 inches

Straight chord between the two outside corners:

chord = 2R sin(22.5°)

Expected:

chord ≈ 3.061467 inches

Radial side lengths:

4.000 inches each

Generate the cross-section mathematically.

Do not approximate it as a triangle.

The curved outer tree surface must remain genuinely circular.

Use configurable radial segmentation for rendering, defaulting to at least 12 segments across the 45° arc.

---

# 6. ABSOLUTE ORIENTATION CONVENTION

This convention must be used everywhere and must never be reversed.

For every wooden wedge:

CURVED ORIGINAL TREE SURFACE = TOWARD DOME EXTERIOR / SKY

POINT OF THE SECTOR / ORIGINAL TREE CENTER = TOWARD DOME INTERIOR / SPHERE CENTER

In a cross-section perpendicular to a strut:

```
            DOME EXTERIOR
                  ↑

            curved arc
          ╭───────────╮
         /             \
        /               \
       /                 \
      /                   \
     ●
     ↑
sector point

   DOME INTERIOR
```

The bisector of the 45° sector should nominally align with the outward normal of its triangular panel.

The two flat radial-sawn sides lie at:

+22.5° and -22.5°

relative to that sector bisector.

---

# 7. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: IDEAL DOME EDGE VS PHYSICAL WOOD

Create the geodesic dome first as a zero-thickness mathematical mesh.

Each mesh edge is an IDEAL SEAM AXIS.

Do NOT automatically place both duplicated wooden members directly on this same axis.

Doing so would make the raw 45° sectors interfere with one another.

Instead:

1. Generate the ideal triangle face planes.
2. Generate an independent edge-member for each face.
3. Keep each edge-member longitudinal direction parallel to its corresponding ideal mesh edge.
4. Align its 45° sector bisector with that triangle's outward face normal.
5. Translate the member within the plane perpendicular to the edge until the selected spacer/contact solution is satisfied.

The spacer solver, rather than the ideal edge line alone, determines the final lateral separation of the two duplicated wooden members.

Maintain both:

* ideal mathematical topology, and
* physical offset assembly geometry.

Allow them to be displayed simultaneously.

---

# 8. LOCAL COORDINATE FRAME FOR EVERY PANEL EDGE

For every directed face-edge establish:

T = normalized longitudinal edge direction

N = outward triangle-face normal

S = in-plane vector perpendicular to the edge

S = normalize(cross(N, T))

Flip S when necessary so that it points toward the interior of the triangle.

Use:

local X = T
local Y = S
local Z = N

The wooden wedge is extruded along local X.

In the local Y-Z cross-section:

* sector point is toward -Z / dome interior,
* curved arc lies toward +Z / dome exterior,
* one radial face points toward triangle interior,
* the other radial face points outward across the panel boundary toward the neighboring panel.

Mark the outward-facing radial face as:

SEAM_FACE

Mark the other radial face as:

PANEL_INTERIOR_FACE

This labeling must be visible in debug mode.

---

# 9. COMPUTE DOME FOLD ANGLES FROM GEOMETRY

Do not use hard-coded spacer angles as the authoritative geometry.

For every interior topological edge:

* find the two adjacent faces,
* calculate their outward unit normals N1 and N2,
* calculate the actual angular difference between the face normals.

Define:

fold_angle = acos(clamp(dot(N1, N2), -1, +1))

This is the small angular change from one panel plane orientation to the neighboring panel orientation.

Expected validation values for a standard 2V dome are approximately:

A-type shared seam:
~18°

B-type shared seam:
~22.4° to 22.5°

The precise model-generated value controls the spacer.

Never rely solely upon naming conventions such as "dihedral", because different references use supplementary-angle definitions.

Show both:

small fold angle

and

traditional internal dihedral = 180° - fold_angle

in the inspector.

---

# 10. RAW-SECTOR EXCESS ANGLE

Each raw wooden sector contributes a 22.5° flat-face inclination relative to its panel normal.

Two raw sectors therefore geometrically represent:

45°

of total included sector-face angle.

For an interior seam whose required face fold is F:

raw_gap_angle = 45° - F

This gap angle is the basis of the spacer geometry.

Expected approximately:

A seam:

F ≈ 18°

raw_gap_angle ≈ 27°

B seam:

F ≈ 22.5°

raw_gap_angle ≈ 22.5°

Do not simply use these approximate numbers.

Calculate F from the actual adjacent face normals and derive the gap.

---

# 11. RIGID TRIANGULAR SPLINE MODE

Implement a rigid spline that runs continuously along the seam.

In the cross-sectional plane perpendicular to the seam, the spline is bounded by the two actual SEAM_FACE planes of the neighboring wooden members.

The spline should be generated geometrically from those actual faces.

Preferred algorithm:

1. Project both neighboring SEAM_FACE planes into the seam cross-section.
2. Represent them as two 2D lines.
3. Extend the lines until they intersect toward the dome exterior.
4. This intersection becomes the virtual spline nose/apex.
5. Follow each contact line inward by the desired contact depth.
6. Connect the two resulting endpoints to form the spline's interior base.
7. Extrude this profile along the length of the seam.

Default contact depth:

4.000 inches

This allows full contact with the complete radial face of the raw wood sector.

Expose:

spline_contact_depth

as an editable parameter from 0.25" through 4.00".

For an ideal symmetric approximation with full 4" contact:

base_width = 2R sin(raw_gap_angle / 2)

For R = 4":

A seam around 27°:
base ≈ 1.87"

B seam around 22.5°:
base ≈ 1.56"

These values are validation references only.

The actual polygon must be generated from the real seam geometry.

---

# 12. SPLINE ORIENTATION

The wooden wedge and the spline point in opposite radial directions.

WOOD:

wide curved portion = exterior
point = interior

SPLINE:

narrow nose = exterior
wide base = interior

Conceptual cross-section:

```
                EXTERIOR
                    ↑

      wood        spline        wood
    curved          ▲          curved
      ╲            / \            ╱
       ╲          /   \          ╱
        ╲        /     \        ╱
         ╲      /       \      ╱
          ●____/_________\____●

                    ↓
                 INTERIOR
```

This diagram is conceptual.

The application must calculate the real geometry from the neighboring faces rather than drawing a fixed symmetric icon.

# 13. ROUND HOSE / CIRCULAR GASKET MODE

Add a second spacer implementation consisting of a circular hose running longitudinally along every seam.

The hose is represented initially as a cylinder.

Cross-section:

circle tangent to both opposing raw SEAM_FACE lines.

Given:

gap angle = G
hose radius = r

For an ideal two-line wedge:

distance from virtual face intersection to hose center:

d = r / sin(G / 2)

distance from virtual intersection to each tangent point along the wood face:

t = r / tan(G / 2)

The tangent point must remain inside the available wooden radial face:

t ≤ R

Therefore the maximum uncompressed hose radius that remains fully within a 4" radial contact face is approximately:

r_max = R tan(G / 2)

Calculate this dynamically for every seam.

For approximately 27° A-seam gap:

r_max ≈ 0.96"
max uncompressed diameter ≈ 1.92"

For approximately 22.5° B-seam gap:

r_max ≈ 0.80"
max uncompressed diameter ≈ 1.60"

These are geometric reference values, not material recommendations.

Allow arbitrary hose OD and show a warning when the ideal tangent locations exceed the available wood face.

# 15. IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL BEHAVIOR OF A HOSE

The visualization must distinguish between:

ANGLE-DEFINING CONNECTOR

and

GAP-FILLING GASKET.

A rigid spline can geometrically index the panel angle if it has full mating surfaces and adequate fastening.

A round hose by itself generally does not uniquely establish the panel fold angle.

The panels may rotate around the hose unless separate clamps, bolts, straps, plates, or node connectors constrain them.

Therefore include two hose assembly modes:

HOSE_GASKET_ONLY

HOSE_WITH_MECHANICAL_ANGLE_CONSTRAINT

The second mode adds configurable fasteners or plates spanning the seam.

Do not imply that a soft hose alone is a rigid structural spline.

# 17. PANEL CORNERS

The edge spline system solves panel-to-panel seams.

It does NOT by itself solve the places where several triangular panels converge at one dome vertex.

The program must explicitly model this.

The 2V hemisphere has 26 vertices.

Expected topology:

6 interior five-valence nodes
10 interior six-valence nodes
10 base four-valence nodes

Because each triangular panel owns its own edge members, several physically separate wood ends and several spline ends converge around each mathematical geodesic vertex.

Do not hide this with intersecting meshes.

Create a NODE CONNECTOR subsystem.

# 18. NODE CONNECTOR / VERTEX KEY SYSTEM

For each mathematical dome vertex:

1. Collect every panel corner touching the vertex.
2. Collect every wood member terminating there.
3. Collect every spline or hose terminating there.
4. Establish a local radial axis from sphere center through the vertex.
5. Generate a connector volume capable of occupying the central remaining void.
6. Clip or terminate all seam keys cleanly against the connector.

Support node connector styles:

NONE
RUBBER_PUCK
WOODEN_KEY
METAL_PLATE
PROCEDURAL_FILLER
CUSTOM

The procedural filler should be generated from the actual surrounding member surfaces.

A practical implementation can:

* define an inner clipping plane around the node,
* trim member and spline ends,
* collect resulting boundary polygons,
* construct a convex or triangulated central connector.

Render five-way, six-way, and base four-way nodes distinctly.

This node system is critical because the 55 longitudinal seams terminate at the dome vertices.

# 43. MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The simulator must NOT turn this concept back into an ordinary rectangular-lumber geodesic dome.

The defining physical construction is:

RAW RADIAL LOG SECTOR
+
SEPARATE TRIANGULAR PANEL
+
DUPLICATED PANEL EDGE
+
CONTINUOUS EDGE SPLINE / GASKET
+
NODE CONNECTOR

The raw wood cross-section is a feature of the system, not a temporary shape to be machined away.

Only necessary longitudinal end cuts are allowed.

The two radial-sawn longitudinal faces and curved outer-log surface remain intact.

---

# 44. VISUAL DESIGN INTENT

The final result should visibly read as a dome made from trees split like pizza slices.

From outside:

the curved former-bark surfaces should form the visually dominant wooden ribs.

From inside:

the pointed radial portions of the sectors should project inward.

Between adjacent triangle frames:

the separate spline, key, or gasket should be visibly identifiable as a continuous third component.

The assembly should make this paradigm visually obvious without requiring an explanation.
