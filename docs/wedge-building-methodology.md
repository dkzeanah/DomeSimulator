# Core wedge building methodology

The wedge is both a framing member and a possible panel-infill stock. Plan the
frame, infill and foundation together, but keep their material ledgers separate.
The reference arithmetic is implemented in `infill.methodology`; books use its
`wm.*` tokens and the existing `cabin_every_piece` lesson reads the same functions.
The attached discussion is the source of the worked questions, not an independent
cut list or a physical test result.

## Pentagon and star

Use the regular polygon already generated by `infill.base.pentagon`. If its side
is S, its diagonal is D = phi S, where phi = (1 + sqrt(5))/2. The two diagonal
crossings give L = D-S and C = D-2L. Thus L+C = S and 2L+C = D.

The star has ten long and five short geometric segments; the perimeter adds five
sides. Fifteen interior pieces and twenty pieces including the perimeter are
different counts. In the ideal side-length stock plan, ten additional logs provide
ten long pieces, five required short pieces and five spare short-length remnants.
The five perimeter logs bring that ideal stock total to fifteen.

This complement is a centreline identity, not a zero-waste cutting guarantee. A
blank exactly S long cannot yield two exact centreline pieces summing to S and
also pay for a kerf. Member width, side-face bearing, pinwheel seating, trimming
and end cuts change the actual lengths. Use `infill.base` and its numbered solid
pieces and recipes for fabrication, not the golden-ratio labels alone.

A regular pentagon's included corner is 108 degrees and its direction change is
72. The symmetrical perimeter mitre is 36 degrees off square. A nominal
perimeter-to-perimeter side-face butt is 18 degrees off square in plan; a
diagonal-to-perimeter side-face butt at a 36-degree included angle is 54 off
square. These describe different joints. They are not bevel angles through the
timber, and they do not replace a solved compound cut. The current half-log
perimeter uses the solver's mitred corner, not the nominal side-face butt.

## Nominal edges, physical members and stock blanks

The raw-wedge solver supplies the nominal-chord reference. Each triangular panel
owns three members; the shared-edge dome and independent-panel dome consequently
have different member counts. Sum nominal panel perimeters for the drawing's
footage, sum the solver's member cut-stock lengths for physical framing, and
reserve full blanks separately. Do not replace a raw-stock requirement with the
shorter finished total. Frame offcuts can reduce infill purchases only after a
course-by-course cutting and reuse plan assigns them useful jobs.

The attachment rounds the short chord before calculating. The shared model keeps
the solver's full precision, so its area and footage differ slightly from those
rounded examples. The reference long dimension is a nominal chord. The Triangle
Infill tool's maximum-finished-member dimension is another scale; its solved tray
and cut list remain authoritative for the actual pictured assembly.

## One exposed face and paired infill

For log diameter d and n radial splits, the ideal outer chord is
w = d sin(pi/n). It is not automatically the installed continuous coverage.
For each triangle, area A and perimeter P give inradius r = 2A/P. The attached
planning assumption moves each boundary inward by w/2, giving opening area
A_open = A max(0, 1 - (w/2)/r)^2. Sum over the solver's panel classes. This is a
declared planning inset, not the actual tray opening: use `PanelType.opening(z)`
for the selected installed face of the solved frame.

If TWO wedges continuously cover a pair pitch c on ONE exposed face, the total
wedge length is L_infill = 2 A_open 12/c, with square feet and inches converted
explicitly. The attached overlap scenario assumes c = (2-p)w for p from zero to
one. Its endpoints are A_open 12/w and 2 A_open 12/w. They are conditional
quantity bounds under that assumed coverage rule. Measure continuous coverage
on a sample before claiming a workable minimum. A wide bowtie envelope can
contain gaps; point contact does not demonstrate fastening or a weather seal.

The infill is a design option within the core method, not a requirement that
every building wear a solid wooden skin. A facing can still be a sheet or another
qualified panel. Course ends, kerf, bark, taper, trimming, defects, drying,
movement, seals and actual packing add stock beyond this area estimate. The
existing infill solids, layouts and stock allocator provide the next calculation.

## Orientation and cavities

State which axis is inward and which face must be continuous. The tool's solved
alternating-point flip-flop, two wedges with both points toward the dome interior,
and opposed-point chord triangles are different arrangements.

For the separate ideal opposed-point experiment, let R=d/2 and h=R cos(pi/n).
Engagement e gives envelope depth h(2-e), contact length Re, and an assumed
periodic-cell solid fraction 1/(2-e). Its void fraction is the complement. The
two chord triangles retain the same wood area as they slide. This envelope depth
is not the continuous face pitch and is not used to reduce the timber takeoff.
The experiment omits the raw sector's curved back and saw kerf. It does not
establish strength, R-value, usable ventilation, or achievable same-inward-point
seating. Test orientation, contact, fixings and face closure together.

## Harvesting ledger

At n splits, each uniform usable trunk foot ideally yields n wedge feet. Reserve
the framing blanks first: L_frame_trunk = member_count blank_length/n. Add the
infill trunk equivalent L_infill/n, then divide by the stated usable reference
trunk length and round upward. Count usable trunk at the required diameter,
not the height of a standing tree. Whole-tree equivalents are a stock estimate,
not a guaranteed harvest count. Taper and defects can change the diameter, chord
width, course pitch, yield and tree count at the same time.

## Teaching and verification

`py -3.12 -m infill.methodology` proves the polygon intersections and identities,
checks quantity bounds and raw-blank reserves, and prints the computed reference
table. `py -3.12 -m infill --selftest` includes these checks alongside the actual
solids and cut recipes. The books and lexicon use the same token table. The
existing infill film now declares this model before its footage, coverage and
tree-budget worksheets, and explains the pentagon stock identity alongside the
solver's actual foundation. Inspect its stills before making a new film through
`py -3.12 -m film make cabin_every_piece`.
