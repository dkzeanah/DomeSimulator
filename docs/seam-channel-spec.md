## Hubs (service junctions; the frame itself stays hubless)

6 5-way, 10 6-way, 10 rim. As built the channels close to a point at each vertex: fit a printed rosette junction box on the inside of the vertex. With the 4 in duct spacer each vertex opens to a node 10.9 in across (5-way), 11.2 in across (6-way).

## Making the key

1. **Profile.** One per seam type: the V less its nose, 0.50 in down the faces, with 3 mm walls.
2. **Split it** on the seam's centre plane. Each member carries its own half, screwed to its sawn face before the panel is built; two panels meeting close the channel.
3. **Print** in pieces no longer than the bed (250 mm): 8 per stick, 7 per stick, joined end to end.
4. **Whole dome:** 110 half-keys, 2 profiles, 820 pieces, 121 kg of PETG, about $2,425, 1,768 printer hours. **Per seam:** 2.2 kg, 32 h. Print the seams that carry services; cut solid keys from the offcuts for the rest.
5. **Bolts** cross near the ridge, where the V is narrowest; route services in the wide belly below them.
6. **No pressure fittings inside a seam.** Run PEX continuous; make every joint in a rosette you can open. Whether cable may run in this chase is the inspector's call.

## Assumptions

- **fill_fraction** = 0.4 of area -- rule borrowed from conduit fill (NEC Chapter 9, Table 1): more than two conductors may fill 40% of a raceway. Used here as the packing allowance for a mixed bundle -- the channel is not a listed raceway
- **key_wall_in** = 0.11811 in -- assumption: a printed key wall of 3 mm, stiff enough to carry the bolt bosses and the clips
- **printer_bed_in** = 9.84252 in -- assumption: the longest axis of a common desktop printer, 250 mm
- **filament_g_per_cm3** = 1.27 g/cm3 -- nominal: density of PETG, which takes outdoor heat better than PLA
- **filament_usd_per_kg** = 20 USD/kg -- assumption: a spool of PETG bought by the kilogram
- **print_mm3_per_s** = 15 mm3/s -- assumption: a 0.4 mm nozzle laying walls at a brisk but reliable speed
- **quiet_air_fpm** = 400 ft/min -- rule of thumb: keep air in a small residential duct under about 400 to 600 ft/min or it is heard; the low end is used
