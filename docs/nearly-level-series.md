# Nearly Level

An original PG-13 family DIY comedy created for sketch-gpt. The dome is the
family's recurring home and workshop setting. The stories are about pride,
siblings, household chores and internet-age shortcuts; they work without a
dome sales lecture. Familiar multi-camera family-comedy energy, original cast,
dialogue, storylines and visual identity.

## Pilot: Shelf Esteem

Cal announces a simple shelf project with no apps or drama. Theo supplies an
anxious smart shelf. Cal tries to level his selfie camera. June recognizes an
entire reaction-channel business model. Milo accidentally reinvents the table.
Mara redirects the family toward doing the dishes. Cal's response sets up the
next episode: a Bluetooth dishwasher nobody asked for.

The first production is a 66-second captioned animatic: ten dialogue shots and
a short closing series card. It demonstrates the characters, camera moves,
reaction timing and editing workflow. It is not represented as a finished voiced
television episode.

## Comedy and visual grammar

- Cold open with a modest task and wildly inflated confidence.
- A/B conflict between practical household needs and attention-seeking shortcuts.
- Reactions get a beat before the next line; a push-in can carry a realization.
- Meme captions tag the joke without inventing a second plot.
- Use wide family coverage, clean singles, reaction shots, lateral reveals and
  occasional exaggerated dolly zooms. Save fast moves for a clear comic reason.
- Affection survives the argument. The family repairs its own mess.
- Mild language can punctuate a joke; avoid slurs, explicit sexual material,
  graphic violence or unsafe tool stunts framed as instructions.

Natural dome placement comes from repeatable views of a pleasant, occupied
home: family space, light, workshop routines, a table that acquires jobs. There
are no invented performance claims, unmeasured price promises or subliminal
frames. A later promotional release can identify its DomeSim production context.
