# My Own Home, My Own Dome

*A rhyming picture book of the wedge-dome build, for reading aloud.*

The manuscript: one stanza per spread. Stanzas are separated by a blank line;
zeanahlab.com's `/kids` page shows the first few (`website/scripts/export_facts.py`
reads this file). The illustrated PDF is still to be made.

---

In a town full of boxes, all stacked up so tall,
lived a girl named Pip Pine who said, "Boxes? Not all!
A box has four corners, and corners grow dust.
I want a round home. A round home! I must!"

"A dome!" Pip decided. "A dome I will build,
with a roof like the sky when the sky is all filled
with the curve of the morning, the bend of the sun --
a home with no corners! Not one! Not a one!"

So she marched past the lumber yard, stacked to the eaves
with boards wrapped in plastic and tags on their sleeves.
"No thank you," said Pip. "I won't buy all my sticks.
I will find them, and split them, and fit them like bricks."

At the edge of the forest a fallen pine lay,
just as round as a barrel and long as a day.
"Hello, Mister Log," Pip said, tipping her hat.
"Would you like to be home? Would you, please, become that?"

The log didn't answer (most logs never talk),
so Pip took the silence as "Yes!" and a "Walk!"
She fetched one good saw and one bright chalky line,
one level, one tape, and one lunch box. Just fine.

Zzzip! went the saw, and the log split in two.
Zzzip! went the saw, and the twos split anew.
Then halves became quarters, and quarters made eight --
eight wedges like pie slices stacked on a plate.

"A wedge," Pip declared, "is a bone of my dome!
Each wedge is a rib of my own little home.
I'll cut them to length -- not too short, not too long --
for a strut that's too short makes a dome that goes wrong."

She measured one strut, just the longest of all,
and she wrote it in chalk on the side of the wall.
"From this one little number," said Pip with a grin,
"I can count out the whole of the house I'll live in!"

"How tall? And how wide? And how much will it weigh?
How many triangles? How long will it stay?
It's all in the number. The number's the key.
If I know my long strut, then my dome will know me."

Then wedge after wedge went in pinwheels of three,
each one leaning on one, like a hug, don't you see?
No nails at the corners, no hubs made of tin,
just a wedge holding wedge holding wedge holding in.

Triangle! Triangle! Up the ring rose,
little triangle windows in tidy round rows,
and the rows bent together and curved toward the top,
till the very last triangle -- click! -- made it stop.

The neighbours came running. "What IS that?" they cried.
"It is round on the outside! It's round on the inside!"
"It's my home," Pip said proudly. "My dome. It is mine.
I can tell you its weight. I can tell you each line."

"I can tell you the wood and the screws that it took.
I can tell you the hours -- they're all in my book.
There's no secret, no guess, no 'oh, roughly, we think' --
just numbers you count, written down in black ink."

Then a boy from the boxes came up with a frown.
"My dome has a hole, and a panel fell down."
Pip asked, "What's your long strut?" He said, "Same as yours!"
"Then my panels fit yours, like the doors fit their doors!"

So the kids with same domes started up a big club,
where they swapped out their panels and shared tools and grub,
and they learned from each other which way the wind blows,
and which wedge goes where, and how everyone grows.

Now the town full of boxes has domes on the hill,
little round ones and big ones, and more coming still.
And at night, when the sky makes a dome of its own,
Pip curls up in hers. "My own dome. My own home."
