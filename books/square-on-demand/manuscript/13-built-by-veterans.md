# Built by Veterans

This method was written by a veteran, and it fits the way a lot of veterans
already work.

On one night in {{sq.vets_when}}, {{sq.vets_pit}} veterans in this country had
no home of their own, and {{sq.vets_out}} of them were unsheltered. A building
that one crew can frame in about a working week, from timber that is already
standing, is one answer to that number. A village of them is a better one.

## Why it fits

* **It is a system.** One body, the same parts every time, and a maintenance
  schedule rather than a renovation: the skin recoated on a calendar, the core
  serviced, a panel swapped when it wears. Anyone who has kept equipment running
  to a checklist already knows how to keep one of these.
* **It is crew work.** Felling, splitting, pairing and raising are jobs for a
  small team with a safety lead and assigned roles, and a crew can be taught the
  whole method in a season.
* **The skills carry.** Saw work, measuring, rigging, fabrication, repair --
  the trades many people learned in the service are the trades this method uses.
* **It is a place, not a unit.** Buildings on serviced pads, with a shared shop,
  make a community: the way a base works, without the base.

## A village

The Veterans Community Project in Kansas City built a village of {{sq.village}}
small homes around a shared community centre. A village of wedge-built
buildings could follow the same pattern: the homes, a workshop where the
wedges are paired and planed, and the shop as the place where residents learn a
trade and keep their own homes running.

It is designed to sit beside the programs that already exist to house veterans
-- {{sq.programs}} -- and to be built and maintained by veterans. Whether any
particular project qualifies for any of them is for those programs to decide.

:::box do "For a crew leader"
Run the build as a training course. One session at the log on felling and
safety. One on splitting. One on pairing, shimming and planing. One on the jig.
Every member of the crew does every job once, and leaves knowing how to build
the next one.
:::
