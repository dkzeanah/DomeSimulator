# A Zome From Wedges

A zome is built from rhombus panels set in rows that twist around a centre. The
Complete Edition works one through: a zome of {{zm.panels}} panels in {{zm.rows}}
rows of {{zm.per_row}}, {{zm.rim_ft}} feet across its rim and {{zm.height_ft}}
feet tall, every panel side {{zm.side_in}} inches. This chapter builds the same
zome with wedge lumber.

[[image:plate-zome-model.png|The Complete Edition's zome: each row is one panel, framed flat, repeated round the ring.]]

## Panels without a bevel

Each panel is four sticks in a pinwheel, framed flat, the way a dome's
triangles are. A rhombus's corners alternate sharp and blunt, and all four
sticks of a panel come out the same length with the same cut at each end. No
stick is bevelled. Panels meet only along their inside edges, which is where
they are fastened.

The panel sticks can be skewed pairs, planed square only where they meet the
next panel's face, or wedges stood on their backs, with their faces toward the
inside.

## The gap is a wedge

Square-edged panels that fold away from each other leave a V-shaped gap on the
outside of every fold. The Complete Edition fills it with a wedge-shaped block,
ripped to half the fold on each face. With wedge lumber the block is not ripped
from a board: **it is a wedge, split from a log the right number of ways.** A
wedge's point is three hundred and sixty degrees divided by the split, and the
gap's V is the fold, so the split each row wants is three hundred and sixty
divided by its fold:

    {{sq.t_zome}}

The folds run from {{sq.zome_fold_lo}} to {{sq.zome_fold_hi}} degrees, so the
blocks want logs split between {{sq.zome_n_lo}} and {{sq.zome_n_hi}} ways. Those
splits cannot be made by halving. Mark them on the end grain with a cardboard
template cut to the angle, cut to the marks, and every block for that row is
the same.

Halving alone gets as close as sixteenths, and the last column shows how far
off that leaves each row. On a small zome that is near enough, with the
difference taken up by the fastening. On a large one, cut the true split.

## Why a zome suits wedges

* Its panels are flat and identical within a row, so they are built on one jig,
  like a dome's.
* Its folds are filled by wedges, which the log supplies at any angle you
  mark.
* Its rows slope, so roofing laps down it like shingles, and the V seams between
  panels drain the way the roof does.

:::box watch "Watch out"
The top rows of a zome sag under their own weight until the ring closes. Brace
them during assembly, and have an engineer check the finished frame for snow and
wind before anyone lives in it.
:::
