# Joints Wedges Make Easy

Wedge lumber has flat faces at known angles, a point, and a back. Each of those
is the start of a joint that square lumber has to be cut to make.

## The pinwheel

The dome books' joint. Where several members meet at a corner, none of them
runs into the corner: each stops a little short and bears on the side of the
next, round the corner like the blades of a pinwheel. Nothing meets at a point,
so nothing has to be cut to a compound angle there. It works with sticks of any
section, which is why it suits wedges.

## The slant lap

Two wedges laid slant to slant already lap along their whole length. Offset one
by a foot and you have a scarf: a long member made of short wedges, the joints
staggered so no two fall together. Bolt or peg through the lap.

## The bird's mouth on the point

A wedge's point is a ready-made bird's mouth. Lay it point-down over a square
plate and the plate's corner sits in the angle between the two faces. Cut the
point back by a little and it seats on a square corner of any size.

## The keyed seam

Two wedges side by side on their backs open a V between them. Drive a diamond
or a thinner wedge into the V and it locks them together. Leave it open and it
is a channel -- which is the next chapter.

## The butterfly and the dowel

A pair glued slant to slant can be stitched across its joint with a butterfly
key let in across the seam, or held with dowels through both wedges. Neither
needs a square face to reference from: the joint line is the reference.

## Flush-cut in place

Wedges are not cut to finished length on the bench. Set them in the frame long,
then run the saw across the joint so both pieces are cut by the same pass.
Whatever the angle is, both faces get it, and they meet.

## Jigs that hold a triangle

Every one of these joints is easier with a jig that holds a triangular section
still: two boards screwed into a V at the wedge's point angle. A wedge dropped
into it sits on its faces, back up, and cannot roll. One jig per split angle
does every wedge from that split.

:::box watch "Watch out"
Fasteners near a wedge's point split it. Keep screws and bolts in the thick part,
toward the back, and drill pilot holes in the thin part every time.
:::
