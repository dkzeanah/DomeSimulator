# Safety at the Log

Everything in this book starts with a chainsaw in a log. That is the most
dangerous part of it, and this page is the one to read twice.

* **Never alone.** Two people, minimum, at every session: one cutting, one
  watching, who knows how to stop the saw and call for help.
* **Wear the kit.** Chainsaw-protective chaps or trousers, a helmet with a mesh
  visor and ear defenders, gloves and boots. Ripping along the grain throws long
  strings of wood, not chips.
* **Hold the log still.** The first cut is the only one where the log can roll.
  Cradle it on blocks or in a pair of chocks; after the first cut, every piece
  has a flat face to sit on.
* **Rip with the right chain.** A chain filed for ripping cuts along the grain
  far faster and more safely than a crosscut chain forced to do it.
* **Keep the bar oiled and the chain sharp.** Ripping buries the bar for minutes
  at a time.
* **Split with the saw, always.** This method does not use axes, mauls or
  splitting wedges driven into the log. The saw makes every cut, along a line you
  marked.
* **Fasten in the thick part.** A screw near a wedge's point splits it. Drill
  pilot holes.
* **Have it checked.** Anything that carries people, snow or wind is checked by
  a licensed engineer before it is built, and members made from paired wedges are
  not graded lumber.

:::box watch "One rule above all"
If you are tired, stop. The owner's timed sessions at the log are
{{hr.session}} hours long, with good reason.
:::
