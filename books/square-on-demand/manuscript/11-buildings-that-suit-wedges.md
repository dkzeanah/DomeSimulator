# Buildings That Suit Wedges

Some buildings want boards. Others are built from triangles, octagons, slopes
and posts -- and those are the ones wedges were made for. Here they are, with
the split and the way of working that suits each.

## Domes

The reference. A geodesic dome is all triangles, so its members are pushed and
pulled along their length and the stick's section does not matter. The dome
books frame a cabin from {{dome.members}} wedges, split {{cut.splits}} ways,
joined in pinwheels. Nothing is squared.

## Zomes

A zome is a dome made of rhombus panels in rows, a spiral rather than a
sphere. Its panels can be framed flat, and the gaps where they fold are filled
by wedges. It has a chapter of its own, next.

## A-frames and trusses

Triangles again. Rafters can be skewed pairs, whose faces already lie at a
roof pitch. Collar ties and struts stay as wedges. Only the plate the frame
stands on needs to be square.

## Octagons: gazebos, round decks, yurt floors

An eighth's point is the octagon's centre angle. Radial joists of trimmed
wedges meet at the middle without a measured cut, and the rim is the wedges'
backs.

## Sheds and greenhouses

A shed roof at {{sq.pitch12}} in twelve is twelfths' natural slope. The walls
want square posts at the corners -- quarters back to back -- and wedges or
skewed pairs between. Greenhouse glazing bars are wedges on their backs: the
glass seats on two sloping faces and the V between bars drains condensation.

## Posts, fences and decks

Quarters back to back make the heaviest square in the book, with nothing to
plane. Fence rails are wedges on their backs, shedding rain both ways. Deck
joists are skewed pairs set on edge, decking can be sixteenths paired into
boards.

## Furniture

Benches, trestle tables and stools are triangles and posts. A bench seat from
two skewed pairs, legs from quarters, braces from wedges.

:::box why "The pattern"
Square the pieces that meet the level world: posts, plates, door and window
frames. Leave everything that is triangulated, sloped or radial as wedges. Most
of a building in this list is the second kind.
:::
