# Use the Skew

Planing the skew out is one choice. The other is to keep it, because buildings
are full of angles that are not ninety degrees, and the wedge brings its own.

## The skew is a roof pitch

A skewed pair laid flat has its short faces sloping at half the wedge's point
angle -- {{sq.shim_deg}} degrees for an eighth. As a roof pitch that is a rise of
**{{sq.pitch8}} in twelve**: almost exactly the five-in-twelve of an ordinary
roof. Use pairs of eighths as rafters or purlins and their faces already lie at
the slope, so sheathing and roofing seat flat without a single bevel cut.

Other splits give other pitches:

* quarters: {{sq.pitch4}} in twelve, a steep forty-five degree roof;
* sixths: {{sq.pitch6}} in twelve;
* twelfths: {{sq.pitch12}} in twelve, a low shed roof;
* sixteenths: {{sq.pitch16}} in twelve.

Choose the split for the roof you want, and the wood arrives cut to the pitch.

## The octagon is already in the log

An eighth's point is {{sq.theta}} degrees -- the angle at the centre of an
octagon. Eight trimmed wedges laid points-in close into an octagon with every
joint the log's own angle, and every outside corner the {{sq.oct_interior}}
degrees an octagon needs.

[[figure:octagon|Eight trimmed wedges, points in. An octagonal floor, table or gazebo deck is the log put back together, with its pieces flat.]]

That makes octagonal decks, gazebo floors, round tables, and the radial joists
of a small round building into the most natural thing to build from wedges:
the members meet at the centre without a single angle being measured.

## Other places to keep the angle

* **Bevel siding.** A wedge sawn along its length into thin slices gives
  tapered boards that lap like clapboard, thick edge down.
* **Trusses and A-frames.** A triangulated truss has members meeting at angles;
  the wedge's faces seat against each other without a square edge in sight.
* **Sloped sills and drip edges.** A wedge laid on its back sheds water both
  ways.
* **Fences and rails.** A wedge rail on its back is a capping that does not hold
  rain.

:::box why "Plane it, or keep it"
Square where the building is square: posts, plates, door and window frames,
anything that meets a level floor. Keep the skew where the building already
slopes. Every face you do not plane is wood you keep and an hour you save.
:::
