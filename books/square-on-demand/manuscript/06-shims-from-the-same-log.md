# Shims From the Same Log

Sometimes a wedge has to meet a square world for a short distance: where a
strut lands on a level plate, where it passes a door jamb, where a triangular
member turns into a square one. You do not have to square the whole stick for
that. Shim it where it needs to be square, and leave it a wedge everywhere else.

[[figure:shim|An eighth lying on its back, with a thin wedge set against one face. The shim's angle is half the wedge's point, so the face it makes is plumb.]]

## The shim is a thinner wedge

An eighth lying on its back presents two sloping faces, each at {{sq.beta}}
degrees to the ground. To make one of them plumb you need to add
{{sq.shim_deg}} degrees -- exactly half the wedge's point. And a wedge with a
{{sq.shim_deg}} degree point is what you get when you split a log
{{sq.shim_split}} ways. **The shims come from the same log: halve one more
time.** Over the full face of a reference wedge, that shim is
{{sq.shim_thick}} inches thick at its heel.

## Where to shim

* **At a seat.** Where a strut bears on a sill or a plate, shim the last foot so
  the bearing is square and full-faced. The rest of the strut stays a wedge.
* **At a transition.** Where a triangulated frame meets a square-framed wall, a
  pair of shims either side turns a wedge into a stud for the length of the
  joint.
* **Along the axis, at points.** A long wedge can be shimmed at intervals --
  every place a cross member or a fixing lands -- instead of being planed square
  for its whole length.
* **To take up the skew.** A skewed pair laid on a flat surface rocks on one long
  corner. A tapered shim under its low side makes it sit square without
  planing.

:::box do "Make a set of shims"
Take a short, straight section from the top of the log. Split it {{sq.shim_split}}
ways. Trim the backs flat. You now have a box of identical {{sq.shim_deg}} degree
tapers, matched to every wedge from that tree.
:::

:::box why "Why half the point"
A wedge on its back has corners of {{sq.beta}} degrees at the ground. A plumb
face needs ninety. The difference, ninety minus {{sq.beta}}, is always half the
point angle, whatever the split.
:::
