# Against the Mill

else does it, and where it has to say what does not help it.

## What these numbers say

**Wedges kept as wedges beat the mill by a wide margin**, at every size.
That is the method's whole advantage, and it is why the dome frame comes from
so few trees.

**Pairs planed into boards beat the mill on the reference log.** Split
{{sq.best_n}} ways, the log keeps {{sq.best_kept}} per cent as boards against the
mill's {{sq.sawn_pct}}.

**Pairs planed square do not beat the mill on a good log.** On logs of ten inches
and more, the simulator's mill keeps more than eighths paired and planed square.
Squaring every member would give the advantage away. That is the reason for the
title: square only on demand, and only the members that need it.

The simulator's mill is a simple one. It packs true two-by-fours in rows and
sizes each section on its small end. A good sawyer cutting mixed sizes does
better; the project's books allow such a mill about {{pine.mill_pct}} per cent.
The wedge's lead over a good mill is smaller than its lead over the simulator,
and it is still there.

## Not only wood

The wedge method also skips everything a mill needs: the truck to the mill, the
mill, the kiln, the truck back, the yard and the shelf. Its tools are one saw, a
plane and a jig, and it is done where the tree fell. The owner timed the work:
{{hr.split_session}} wedges in a {{hr.session}}-hour session at the log, with the
saw burning {{rip.tanks_hour}} tanks of fuel an hour while it rips.
