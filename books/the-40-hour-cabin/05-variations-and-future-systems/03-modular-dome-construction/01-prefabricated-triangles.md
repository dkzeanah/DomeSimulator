# Prefabricated triangles

A prefabricated triangle is one bay assembled on a bench and carried to the
dome as a unit, rather than built in place stick by stick.

WHY IT IS TEMPTING

Work at waist height instead of overhead. That alone changes the job from
uncomfortable to pleasant, and most of a dome is overhead.

Jigs. A bench with stops on it makes every triangle the same, and every
triangle being the same is worth more than any single one being accurate.

Parallel work. Two people making triangles produce more than two people
climbing a frame.

Weather. Triangles can be made in a shed in February and the dome raised in
April.

WHY THE PINWHEEL RESISTS IT

The wedge method does not build triangles. It builds a pinwheel: each
triangle has its own three members, and they run *past* each other rather
than meeting at the corners.

That is why an A member is 70.182 inches of stock
against a 72-inch chord -- one end stops short of the
corner and the other runs past it, and neither lands on a vertex.

But the pinwheel is *inside* one triangle, not between two. Neighbouring
panels do not share a stick at all: each carries its own member along the
shared edge and the two meet through a gasket.

So a triangle can be prefabricated. What it cannot do is be adjusted
afterwards -- its three members are locked into each other, and the panel's
overall size is fixed the moment the third one goes in.

WHAT WORKS INSTEAD

Sub-assemblies of two or three triangles, where the overlaps are internal to
the group and only the group's edges stick out.

Five triangles at the apex as one cap, lifted whole. That is the worst part
of the build done at ground level, and it is the sub-assembly worth making.

And the base ring as a unit, because it is a single closed loop and it wants
to be accurate more than anything else in the building.

THE ORDER THAT FOLLOWS FROM THIS

Base ring first, accurately. Then upward in rings. Apex cap last, or first
and lifted.

Not triangle by triangle from one side, which is how a pinwheel frame ends
up with the last member not fitting.
