# Panel modules

A panel module is a bay's infill treated as a product: made once, fitted in
minutes, and swapped later for a different one.

THE REFERENCE BUILD'S VERSION

The panel sits on the *outside* face of the frame and is pulled into it by
screws landing in threaded inserts set in the wedges.
4 per bay, 160 for the dome.

That arrangement is not a detail. An earlier version compression-fitted the
panel into the bay from outside, held by friction -- and most of a dome's
bays are overhead, so a friction fit overhead spends the life of the
building arguing with gravity.

Screwed in, gravity works with the fixing.

THE TWO SIZES

Ten AAA bays at 15.59 square feet and thirty BAB at
13.13. Two panel types, forty panels, and every one of them
interchangeable with its own kind.

That is the manufacturing argument in one sentence: a gym and a garage differ
only in which panels are in the bays.

WHAT A PANEL CAN BE

Solid. Glazed. Louvred. Insulated. A door. A hatch. A solar bay. A blank with
a shelf bolted to its inserts.

Each of those is the same outline, the same four fixings and the same twenty
minutes.

THE SECOND JOB THE INSERTS DO

They are a grid of structural anchors on a known pitch. Three together hold
about 540 pounds, which is a shelf, a bunk, a hanging rail or a kitchen run
spanning two or three triangles.

So a bay does not have to be *used* to be useful. It is a barrier with
fixings in it, and that is worth more than the panel.
