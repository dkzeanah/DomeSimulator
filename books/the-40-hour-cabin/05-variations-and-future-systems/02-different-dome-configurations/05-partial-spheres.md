# Partial spheres

A partial sphere is cut somewhere other than the equator, and which way you
cut changes the building completely.

CUT BELOW THE EQUATOR

More than half a sphere. The walls come back in below the widest point, so
the floor is smaller than the widest part of the building.

Nobody does this deliberately for a house -- you lose floor and gain an
overhang that has to be supported. It appears in water tanks and in
structures where the enclosed volume is the point rather than the floor.

CUT ABOVE THE EQUATOR

Less than half a sphere: a shallow cap. More floor for the height, a lower
profile, less material.

This is a good shape for something that does not need headroom at the
perimeter -- a workshop with benches round the edge, a store, a cover over
something. It sheds snow less well and it is less stable in wind, because
there is less depth to resist overturning.

THE TRADE, PLAINLY

Cut low and you get a tall building with a small floor. Cut high and you get
a wide building with a low ceiling. The equator is the balance and that is
most of why it is the default.

THE STRUCTURAL CATCH

A cut above the equator has a base ring that is not in pure hoop stress. The
shell above it leans outward, so the ring is being pushed out as well as
squeezed, and it needs either more section or a tie across the floor.

A ring beam and a tension ring in the floor deck handle it. It is not
difficult. It is just not free, and the hemisphere gets it free.
