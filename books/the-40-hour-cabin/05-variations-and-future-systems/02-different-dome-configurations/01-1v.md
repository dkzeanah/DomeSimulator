# 1V

A 1V dome is an icosahedron cut in half: twenty triangles on the whole
sphere, and every one of them identical.

WHAT IT IS GOOD FOR

Learning. One strut length, one triangle, one angle. You can build a model of
one in an evening out of drinking straws and understand more about geodesic
geometry than a week of reading will give you.

Cheapness. Fewer members, fewer joints, less of everything.

Speed. There is nothing to sort, because every piece is the same piece.

WHAT IT IS BAD AT

Being round. A 1V dome is visibly a faceted polyhedron, not a sphere. The
flat triangles are large relative to the building and the shape reads as
angular.

That matters structurally as well as visually: a large flat panel in a shell
carries bending that a smaller, more curved arrangement would resolve as
membrane action.

Headroom. The facets cut in aggressively, so the usable floor is a smaller
fraction of the footprint than in a higher frequency.

WHERE IT ACTUALLY BELONGS

Small structures. A greenhouse, a play structure, a chicken house, a shed.
At small spans the faceting does not matter and the simplicity is everything.

And as the first thing anybody builds. Build a 1V, in a weekend, before
committing to something that takes a month. Everything you learn transfers.
