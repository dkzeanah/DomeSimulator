# Triangle tables

Two triangles, forty faces, and one of them will not fit a sheet of plywood.

THE FACES

AAA: 10 of them, equilateral, made of three A struts.
Perimeter 216 inches at the reference diameter, and
62.354 inches across the narrowest altitude.

BAB: 30 of them, isosceles, two B struts and one A.
Perimeter 199.34 inches, narrowest altitude
52.516.

THE SHEET PROBLEM, IN ONE LINE

A sheet of plywood is 48 inches wide, and the narrowest altitude of a BAB
face scales directly with the dome.

At the reference build it is 52.516 inches, so the crossover
is at 17.75 feet of diameter.

Under about 17 feet, ordinary sheets work. Over it,
no panel of the dome fits a sheet in any rotation, and the skin has to be
sheeted across the frame, built up from boards, or ordered oversize.

That single fact decides how the skin of any dome in this book gets built,
and it is worth checking before the diameter is chosen rather than after.
The reference build is over the line -- because it was sized from a six-foot
member rather than from a sheet of plywood, and that was a deliberate trade.

WHAT THE TWO TYPES MEAN FOR ORDERING

Ten of one and thirty of the other. If panels are being cut from sheet, the
BAB is the one to work out the nesting for, because there are three times as
many of them.
