# Blank project sheets

Copy these. Fill them in with a pencil before anything is cut.

THE DESIGN SHEET

    diameter  _________ ft
    apex height  _________ ft   (half the diameter)
    A chord  _________ in   (radius x 0.618034)
    B chord  _________ in   (radius x 0.546533)
    A cut length  _________ in   (A chord MINUS 1.818)
    B cut length  _________ in   (B chord MINUS 0.990)
    member width  _________ in   (changes the bite, not the chord)
    narrowest face  _________ in   (under 48 to use sheets?)
    floor, ring  _________ sq ft
    frame stock  _________ ft   (+ waste)
    trees needed  _________

THE FOUNDATION SHEET

    pad diameter  _________ ft
    type  gravel / piers / ring / slab / portable
    frost depth  _________ in
    fall away from pad  _________ in per ft
    service port position  _________

THE SERVICES SHEET

    water from  mains / tank / rain / well
    tank capacity  _________ gal
    waste to  sewer / septic / holding / greywater
    fall to drain  _________ in per ft   (min 0.25)
    power from  mains / generator / solar / hub
    wet-to-electrical clearance  _________ ft   (min 3)

THE PANEL SCHEDULE

Forty rows. Bay number, type (AAA or BAB), infill, and a column for what it
became later -- because it will become something later, and the sheet is the
only record of what the building used to be.

    bay  type  infill  changed to  date
    ____  ____  _________  __________  ______

SUBTRACT, DO NOT SCALE

The bite is a fixed length set by the member's width. It is the same at
every diameter, so the cut is the chord minus a constant and never the
chord times a fraction. The bites above are for the reference build's
4.592-inch member; heavier stock wants its own solve.

THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST

The last column. Everything else in this book can be worked out again from
the geometry. What you did to a particular bay in a particular spring cannot.
