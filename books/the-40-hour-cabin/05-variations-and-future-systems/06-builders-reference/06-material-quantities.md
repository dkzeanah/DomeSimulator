# Material quantities

Timber, by diameter, for a 2V hemisphere with a
4.592-inch member.

      diameter   A stock ft   B stock ft    frame ft    seam ft
    --------------------------------------------------------
         8.00'         145         129        274       127
        12.00'         217         194        411       191
        16.00'         289         258        547       255
        20.00'         361         323        684       318
        19.42'         351         313        664       309
        24.00'         434         387        821       382
        30.00'         542         484       1026       478

WHAT IS AND IS NOT IN IT

This is the stock in the frame. It does not include waste, and the reference
build's model carries an explicit waste fraction on top.

It does not include the base ring's blocking, the panels, the shell or
anything bought rather than cut.

HOW MANY TREES

The model's assumed log -- 15 inches at the
butt, 60 feet usable -- yields about
391 board feet of wedges, enough for
80 struts.

At the reference diameter the dome needs 120, so two trees, with a
third of the stock spare for mistakes. Read that as two trees for anything up
to about twenty feet and three above it.

THE HARVEST COMPARISON

Milled, a log yields about 45 percent of itself as usable lumber. Split into
wedges it yields about 88, because nothing is edged.

That is a 1.95x advantage. Against the pinwheel's 1.80x stock penalty, the
net is about 92 percent of the trees a mitred dome would take.

Eight percent, which is small -- and the point was never the eight percent.
It is that the eight percent arrives with no mitre saw, no jig, no shop, and
a log instead of a lumber order.
