# Solid wedges

A solid wedge is one piece of timber, sawn to a triangular section, and it is
what this whole method assumes unless something says otherwise.

WHAT IT IS

A sector of a log. Split a round log into 8 radial pieces and each
one is a 45-degree wedge -- flat on two faces, curved on the third,
and pointed along one edge.

The reference build's member is
4.59 inches across the wide face and
6.0 inches deep.

WHY THE SHAPE IS THE POINT

Two sawn faces meeting at an angle is a joint you can make with a saw.

The 45-degree sector is not arbitrary: it is the whole seam budget
of the dome. The A seams fold 18.029 degrees and open 26.971; the B seams fold
22.459 and open 22.541. Both pairs sum to 45, which is the raw
sector you split out of the tree.

That is the closest thing this method has to a theorem, and it is why a split
log is the right stock for this building rather than a convenient one.

THE ADVANTAGES

Strength. One piece of continuous grain, no glue line, no fastener in the
section.

Cost. It is what comes out of a tree. There is no processing between the
splitting and the using.

Simplicity. Nothing to delaminate, nothing to come apart, nothing that needs
a shop.

THE LIMITS

Size. The member cannot be bigger than the log. Two trees give
160 members where the dome needs
120, which is comfortable at this size and would not be at
twice it.

Movement. Solid timber moves as it dries, and a wedge sawn green will not be
the same wedge in a year. Build green and accept the movement, or dry and
accept the wait.

Defects. A knot in a solid member is a weakness in that member. There is no
lamination to spread the load round it.
