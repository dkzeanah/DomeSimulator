# Manufactured wedges

A manufactured wedge is one made to a specification, in quantity, by somebody
else -- and it is the version of this method that could actually be sold.

WHAT CHANGES

Consistency. Every member identical to a thousandth, which makes everything
downstream easier: panels that fit, snap fittings that work, assembly
instructions that are true.

Grading. Stress-graded stock with a number on it, which is what an engineer
needs and what a building inspector will ask for.

Treatment. Pressure treatment, fire retardant, or preservative, applied
properly rather than brushed on.

Species choice. The right timber rather than the one growing on the site.

WHAT IS LOST

The tree. The method's central economic claim -- your frame is already
standing on your land -- does not survive being bought in a bundle.

The recovery advantage. A factory making rectangular-ish stock into wedges
starts from milled lumber and the 45 percent.

The independence. A manufactured wedge is a supply chain.

THE HONEST ASSESSMENT

Manufactured wedges make a better product and a worse argument.

For a kit sold to somebody who wants a dome and does not want a chainsaw,
they are obviously right. For the person this book is mostly addressed to --
somebody with land, trees and time, and not much money -- they remove the
only reason the method was interesting.

Both markets are real. They are not the same market, and a design that tries
to serve both usually serves neither.
