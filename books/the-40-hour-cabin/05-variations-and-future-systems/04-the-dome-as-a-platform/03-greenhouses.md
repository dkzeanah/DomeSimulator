# Greenhouses

A greenhouse is the use case where a dome's disadvantages become advantages,
and it is underrated.

WHY THE SHAPE SUITS IT

Glazing the top is the right answer here, not the wrong one. Everything the
cooling chapter warned about -- upper panels facing the sky, gain all day,
the curve concentrating light toward the centre -- is what a greenhouse
wants.

Light from every direction. A dome has no north wall in the way a pitched
greenhouse does, so plants get even light and do not lean.

Strength in wind and snow, which matters for a structure that is mostly glass
and usually out in the open.

And the volume-to-surface efficiency means it holds heat better overnight
than the same floor area under glass in a box.

WHAT CHANGES IN THE SPECIFICATION

Glazing most bays, which is the opposite of every other use here.

Ventilation, urgently. A glazed dome overheats faster than anything else in
this book. The apex is the natural exhaust and it wants to be a proper
automatic vent, not a cap somebody remembers to open.

Frame protection. Timber in a permanently humid glasshouse needs treating or
it will not last.

Shading, seasonal. Removable panels over selected bays, or a shade cloth over
the whole thing in July.

THE VERSION WORTH BUILDING FIRST

A small one. A 1V or a small 2V greenhouse is a weekend, it costs very little,
and it teaches the method on a building where a leak is a nuisance rather
than a disaster.
