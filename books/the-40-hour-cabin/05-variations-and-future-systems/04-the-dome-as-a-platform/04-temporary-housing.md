# Temporary housing

Temporary housing is the use this method was arguably designed for without
setting out to be.

WHAT MAKES IT SUIT

Speed. A frame goes up in a day with help. The whole building is a few
weekends.

Cost. $12,036 for the standard article, and a cheaper version is
possible by leaving out everything the deferment ladder defers.

It comes apart. That is not a nice feature here, it is the requirement: the
building has to leave when the situation that needed it ends.

It travels. Nothing longer than a pickup bed.

And it does not need a foundation. A prepared pad at
$443, or a portable platform, and no concrete.

WHAT IT NEEDS THAT THE REFERENCE BUILD DOES NOT

Speed of erection over cost of parts, which flips several decisions: bolted
connections over anything glued, pre-cut members over site-cut, and a shell
that goes on in one piece.

Storage between deployments. A folded cap, stacked panels and bundled members
want a container, and the design should suit the container.

A documented assembly sequence somebody can follow who has not seen one
before.

THE HONEST CAVEAT

A dome is unfamiliar, and unfamiliar is expensive in exactly the situations
where temporary housing is needed -- the people putting it up are usually
tired, untrained and working against a clock.

That argues for a kit with very few decisions in it, not for the version of
this method that starts with a chainsaw.
