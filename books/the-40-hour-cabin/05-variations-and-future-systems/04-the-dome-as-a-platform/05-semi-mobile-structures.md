# Semi-mobile structures

Semi-mobile means it moves, but not often and not quickly -- which is a
genuinely different category from either a building or a caravan.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

The dome comes apart and travels as parts. It goes up on a new pad in a
weekend. It does that perhaps once every few years.

The utility column moves with it -- the campaign film prices that move at
$260 and rates the core for
25 years, which is longer than several of the
buildings it will stand in.

The pad stays. That is the host's.

WHY THIS IS THE METHOD'S NATURAL SHAPE

Every decision in this book has been pushing toward it: demountable panels,
a removable shell, a column that unbolts at both ends, connectors instead of
splices, gaskets instead of sealant.

None of those were chosen for mobility. They were chosen so the owner could
change their mind. Mobility is what you get for free when everything can be
undone.

THE PRACTICAL LIMITS

Gaskets take a set and want replacing after a few cycles.

Timber that has dried in place does not go back exactly as it came apart.
Label everything, and expect the second assembly to be a little fussier than
the first.

And the services at the new site have to match. A standard pad port is what
makes that true, and a site without one is a site that needs one built.

THE ARRANGEMENT THIS ENABLES

Pads as infrastructure and domes as tenants -- which is the next section but
one, and the most commercially interesting idea in this book.
