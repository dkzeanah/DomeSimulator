# First course

The first course is the ring of triangles sitting on the foundation, and it is
the one that decides whether the rest goes together.

WHAT IT IS

On a 2V hemisphere the bottom course is ten BAB triangles, alternating point
up and point down around the base ring.

Ten panels, and when the tenth one goes in the ring closes on itself.

WHY IT IS THE HARD ONE

Because until it closes, nothing is stable.

Each panel wants to fall inward. There is no completed structure to lean on,
and every joint you make is holding two panels that are themselves being held
by props.

It is also the course where a layout error announces itself. If the base
polygon is out, the tenth panel will not fit, and you will have made nine
joints before finding out.

THE ORDER

Set all ten panels in place, loosely propped, before fastening any of them
permanently.

Do not build round the ring fastening as you go. That is how the accumulated
error ends up entirely in the last joint.

Set them all, check that the ring closes, adjust, and then fasten.

CHECK BEFORE COMMITTING

With all ten standing and loosely held:

* every base vertex on its mark
* the ring closes without forcing
* the top edge of the course is level all the way round
* opposite vertices measure the full diameter, 19.416 feet, in several
  directions

If all four are true, fasten. If any is not, find out why now.

THE HONEST PART

This course takes longer than the four above it put together.

That is normal and it is not a sign you are doing it wrong. The first course
is the one with no structure to help you and the most consequences if it is
out.
