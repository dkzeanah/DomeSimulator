# Establishing reference points

A reference point is something you can measure from that will still be there
tomorrow.

You need several, because the centre stake disappears under the building.

WHAT TO ESTABLISH

The centre, marked on the foundation itself rather than only on a stake.

A north line, or any fixed bearing, scribed on the foundation. Every base
vertex is at a known angle from it -- 36 degrees apart -- and it is how you
re-establish the ten points if a mark gets scuffed off.

Two offset points, well outside the building, on that bearing. If the centre
gets covered, two offsets let you find it again by measurement.

The datum height, marked somewhere that will not move. A screw in a nearby
post, a mark on a wall.

WHY THIS IS WORTH TWENTY MINUTES

Halfway through the build you will need to answer a question like "is this
vertex in the right place" and the answer requires measuring from the centre.

The centre, by then, has a floor over it.

If you have offsets you measure from those and get an answer in a minute. If
you do not, you either guess or you take something apart.

WRITE THEM DOWN

On paper, in the shop, not only on the ground.

Radius, datum height, bearing, offset distances. Four numbers. They are the
only things that connect the building to the site, and remembering them is not
a plan.

THE ONE THAT SAVES THE MOST TIME

Mark the ten base vertex positions permanently on the foundation before the
frame goes on -- a screw, a scribe, a paint mark.

Then when a panel does not sit where you expect, you can see immediately
whether the panel is wrong or the layout was.
