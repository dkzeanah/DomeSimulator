# Reinforcing openings

The job of reinforcement is to give the load a path around the hole.

WHAT THAT MEANS PHYSICALLY

The members that used to run through the space now stop at its edge. Whatever
they were carrying has to get to the members that are still continuous, and it
gets there through whatever you put around the opening.

So the reinforcement is a ring. Not a lintel over the top -- a ring all the
way round.

THE SIMPLE VERSION

Double the members that border the opening.

A second stick beside each bordering member, fastened to it along its length,
roughly doubles the section and is the cheapest reinforcement there is. It
costs three or six extra members and an afternoon.

For a single-triangle opening in a 2V hemisphere, that is usually enough.

THE HEADER

For a multi-triangle opening, add a member straight across the top of the
hole, from one continuous vertex to another.

That is a header, it is doing what a header does in a stud wall, and it is the
piece that stops the shell above the opening from settling into it.

Size it generously. A header is one stick and it is carrying what several used
to.

WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not cut a member and leave the cut ends unsupported. A member that stops in
mid-air is not carrying anything and its neighbours are carrying it.

Do not rely on the door or window frame as structure unless you designed it as
structure. A window frame is a weather part.

Do not open the shell in more than one place on the same ring without thinking
about it. Two openings opposite each other on the base ring halve that ring's
ability to act as a compression ring, and the compression ring is what stops
the dome spreading.

THE HONEST LIMIT

Everything above is reasoning, not engineering.

A single triangle out of a forty-triangle shell, with its border doubled, is
within what a careful builder can decide. Three in a row with a header over
them is at the edge of it. More than that, or anything in a place where people
sleep, wants somebody who can put a stamp on it.
