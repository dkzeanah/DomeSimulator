# Portable platform

A platform that comes apart is the foundation that matches a building that
comes apart, and it is the least-built option on this list for reasons that
are mostly habit.

WHAT IT IS

A deck built in panels, bolted together, sitting on adjustable feet or blocks
rather than anything poured.

Four to ten panels for a 22.4-foot pad. Two people carry a panel. The
whole thing goes together in a morning and comes apart in an afternoon.

WHAT IT COSTS

Broadly the $2,916 of the framed platform, plus the hardware that
makes the joints demountable and minus the piers you no longer pour.

It is not cheaper. It is *reversible*, which is a different kind of value and
the one this method keeps reaching for.

WHERE IT EARNS ITS KEEP

Rented land. A platform that leaves no trace is a platform a landowner will
say yes to.

Permitting. In many places a structure on a non-permanent foundation is a
different animal from one on a poured footing, and the difference is
sometimes the difference between allowed and not. Ask locally; do not assume.

Trial siting. Put the dome where you think you want it, live with it a
season, and move it fifty feet when you find out you were wrong about the
morning sun.

THE COMPROMISES

It moves. Adjustable feet need adjusting, seasonally, and somebody has to
remember.

It does not like slopes. Every inch of fall is another inch of adjustment
range you need at one edge.

And the services have to be demountable too, which is easy if the design
allowed for it and infuriating if it did not. The reference build's utility
core exists partly for this reason.
