# Raised deck

A raised deck is a pier foundation with the piers made taller, and it changes
what the building is.

WHAT IT BUYS

Level, on ground that is not.

Airflow underneath, which on a timber building in a wet climate is worth more
than most of the things people spend money on instead.

Height, which gets the floor above snow, above runoff, and above the wildlife
that would otherwise investigate the underside.

Storage and services in the void, at a height you can stand in rather than
crawl through.

WHAT IT COSTS

Above the $2,916 for the standard platform, the extra is posts,
bracing and a stair. Height is cheap in material and expensive in the bracing
it demands -- a deck two feet up needs almost none, and one six feet up needs
diagonal bracing in both directions or it will sway.

THE THING THAT CATCHES PEOPLE

A tall deck is a structure in its own right and it wants engineering the dome
above it does not.

The dome is a triangulated shell that resolves its own loads. A post-and-beam
platform under it is a frame that racks, and the taller it gets the more of a
problem that is. The dome will not save it.

Brace it properly, in both directions, and do not let the bracing stop at the
fun part.

THE DETAIL WORTH GETTING RIGHT

A step is not a stair. Anything over about eighteen inches needs real treads,
real risers and a handrail, and putting that in at the start is a fraction of
the cost of retrofitting it after somebody falls.
