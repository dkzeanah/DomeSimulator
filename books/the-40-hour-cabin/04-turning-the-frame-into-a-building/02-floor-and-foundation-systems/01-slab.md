# Slab

A concrete slab is the most expensive foundation in this chapter and the only
one that is also the finished floor.

WHAT IT COSTS

$2,468 under a 22.4-foot dome.

That is the whole platform: excavation, gravel, forms, reinforcement,
concrete and finishing. It is not the cheapest and it is not far off the
framed alternatives, because a slab has no timber in it and timber is what
the others spend their money on.

WHAT YOU GET FOR IT

A floor. Nothing else on this list is a floor -- the others are platforms
that a floor goes on top of, and that second floor is a cost the comparison
tends to forget.

Thermal mass. A slab holds heat and gives it back, which in a building with
as little mass as a timber dome is worth having in some climates and a
liability in others.

Permanence. It does not rot, it does not move, and it does not need looking
at again.

WHAT IT COSTS YOU

The dome can never move. That is the whole trade, and in a method built
around a building you can take apart, it is a large thing to give up.

Services are cast in. What you did not put in the pour, you cut for later.

It is a contractor job, or a very organised weekend with help. Concrete does
not wait for you to think.

Damp, if there is no membrane. Put one in.

WHEN TO CHOOSE IT

When the building is never moving, when the climate rewards mass, and when
you want the floor and the foundation to be one purchase rather than two.

Otherwise, read on.
