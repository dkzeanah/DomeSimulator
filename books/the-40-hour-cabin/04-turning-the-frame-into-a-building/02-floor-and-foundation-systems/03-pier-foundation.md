# Pier foundation

Piers are the cheapest permanent thing you can put a building on, and for a
dome they are close to ideal.

WHY

Ten load points. Ten piers. There is nothing in between for a foundation to
do.

A dome does not need a continuous footing because it does not have a
continuous wall. Every other building on a pier foundation is compromising;
this one is matching.

WHAT IT COSTS

$2,916 for piers, beams, joists and a sealed 2x6 deck --
116 boards.

That is the reference build's staple and the bare minimum that hosts a dome.
Two people and a weekend.

Add $4,263 if you want a floor you can mop: the same platform
with plywood and epoxy over it. The ply is not optional. Epoxy over gapped
deck boards cracks at every joint, every time.

WHAT A PIER IS

A precast block on compacted gravel, a poured footing, a screw pile, or a
treated post in the ground, in rising order of cost and permanence.

Blocks on gravel are what the reference build prices, because they are what
somebody can actually buy on a Saturday.

THE TWO THINGS THAT GO WRONG

Frost. A pier that does not reach below the frost line will lift, and it will
lift unevenly, and a dome on an uneven ring racks. Know your depth.

Settlement. Ten piers on undisturbed, compacted ground settle together. Ten
piers where one sits on backfill settle nine ways. Compact properly or find
different ground.

WHEN TO CHOOSE IT

Almost always, for a first building. It is cheap, it is quick, the dome can
leave, and everything under the floor stays reachable.
