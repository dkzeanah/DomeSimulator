# Shading

Shading is the cheapest cooling there is and the one that suits this shape
best, because the surface you need to shade is the one you were going to put
something over anyway.

THE CAP IS ALREADY THERE

The reference build's outer layer is a rain-slick cap strapped over the
building.

Its colour is a free decision and it is worth making deliberately. A light
cap reflects; a dark cap absorbs. On a surface this size that is a
substantial difference in summer and a small one in winter, because winter
sun is low and weak.

Light, unless there is a specific reason.

EXTERNAL BEATS INTERNAL, ALWAYS

A blind inside a window stops light after it has already entered the
building. The heat is in the room; the blind only decides where it lands.

Anything outside stops the heat before it arrives. An external shade is
several times more effective than an internal one and it is the
straightforward thing people skip because internal blinds are easier to buy.

WHAT SUITS A DOME

A brise-soleil over the upper bays: a panel standing off the shell,
shading the glass and letting air move behind it.

Deciduous trees. Shade in summer, bare branches in winter, and the site
probably has some. This is the best shading device available and it costs
nothing if you site the building thoughtfully.

A removable panel over a glazed bay. This one is specific to the method: the
bays are demountable, so a glazed bay can become a solid bay for the summer
and back again in October. That is twenty minutes with a driver.

THE SHADING THE SHAPE GIVES YOU FREE

A dome shades itself. The curve means one side is always turned away from the
sun, and the transition is gradual rather than the hard shadow line a box
casts on itself.
