# Insulation

A dome's advantage is that it has less outside than a box does. Its
disadvantage is that the outside it has is curved, and insulation comes flat.

THE SHAPE ARGUMENT, FIRST

For the same floor area, a hemisphere has substantially less envelope than a
rectangular building -- the reference build's own model puts it at 41 percent
less.

That is real and it is the single biggest thermal thing about this shape.
Less surface means less heat leaving, before anybody has installed anything.

It also means the insulation you do buy goes further, because there is less
of it to buy.

THE STACK

Bare, the reference build's skin is about R-1.5. That is panels,
a membrane and the air films, and it is nearly nothing -- it is a tent with
opinions.

Each quilted layer adds about R-1.6. Seven of them reach
R-12.7, which is a real wall.

TWO WAYS TO BUY IT

In the bays, behind the panels. The frame gives six inches of cavity for
free, because the wedge's inward face stands proud of where the panel sits.
Fill it and it is done.

Or over the frame, under the cap. This is the reference build's route:
quilted recycled clothing, sewn by the owner, at a declared
$50 a layer.

The difference matters. A bay holds 4 layers and then it is full.
A cap is a bag -- every layer makes the next cap a size up, and there is no
limit. Seven layers makes the cap 15.6 percent bigger and nothing else
changes.

That is why this method insulates outside. Not because it performs better --
at the same R it does not -- but because it can keep going.

THE DEFERMENT ARGUMENT

Insulation bought a layer at a time is insulation bought when there is money.

The building works at R-1.5. Badly, in January. But it works,
and every winter after that it is warmer, and nothing has to come apart for
that to happen.
