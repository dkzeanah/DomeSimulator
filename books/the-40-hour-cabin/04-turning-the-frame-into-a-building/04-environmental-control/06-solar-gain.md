# Solar gain

Solar gain is heat arriving through glass, and in this shape it needs
counting rather than guessing.

THE NUMBER

2.6 BTU/h per square foot of glazing is
what the reference build's cooling model allows.

Multiply by the glazed area and you have the summer penalty in the same units
as the heater and the cooler. Three glazed bays at roughly 15 square feet
each is 45 square feet, which is about 117 BTU/h -- small. Ten bays is 390,
which is a meaningful fraction of the whole cooling load.

That is the calculation, and it takes a minute.

WHICH BAYS

Low bays face outward and give a view. Their gain is seasonal and modest,
because in summer the sun is high and strikes them at a glancing angle.

Upper bays face the sky. Their gain is worst exactly when you least want it,
because in summer the sun is high and strikes them square on.

That is the whole geometry of it, and it is why the answer to "where should
the windows go" is *low*.

THE WINTER SIDE OF THE LEDGER

Solar gain is free heat in January.

A dome with glazing on its equator-facing side collects usefully in winter,
when the sun is low and strikes those panels square. The same panels that are
glancing in July are perpendicular in December.

So: glazing low on the sun side is good twice. Glazing high is bad once and
good never.

THE HONEST LIMIT

None of this is a substitute for a real energy model. It is enough to stop
somebody putting eight glass triangles in the roof because it looked good in
a render.
