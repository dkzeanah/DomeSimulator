# Snap-in concepts

A snap-in panel goes in without tools. It is the direction this method is
heading and it is not where the reference build is yet, so this section is
honest about which is which.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT WORK

A gasket that compresses when the panel is pushed home, and a catch that holds
it against the gasket's spring.

That is a door seal, and door seals are a solved problem -- but they are solved
on rectangular doors in rigid frames, and this is a triangle in a frame made
of split logs.

THE PROBLEM THAT HAS TO BE SOLVED FIRST

Split timber is not dimensionally identical bay to bay.

A snap fitting needs the opening to be the same size every time, to within a
millimetre or two, or the catch either does not reach or does not release.

Forty bays made from real trees are not that consistent, and making them that
consistent means machining the inside faces of every member -- which is
exactly the work the wedge method exists to avoid.

THE HONEST POSITION

Snap-in is the right answer for a manufactured panel in a manufactured frame.

For a frame split out of a buyer's own trees, screws into inserts are the
right answer, and the four screws per bay are not the thing slowing anybody
down.

WHAT IS WORTH BORROWING FROM THE IDEA ANYWAY

Captive fasteners. A screw that stays in the panel when it is undone means no
bag of screws to lose and no dropped fastener at the top of a dome.

A single-handed catch at one corner, with screws at the others, so a panel can
be held with one hand while the first fixing goes in.

Both of those give you most of the convenience without needing the frame to be
machine-consistent.

The reference build should probably do the first. It does not yet.
