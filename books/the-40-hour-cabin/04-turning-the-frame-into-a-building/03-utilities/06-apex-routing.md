# Apex routing

The top of the dome is a socket, and everything the building grows later
leaves through it.

WHAT IS UP THERE

A sleeve through the apex panel, the riser passing through it, and a gasketed
seal cap over the lot, $195.

The riser does not stop at the apex. It stands proud of it, and the part
standing proud is the interface: a place a line can be picked up from outside
without opening the building.

WHY THIS IS WORTH THE TROUBLE

Because it means nothing ever cuts a new hole in a weathertight surface.

Solar goes on later, and it comes down from the apex. A satellite dish, an
aerial, a weather station, a vent, a flue for a stove that was not there
originally: all of them leave under the cap and run down the outside.

The alternative -- one penetration per addition -- is how a roof becomes a
leak.

THE CAP

Gasketed, with over-centre catches, meant to stay shut for years and come off
in ten minutes.

It is the single best weather decision in the building, and the one to
protect. Anyone who drills the shell to save walking a cable up has undone
it.

THE PART PEOPLE DOUBT

A hole in the highest point of a roof sounds like the worst possible place
for one.

It is the best, for the same reason a boat's deck fittings are on the high
side: water does not collect there. A penetration halfway down the curve sits
in the path of everything running off the surface above it. The apex has
nothing above it.
