# Central utility column

The utility column is the single best idea in this building and the one
hardest to explain on paper.

WHAT IT IS

A metal column standing in the middle of the dome, floor to apex, carrying
everything: water up, waste down, electrical up, and out through the top.

Priced at $1,405 complete, over 6.5 hours to
assemble on a bench.

WHY THE MIDDLE

Because a dome has no good walls and one excellent middle.

Every service in a conventional house runs in a wall cavity. A dome's
equivalent of a wall cavity is a triangular bay in a curved shell, and
running services there means they follow the curve, cross the seams, and end
up somewhere different from where you left them.

The middle is vertical, straight, and the same at every height.

WHY IT IS ONE OBJECT

Because then it can leave.

The column unbolts from the pad port at the bottom and the apex sleeve at the
top, and goes into the next dome. The campaign film prices that move at
$260 and rates the core for
25 years, which is longer than most of the
buildings it will stand in.

That is the modular argument, and it only works because the services are one
thing rather than forty things buried in a shell.

WHAT IS ON IT

The housing and apex sleeve, $380. The water
manifold. The drain stack. The sub-panel. A service chase running
10.5 feet from floor to apex at
$11 a foot. And the seal cap over the apex
penetration at $195.

Six items. That is the entire service infrastructure of the building, and
you can walk round it.
