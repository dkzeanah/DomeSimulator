# Water entry

Water arrives where the electricity does: at the pad port, in the middle of
the floor, and rises from there.

THE MANIFOLD

A water manifold and rise from the pad, $240.

One inlet, one shut-off, and branches off it at working height. That is the
whole thing, and the reason it is one object rather than a run of pipe is so
that disconnecting the dome from the pad is one action.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

Four honest options, in rising order of independence:

A spur from a mains supply. Cheapest to run, most dependent.

A tank, filled by truck or hose. The reference build prices
120 gallons under the floor at
$1.35 a gallon of capacity, which is a few
hundred dollars and buys a week or two depending entirely on how you live.

Rainwater off the shell. A dome is a very good catchment -- it is a smooth
curved surface with a single low edge -- and the seam network can be run as
the collector.

A well. Different chapter, different trade, and the only one on this list
that needs a rig.

FREEZING

A pipe in an unheated void freezes. The under-floor space is an unheated
void.

Either the tank and the rise are inside the insulated envelope, or they are
heat-traced, or the system drains down when the building is empty. Pick one
deliberately. Discovering in February which one you picked by accident is a
bad afternoon.
