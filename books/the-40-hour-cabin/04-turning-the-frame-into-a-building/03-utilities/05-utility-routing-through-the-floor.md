# Utility routing through the floor

Everything arrives through one hole in the floor, and that hole is the most
important detail in the building's services.

THE PORT

A service port up through the deck, $340.

One opening, lined, sealed to the deck, with the water, waste and electrical
passing through it and the column landing on top of it.

WHY ONE HOLE

Because a hole in a floor is a place for air, water and animals, and one of
them is manageable.

Also because the dome has to be able to leave. Lift it off and what is left
is one capped port in a platform, not a scattering of stubs.

THE VOID UNDERNEATH

On a framed platform there is a crawl space, and everything upstream of the
port lives in it: the tank, the incoming spur, the shut-off, the drain run
out to wherever it goes.

That space is unheated, so see the freezing note two sections back, and it is
where the reference build puts $420 of storage
because the space exists whether you use it or not.

On a slab, none of this is true. Everything is cast in, it is in its final
position, and the crawl space does not exist. That is the slab's real cost
and it does not appear in its price.

THE ONE THING TO GET RIGHT

Seal the port to the floor, not to the column.

The column comes out. If the seal belongs to the column, taking the column
out opens the floor. If it belongs to the floor, the floor stays closed and
the column lifts through it.
