# 77. One Menu at a Time

A style changes four things at once. To see what each one is worth, change
them one at a time. Every table in this chapter starts from the bare cabin of
chapter {{ch.stycabin}} -- ${{sty.bare_total}} -- and changes exactly one
menu, rebuilding the cabin for every row.

## Change one thing

Each table shows the line that menu controls, the weight of the cabin it
makes, and the cabin's new total. The rows run cheapest first.

## The frame

A frame material is not only a price per kilogram; it is a shape and a
thickness, because nobody builds a steel dome out of solid steel wedges. The
Creator weighs a frame as solid struts of the shape and width it is given,
so each material here is made the way one of the Creator's own designs makes
it -- and where no design uses a material, it is given a tube, and the table
says so.

    {{sty.by_frame}}

At one end, {{sty.by_frame_lo}} brings the cabin to
${{sty.by_frame_lo_usd}}; at the other, {{sty.by_frame_hi}} takes it to
${{sty.by_frame_hi_usd}} -- a swing of ${{sty.by_frame_span}}. Remember the
cabin's actual frame is split eighth-log wedges from trees the wedge method
counts and prices itself, at ${{money.frame}}; this table is what the same
dome costs framed some other way, by the Creator's arithmetic.

## The panels

Fifteen ways to fill forty triangles:

    {{sty.by_panel}}

Plastic sheeting is the floor of the table and mirror is the ceiling:
{{sty.by_panel_lo}} at ${{sty.by_panel_lo_usd}}, {{sty.by_panel_hi}} at
${{sty.by_panel_hi_usd}}. Solar panels sit near the top, and are the only
row here that pays something back; precast concrete is the heaviest by far,
which matters to a frame designed around wood.

## Cladding and foundation

Over the panels, one layer of cladding:

    {{sty.by_layer}}

And under the floor, the foundation:

    {{sty.by_foundation}}

The foundation menu swings ${{sty.by_foundation_span}} on its own. The pad
the wedge method builds in chapter {{ch.floor}}, and the pentagon floor of
chapter {{ch.everypiece}}, are both cheaper than the dearest rows here,
because they are built rather than bought.

## Where to spend

Ranked by how much each menu can move the cabin's price on its own:
{{sty.ranking}}.

Colour does not appear on that list because it costs nothing at all: every
frame colour and every panel colour prices the same. The cheapest decision in
the whole building is also the one people agonise over most.

So, for a cabin built the wedge way: the frame is already settled and cheap,
and the panels are the menu that moves the money most. Spend it on the
panels people will look at and touch -- and think hard before buying an expensive
foundation for a building this light, which is the argument chapter
{{ch.floor}} makes from the other direction.
