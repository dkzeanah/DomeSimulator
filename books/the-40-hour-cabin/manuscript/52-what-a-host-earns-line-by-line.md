# 52. What a Host Earns, Line by Line

<!-- concept: byod/why_host -->
<!-- concept: byod_deepseek/why_host -->

Why would anybody host a dome?

Cash. Income from land, without also buying and maintaining the house somebody
else lives in. Everybody knows the pains of landlords and everybody knows the
pains of tenants, and this arrangement is an attempt to take the worst of
both out at once: the host looks after a deck and a connection, and replacing
somebody's kitchen, sofa or bedroom carpet stops being the host's business.

That is the pitch. This chapter is the arithmetic under it, worked with the
same pad model as the films and with the lines a pitch usually leaves out
left in.

## The cheapest working pad

Before any rotating base or adjustable rim, the starting point: a small framed
deck on precast blocks, a fixed rim, and one shared power panel and water
manifold serving four pads, with a short spur to each.

    {{pad.starter}}

Note the site allowance. Roads, trunk services and drainage do not become free
because four people share them, so a share of them is in the total whether or
not anybody likes it.

## What ten thousand dollars would return

<!-- concept: byod/target -->

Suppose the design work does get a complete serviced pad -- power, water and
drain included -- to ${{park.pad_target}}. What would it earn at the rents a
host might actually ask?

    {{park.w_target}}

At ${{park.rent_high}} a month it repays in {{park.months_high}} months. At
${{park.rent_mid}}, {{park.months_mid}}. At ${{park.rent_low}}, it takes
{{park.months_low}} months -- more than two years.

Three things about that table, all of them caveats:

- **It is payback on net**, after management, tax and insurance of
  ${{park.yearly_costs}} a year, with the pad leased {{park.occupancy}} percent
  of the year rather than all of it. Payback on gross is a brochure number.
- **The build cost is a target**, not a quote; the line above this one shows
  the model's current pad costing more.
- **It is before financing and income tax.** A one-year payback is a
  scenario, not a promise.

The goal under all of it is infrastructure that serves for
{{park.life_target}} years and longer. A pad that repays in two and lasts ten
is a good pad. A pad that repays in one and cracks in three is not.

## What the host maintains

The host's list is the one from Chapter {{ch.pad_kit}}: a deck, a rim, the
connections, perhaps a meter, perhaps a column. Weather and ordinary wear
still matter. The work does not go to zero.

But compare it honestly. The alternative a landowner actually weighs is not
"nothing" -- it is furnishing a place they already own and letting it out.
Carry the same management, tax and insurance on both sides, then add what a
furnished let needs on top:

    {{park.w_exposure}}

A pad host pays ${{tt_net.host_pad_yearly}} a year. The furnished let pays
${{tt_net.host_let_yearly}}: turnover cleaning, a damage reserve, a platform's
fee and a renovation cycle, for ever. That gap is the recurring work a pad
host is trying to stop doing.

![The comparison a host actually makes.](plate-host-bills.png)

That comparison assumes the rental building already exists. Buying or
building one is a different starting point with a much bigger number on it,
and the table does not include it.

## The deluxe pad

Now the top of the range: a larger concrete deck, a rotating base, the
plumbing column, an array of panels, and the adjustable iris on top, because
it is another mechanism with another bill.

    {{park.w_deluxe}}

${{park.deluxe_cost}}. That is more than a furnished let costs to set up, and
Chapter {{ch.network}} said so: on up-front cost alone, the short let wins.
The deluxe pad is a different feature set at a different starting point, and
a house that turns toward the sunrise is a future option -- Chapter
{{ch.turns}} -- not the ticket into the system.

The ticket into the system is a floor and services. Everything else is a
branch a host can grow into: floor heating, water storage, a deep freeze, a
concrete shop floor, rotation, sun tracking, and eventually something smart
enough to run all of it. Each of those can be its own development, and each
can be sold to the host who wants it. None of them is needed to start.
