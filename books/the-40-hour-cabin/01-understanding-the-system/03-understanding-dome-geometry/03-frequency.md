# Frequency

Frequency is how many times you cut each edge of the original shape before you
push the points out onto the sphere.

That is it. That is the whole idea.

Start with an icosahedron -- twenty triangles, the most round thing you can
make out of identical flat faces. Leave it alone and you have a 1V dome:
twenty big triangles, very angular, very cheap, and not very round.

Cut every edge in half and you get a 2V. Each original triangle becomes four
smaller ones. Push all the new points out until they sit on the sphere, and
the shape gets rounder.

Cut every edge in three and you get a 3V. Each triangle becomes nine. Rounder
again.

Higher frequency means more triangles, smaller triangles, more sticks, more
joints, and a shape closer to a true sphere.

WHAT FREQUENCY COSTS

Every step up in frequency is a step up in parts count, and the parts count
goes up faster than the roundness does.

A 2V hemisphere has 65 edges. A 3V has substantially more, and every one of
them is a stick to cut, a joint to make, and a place to be out by a sixteenth.

The reference build is a 2V because 2V is where the parts count stops being
absurd and the shape is still round enough to behave like a dome. Forty
triangles. Sixty-five edges. Twenty-six vertices. Those are numbers a person
can hold.

If you are building something forty feet across, 2V will give you triangles
too big to panel sensibly and you will want 3V. If you are building a
greenhouse over a vegetable bed, 1V is probably enough and you should not let
anyone talk you into more.

THE PART NOBODY MENTIONS

Frequency does not change how hard the *joints* are. It changes how many of
them there are.

A 3V dome is not a harder dome to build than a 2V. It is the same difficulty,
more times. If your 2V joints are coming out clean, your 3V joints will too.
If they are not, going up a frequency will not fix them -- it will just give
you more of them to be unhappy about.

Get the 2V right first.
