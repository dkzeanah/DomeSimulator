# Screws

Screws are the default fastener for this method and they are the right one for
most of the building.

WHY SCREWS AND NOT NAILS

You can take a screw out.

That is nearly the whole argument. A dome frame is a thing you will adjust,
and the first one you build you will adjust a lot. A nailed joint is a
decision; a screwed joint is a proposal.

Screws also pull. A nail holds two pieces where they are; a screw draws them
together. In a pinwheel joint that matters, because the joint wants the butt
face pressed hard against the receiving face, and a screw does that on its
own.

WHAT NOT TO USE

Drywall screws. They are brittle, they snap under torque, and the head shears
off leaving a stub you cannot get out. They cost almost nothing and they will
cost you an afternoon.

Anything not rated for exterior use, anywhere that will ever be damp.

DRILL THE PILOT

In green timber, near an end, without a pilot hole, you will split the wood.

Not sometimes. Reliably.

Every fastener in a pinwheel joint is going in near the end of a member, which
is the worst place for splitting, and split timber at a joint is a joint that
has already failed. Pilot every one.

That is 120 joints with two or three fasteners each. It is a lot of holes and
it is not optional.

LENGTH

The screw has to pass through the butting member and get well into the
receiving one. On a member six inches deep, that means a long screw -- and it
means the screw is working across most of the joint, which is what you want.

Do not use a fastener that only just makes it. A screw that reaches two
threads into the receiving member is a screw that will pull out when the wood
dries and shrinks.
