# Internal brackets

An internal bracket sits inside the joint -- between the members, hidden once
assembled.

WHAT THEY ARE FOR

Alignment, mostly. A small internal plate or dowel that locates one member
against another makes assembly faster and stops a joint sliding out of
position while you drive the fastener.

On a 120-joint frame, anything that makes a joint self-locating is worth real
money in time.

THE DOWEL VERSION

The simplest internal bracket is a dowel: a hole in the butting face, a
matching hole in the receiving face, a hardwood pin between them.

The pin does not carry much load. What it does is hold the joint in exactly
one position while you fasten it, which means every joint ends up in the same
place rather than wherever it drifted to.

That is a repeatability tool disguised as a fastener.

THE COST

Every internal bracket is invisible once the dome is together, which sounds
like an advantage and is also a problem: you cannot inspect it.

If an internal bracket corrodes, splits the wood around it, or works loose,
you will find out from the outside -- a joint that has moved -- long after it
started.

So: internal brackets for alignment, external fasteners for load. Then
anything carrying the building can be looked at and tightened without taking
the building apart.

THAT IS THE RULE

Hidden parts should not be load-bearing parts.

Not because hidden parts fail more often. Because when they do, you cannot
tell.
