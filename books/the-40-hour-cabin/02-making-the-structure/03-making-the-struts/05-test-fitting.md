# Test fitting

Build one panel before you cut a hundred and twenty members.

That is the whole section. Everything below is why.

WHAT THE FIRST PANEL TELLS YOU

Three members, three joints, one triangle. It takes an hour and it checks
every assumption you have made:

* whether your cut lengths are right
* whether your end angles are right
* whether the jig holds what you thought it held
* whether the wedge orientation you chose does what you expected
* whether the joint pulls up tight or leaves a gap

Any one of those being wrong is a thing you want to find out on three sticks
rather than on a hundred and twenty.

HOW TO CHECK IT

Lay the finished panel on a flat floor.

It should sit down on all three corners with no rock. If it rocks, one of your
end cuts is off and the triangle is not planar.

Measure the three sides between the vertex points. They should match the
chords for that triangle type -- all 72.000 for an AAA, or 72.000 and two of
63.670 for a BAB.

Measure both diagonals from each corner to the middle of the opposite side.
For an AAA they should be identical. If they are not, your triangle is not
symmetrical and one member is long.

THEN BUILD THE SECOND ONE

The first panel tells you whether you can build a panel. The second tells you
whether you can build the same panel twice.

Put them on top of each other. They should agree everywhere. Any difference
between panel one and panel two is a difference that will be in all forty, and
it is much easier to find now.

WHAT TO DO WITH THE TEST PANELS

Use them. They are real panels and there is nothing wrong with them.

Unless there is, in which case take them apart and find out why, and be
grateful it cost you two panels.
