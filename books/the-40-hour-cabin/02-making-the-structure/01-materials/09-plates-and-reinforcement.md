# Plates and reinforcement

The short version: you do not need them, and here is when you might want them
anyway.

WHY THE FRAME DOES NOT NEED THEM

The pinwheel joint is a compression joint. Wood pressed against wood, with a
large contact area, at loads well within what softwood takes across the grain.

A steel plate does not improve that. The wood is already carrying it.

Plates and brackets exist in other dome systems because those systems put
their joints in tension and shear, where wood is weak and steel is not. This
one does not.

WHERE REINFORCEMENT IS ACTUALLY WORTH IT

Three places.

The base connection. Uplift is the load case that matters on a dome -- it is a
very good aerofoil and it is light. Every base vertex wants a strap or an
anchor holding it down, and that is a tension connection where steel belongs.

Around openings. Cutting a door or window out of a triangulated shell
interrupts the triangulation. The members either side of the hole are carrying
what the missing ones used to, and they may want help.

Point loads. A hoist, a heavy fixture, a partition carrying something. The
frame takes those locally if you give it something to take them with.

WHAT TO AVOID

Plating every joint "to be safe".

That is not safer. It adds cost, adds thermal bridges through the envelope,
adds visible steel to an interior that is mostly visible, and -- the part that
matters -- it hides whether the joints are working.

If the frame needs plates everywhere, something is wrong with the joints and
the plates are covering it up rather than fixing it.

THE HONEST LIMIT OF THIS CHAPTER

Nothing here is an engineering specification.

Species, moisture, snow load, wind exposure and local code all change the
answer, and no book can know yours. What this chapter can tell you is what the
joint is -- a butt in compression, fasteners holding it closed -- and that is
the description an engineer needs to size it for your site.

If the dome is a workshop, build it as described and sleep fine. If people are
going to live in it, take this description to somebody who can stamp it.
