# Knots and defects

Every real tree has defects. The question is which ones matter and where.

KNOTS

A knot is where a branch was. The grain flows round it, which makes the wood
locally stronger in some directions and much weaker in others.

For this method the important thing about a knot is not strength. It is that
knots ruin splits.

A split runs along the grain, and grain goes round a knot. Hit a knot while
splitting and the split wanders, forks or stops.

So: look at the log before you split it, mark the knot clusters, and buck the
log so the clear lengths become members and the knotty lengths become
something else.

WHERE A KNOT IS ACCEPTABLE

In the middle of a member's length, away from the ends, where the member is in
compression and nothing is fastened.

WHERE IT IS NOT

Near an end. Every member gets fastened near both ends, and a fastener into or
beside a knot splits the wood or gets no grip.

On a sawn face that becomes a seam. The seam faces have to be flat and
predictable and a knot there is a lump.

OTHER DEFECTS

Shake -- a separation along the growth rings. A member with shake through it
can come apart along that plane. Reject it.

Check -- surface cracks from drying. Usually cosmetic on a member this size.
Fine.

Sweep -- a gentle bend along the log. Members are short; a six-foot length out
of a long gentle sweep is nearly straight. Usable.

Spiral grain -- the whole log twisted. Splits come out twisted and members
wind. This is the worst defect for this method and it is worth rejecting the
log.

THE RULE

Defects are not a reason to reject wood. They are a reason to decide what each
piece becomes.

The clear butt log becomes A members. The knotty top becomes B members and
blocking. The bad piece becomes jig stock. Almost nothing is actually waste,
which is another part of why the method suits trees you cut yourself.
