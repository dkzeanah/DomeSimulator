**What you are deciding:** what the building is for -- which, on this frame, is a

## The reference answer

The project's product line starts from the **stem cell**: the bare frame, its
skin, the utility core in the middle and one blank panel. Everything a building
is *for* -- windows, doors, a skylight, a stove flue, benches, a sink, a rack of
shelves -- is a set of panels and modules added to that body. Volume A prices

Look at how many of them are **utility buildings**: cold storage, a workshop, a
garage, a studio, a sauna, a growing room. For most families the first dome is
not the house. It is the building that makes the house work better -- the dry
workshop, the root cellar above ground, the greenhouse that grows food in March
-- and it earns its keep while the family learns the method on something that
does not have to be slept in.

:::box why "Why"
Because every panel is a separate framed triangle, the building's purpose lives
in its forty openings, not in its walls. A frame that has not decided what it is
can be re-decided later for the price of new panels -- which is what makes it a
stem cell rather than a shed. The core carries the services and is built to
unbolt and move to the next dome.
:::

:::box watch "Watch out"
Anything with water needs a drain beside it. Power-hungry modules -- a sauna
heater above all -- change what the electrical panel and any battery bank have
to be. And the line between a shed and a dwelling is a legal one: ask your
building department which side your plan is on before you build it.
:::
