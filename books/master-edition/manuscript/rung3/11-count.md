**What you are deciding:** how many hours and how many dollars -- and which of
those numbers are real yet.

## The reference answer: hours

The title's forty hours are the frame, from wedges to standing, counted stage by
stage in Volume A:

    {{clock.table}}

Felling, the skin, the floor, doors and windows are not on that clock. The whole
build is about {{money.labour_hours}} hours of labour. The paperback counts its
forty hours differently -- erection alone, with splitting added on top -- and the
comparison page at the back of this book sets the two clocks side by side.

## The reference answer: dollars

The standard article -- the stem-cell dome as a product, built by somebody else
at a wage and sold with a stated margin -- is invoiced line by line:

    {{kick.w_invoice}}

It costs {{money.cost}} dollars to make, carries a markup of {{money.profit}}
dollars, and lists at {{money.price}} dollars: {{money.per_sqft}} dollars a square
foot of floor, on ground priced separately. The frame -- the part this method is
about -- is the smallest line: {{money.frame}} dollars. Building it yourself
removes the labour line and the markup; it does not remove the cap, the core or
the panels.

:::box why "Why"
A plan that does not say which of its numbers are measured is a plan nobody can
check, including you. And money you avoid spending is real but it is not income:
it only counts if you were going to buy the thing anyway. The volumes keep that
distinction on every page.
:::

:::box watch "Watch out"
Prices go stale; check every one locally before you rely on it. Log your real
hours and real receipts as you go -- the next family's plan starts from them.
Do not leave the allowance out because the work feels easy on paper.
:::
