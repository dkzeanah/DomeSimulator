**What you are deciding:** the dome's size -- one number, from which everything
else follows.

## The reference answers, and why there are two

Both books build a two-frequency hemisphere with {{dome.panels}} panels and
{{dome.members}} members. Both start from six feet, the longest stick one person
can carry and set alone. And they arrive at different sizes, because they apply
the six feet to different things.

**Volume A** takes six feet as the **long chord** -- the edge of the triangle,
corner to corner -- because that is what the owner's real members measure:
{{cut.a_chord}} inches long, {{cut.b_chord}} inches short. That gives a sphere of
radius {{dome.radius_in}} inches: a dome {{dome.diameter_ft}} feet across and
{{dome.height_ft}} feet tall, with {{dome.floor_sqft}} square feet of floor
inside its ten-sided ring.

**Volume B** takes six feet as the **longest piece you cut** -- the member
itself. Because of the pinwheel, a member is always a little shorter than the
edge it spans, so a six-foot member spans a longer edge, and the dome comes out
bigger: {{tt_dome.diameter_ft}} feet across, {{tt_dome.height_ft}} feet tall,
{{tt_dome.floor_sqft}} square feet counted as a full circle.

Neither is wrong. They are two honest ways of saying "six feet". Pick one
convention for your plan and never mix them.

## Standing room

Floor is not the same as room. Of Volume A's {{mb.floor_circle_sqft}} square
feet of floor circle, {{mb.stand_sqft}} -- about {{mb.stand_pct}} per cent --
has {{mb.head_ft}} feet or more over it; the rest is the low ring round the
wall, which is where beds, benches and storage belong.

## Bigger and smaller

The parts list does not change with size: a {{flat.small_ft}}-foot dome and a
{{flat.big_ft}}-foot dome both have {{dome.members}} members and
{{dome.panels}} panels, and the bigger one has {{flat.ratio}} times the floor.
What grows is the length of each stick, and the stick is limited by what one
person can lift. Past that, a bigger dome needs either a crew or a higher
frequency -- more, shorter sticks.

:::box why "Why"
Every edge of the dome is a fixed fraction of the sphere's radius -- the long one
{{cut.a_factor}}, the short one {{cut.b_factor}} -- so one length fixes them all.
The pinwheel then takes a bite off each member that depends on the stick's
width, not on the dome's size; that bite is why "six feet" means two different
domes. The volumes print both calculations and close the loop between them.
:::

:::box watch "Watch out"
Decide the convention first and write it at the top of your plan. Check your size
against the stick you can actually lift, green and wet. And remember the
headroom: a hemisphere's usable floor is much smaller than its floor area.
:::
