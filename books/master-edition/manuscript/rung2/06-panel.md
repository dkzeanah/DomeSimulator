The real panels are built on a **jig**: one flat board with blocks screwed to
it that hold each stick in its place, and a straight edge the saw runs along.
The picture shows one, labelled. Once the blocks are set, nobody measures
anything again -- the blocks do the measuring, the same way every time.

Why go to the trouble? Because forty triangles that are each *almost* right
will not close into a dome. Every small difference adds up as the rings go on,
and the last triangle will not fit the hole that is left for it. Forty
triangles that are *the same* -- even if they are all the same slightly-wrong
size -- close perfectly, because they all agree with each other.

That is the most surprising idea in building: **making things the same matters
more than making each one perfect.**

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** the side of a cardboard box, a dozen pushpins, a ruler, a pencil,
and paper strips or straws cut to the paper dome's two lengths:
{{mb.paper_long_mm}} mm and {{mb.paper_short_mm}} mm.

1. **Freehand first.** Without the jig, build five short-sided triangles from
   your strips, using the ruler to place them. Tape each one.
2. **Make the jig.** Draw one short-sided triangle carefully on the cardboard.
   Push two pins just outside each corner, so a strip laid along a side is held
   by the pins.
3. **Jig run.** Build five more triangles by laying strips against the pins,
   taping, and lifting each finished triangle off.
4. **Compare.** Measure all three sides of all ten triangles. Write them in a
   table. Which group of five is more alike?
5. **The second shape.** The equal-sided triangles need their own jig. Make it.
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
A jig turns a measurement made once into a position held forever. The real jig
holds each wedge by its two flat sawn faces, never by its bumpy bark, and it
goes through twelve stages for every panel, from the bare bench to the finished
triangle lifted off. The dome needs {{dome.aaa}} equal-sided panels and
{{dome.bab}} short-sided ones, so the real jig is set up twice, just like yours.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
Your measurements show the jig triangles agreeing with each other better than
the freehand ones, and you can say why agreeing matters more than being exact.
:::
