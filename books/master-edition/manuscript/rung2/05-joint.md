Most domes are held together at their corners by a *hub*: a metal star with a
socket for every stick. A hub only works when every stick arrives at exactly
the same angle and is exactly the right length. A pile of split logs never
does.

So the wedge dome has no hubs. At each corner of a triangle, the end of one
stick rests on the *side* of the next, and that one's end rests on the side of
the next, round and round like a pinwheel. The corner itself is empty. The
picture is a corner of the real frame, where five triangles meet and nothing
touches anything at the point.

This chapter builds both kinds of corner, and then does the test that shows why
the pinwheel wins with rough wood.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** about ten craft sticks, white glue (a grown-up can use a glue gun
instead), a few balls of modelling clay, and a pencil.

1. **The pinwheel.** Lay three sticks on the table in a triangle so that the end
   of each one rests on the side of the next, all going round the same way.
   Glue the three overlaps. That is one panel.
2. **The mirror.** Make a second pinwheel going round the *other* way. Lay it on
   the first. Do they match? (Builders pick one direction and use it for every
   panel, or the seams do not line up.)
3. **The seam.** Lay two pinwheel panels edge to edge, with a thin strip of
   card standing between the two sticks that touch. That strip is the *key*,
   and the pair of sticks with the key between them is a *seam*.
4. **The hub.** Push the ends of three sticks into one ball of clay so they
   meet at a point, making a triangle with a clay ball at each corner.
5. **The test.** Snap a little off the end of one stick in each triangle so it
   is shorter than the others. Try to rebuild both. Which one still closes?
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
In the pinwheel, a stick that is a little short simply slides along the side of
its neighbour and lands a little further in, and the panel still closes. The
error goes into the overlap instead of into the corner. On the real frame each
member's resting end -- the *butt* -- gets one careful compound cut. The other
end -- the *head* -- is left deliberately long and sawn off flush once the
panel is assembled, so the length is decided by the panel, not by a tape
measure. Because every panel has its own three members, the dome has
{{dome.members}} members for {{mb.edges}} edges, and each of its {{dome.seams}}
seams is two members with a key between them.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
You have built two pinwheel panels and a seam, and you can say why the
pinwheel closed with a short stick when the hub did not.
:::
