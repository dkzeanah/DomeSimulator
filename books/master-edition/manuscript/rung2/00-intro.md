Rung one told the story. This rung builds it -- small, on the kitchen table,
out of paper, straws, string and tape -- and in building it, explains why
every part is the shape it is.

Nothing here needs a power tool. Anything sharp is a grown-up's job, and every
chapter says which parts those are. The biggest projects are the **paper
dome** (chapter four, with a template to cut out at the end of the chapter)
and the **straw dome** (chapter seven), and between them a family will have
held, counted and pushed on the same forty triangles the real cabin is made
of, at one-{{mb.paper_scale}}th of the size.

Each chapter has the three reads again:

* **the idea** -- the story of the part, a little deeper than rung one;
* **Make it** -- what you need and what to do, step by step;
* **Why it works** -- the reason, in plain words and the real numbers.

Each one ends with **You've got it when** -- a test you can pass. Pass all
twelve and the planning on rung three will make sense the first time through.
