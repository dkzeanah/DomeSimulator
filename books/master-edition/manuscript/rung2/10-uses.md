A round room is not a square room with curved walls. It changes where you can
stand, where the furniture goes, and what the room is good for. Before anybody
builds a dome, it is worth drawing one -- because the answers are measurable,
and they surprise people.

The biggest surprise is headroom. The roof of a dome comes all the way down to
the floor at the edge. In the middle you can stand up easily; near the wall you
cannot. Of the reference dome's {{mb.floor_circle_sqft}} square feet of floor
circle, only {{mb.stand_sqft}} -- about {{mb.stand_pct}} per cent -- has
{{mb.head_ft}} feet or more of ceiling over it. The rest is a low ring round the
wall.

That low ring is not wasted. It is exactly the right height for the things that
do not need you to stand: beds, benches, low shelves, a desk with a window. A
well-planned round room puts the low things in the ring and keeps the tall,
open middle for walking, cooking and the table. The picture shows a dome
furnished that way.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** a big sheet of paper, a pencil tied to a piece of string, a ruler,
coloured pencils, and card for furniture.

1. **The floor.** At one centimetre to the foot, draw a circle {{mb.plan_cm}} cm
   across. That is the reference dome's floor.
2. **Standing room.** From the same centre, draw a second circle
   {{mb.plan_stand_cm}} cm across. Inside it, a grown-up can stand up straight.
   Shade the ring between the two circles.
3. **Furniture.** Cut out a bed, a table, a stove, a desk and some shelves from
   card, to the same scale (a bed is about 3 by 6.5 feet: 3 by 6.5 cm).
4. **Place them.** Low things in the shaded ring, tall things and walking space
   in the middle.
5. **Choose a purpose.** Make a second plan for a completely different use -- a
   workshop, a greenhouse, a studio.
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
A hemisphere's ceiling height drops from its full height at the centre to
nothing at the wall, so a fixed headroom draws a smaller circle inside the
floor. Two fixes exist and both are priced in the volumes: a short straight
wall under the rim lifts the whole dome and pushes the standing circle outward
(a *pony wall*), and furnishing the ring with low built-ins turns it from dead
floor into storage. Because the frame does not care what the room is for, the
same plan can be redrawn for any use.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
You have two plans for the same dome with different uses, and the bed is in the
ring in both.
:::
