On rung one a triangle held and a square fell over. Here is the test done
properly, with one more experiment added at the end -- the one that shows how
far a triangle forgives a stick that is not straight.

The picture shows what you are about to see. Push the top of a rectangle
sideways and it *racks*: the corners turn and it leans over, even though
every stick stays the same length. A rectangle can only stand up if something
else holds its corners square -- a sheet nailed across it, or a stick running
corner to corner. That corner-to-corner stick turns the rectangle into two
triangles, and *that* is what holds it.

A triangle has nothing to turn. Its three corners are fixed by the lengths of
its three sides. For the triangle to change shape, a side would have to get
longer or shorter, and wood is very bad at changing length when you push or
pull it from the ends. That is the whole secret of a dome, and the reason a
dome needs no walls to brace it.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** eight strips of card about 15 cm long and 2 cm wide, a hole punch,
and paper fasteners (split pins).

1. Punch a hole near each end of every strip.
2. Join four strips into a square and three into a triangle, one fastener in
   each corner, loose enough to turn.
3. Lay both flat and push one corner sideways. Watch the square fold.
4. Add the eighth strip across the square, corner to corner. Push again.
5. **The forgiving test.** Make a second triangle, but bend one strip into a
   gentle curve before you fasten it. Push. Now make a third with one strip
   bent into a deep curve. Push that one.
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
A shape made of hinged sides holds only if its side lengths fix its angles.
Three sides do; four or more do not. Load on a triangulated frame therefore
runs *along* its members, as push and pull, which is the direction wood is
strongest. That is why a gently bowed stick still works in this frame -- the
triangle holds it in line -- and also why the forgiveness has a limit: a stick
bowed so far that it bends further when pushed lets the triangle squash. Volume
B grades every wedge for exactly that before it goes on the jig.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
You can say why the square needed the extra strip and the triangle did not,
and which of your bent triangles a builder would reject.
:::
