A dome stands on its bottom ring: {{mb.rim_edges}} edges and {{mb.rim_corners}}
corners touching the ground in a circle, as the picture shows from above. All
the weight of the building comes down through those ten corners.

That ring is the one place where a small mistake grows instead of
disappearing. A frame absorbs a short stick or a crooked one, because the
triangles share the error between them. But if the ring is not level, or not
round, every ring above it is built on a tilted or squashed circle, and the
error is carried all the way to the top. A corner a little low at the bottom
means a top a lot off at the apex.

The good news is that the ring is easy to check, with nothing but a string.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** your paper or straw dome, a flat board or tray, a coin, a ruler and
a piece of string.

1. **Level.** Stand the dome on the flat board. Measure from the board to the
   top point with the ruler.
2. **Low corner.** Slide a coin under one bottom corner. Measure the top again.
   How far did the top move compared with the thickness of the coin?
3. **Round.** Take the coin out. Stretch the string across the ring from one
   bottom corner to the corner exactly opposite, and mark it. Do the same for
   the other four opposite pairs. Are all five the same?
4. **Squash it.** Press the ring gently so it becomes a little oval, and measure
   the five pairs again. Which numbers changed?
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
A circle has the same width in every direction, so five equal measurements
across a ten-sided ring prove it is round, and one odd measurement points
straight at the corner that is wrong. The real builder makes this check when
the ring is first set, again after the first course, and again before the top
closes -- because the ring is the one error the frame cannot forgive. On real
ground, the ring stands on gravel, a deck, piers or a slab, and it is strapped
down: wind blowing over a dome tries to lift it, like a wing.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
You can find a low or squashed corner on your dome using only the string, and
say why the ring is checked three times on the real build.
:::
