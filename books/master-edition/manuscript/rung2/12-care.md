On rung one, jobs were sorted into grown-up jobs and helper jobs. This chapter
turns that into a game a family can play before any real work starts -- and
ends with the one rule on a building site that has no exceptions.

Real builders do this. Before a crew starts a job, they talk through who does
what, what can go wrong, and what everybody does if it does. It is called a
*safety briefing*, and the best ones are short, clear and taken seriously by
everybody, including the youngest person on site.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** twenty index cards and a pen.

1. **Write the jobs.** One job per card: felling a tree, cutting a log to
   length, ripping wedges with the saw, carrying a wedge, sorting wedges by
   length, marking wedges with crayon, setting up the jig, screwing a panel,
   sawing the long ends off, oiling wood, lifting a panel, climbing a ladder,
   holding the tape, checking the ring with a string, wiring a light, timing
   the work, drawing the plan, building a model, making lunch, taking photos.
2. **Sort them** into three piles: *trained grown-up only*, *grown-up with a
   helper*, *helper*.
3. **Gear check.** For every card in the first pile, write on the back what the
   person must wear. (Chainsaw work: helmet with a face screen, ear protection,
   gloves, sturdy boots and chainsaw chaps.)
4. **The stop word.** Choose one word that anybody -- anybody at all -- can
   shout to stop all work instantly. Practise it. Everybody freezes, saws go
   off, nobody moves until the person who called it says why.
5. **The rule with no exceptions.** Write it on its own card: *Nobody uses the
   saw alone.*
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
Most accidents happen in the gap between what one person assumed and another
person did. Sorting the jobs out loud closes that gap before the work starts.
The stop word gives the smallest person on site the biggest power: to stop
everything when something looks wrong. On the real build, children stay at
least two tree-lengths away from any tree being felled and well clear of a
running saw, and the volumes add the rules for felling, ladders, lifting and
the trades that only licensed people do.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
Everybody in the family can say the stop word, the rule with no exceptions, and
which pile their own jobs are in.
:::
