The paper dome was taped together in your hands. A real dome is far too heavy
for that: it goes up from the ground, ring by ring, and leans on props until the
top is closed. The straw dome is how you feel that happen.

The straw dome is built the *other* way from the real one. In a straw dome,
each straw is one edge, shared by the two triangles on either side of it -- so
it needs only {{mb.edges}} straws, where the real frame needs {{dome.members}}
members. That is how most domes in the world are built, with hubs. Build it, and
you will understand both the hub dome and the reason the wedge dome does not do
it that way.

The picture shows the real frame's first ring standing on its own, before
anything is above it.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** {{mb.straw_long_count}} straws cut to {{mb.straw_long_cm}} cm (the
long ones) and {{mb.straw_short_count}} cut to {{mb.straw_short_cm}} cm (trim
{{mb.straw_trim_cm}} cm off a long one), pipe cleaners cut into short pieces, a
few pencils for props, and your paper dome as a map.

1. **Joints.** Every corner is a little bundle of pipe-cleaner pieces twisted
   together, one piece poked into the end of each straw that meets there.
   There are {{mb.corners}} corners.
2. **The bottom ring.** Join ten long straws into a ring on the table.
3. **Ring by ring.** Copy your paper dome: every long edge is a long straw,
   every short edge a short one. Build upward from the ring one row of
   triangles at a time, going all the way round before you start the next row.
4. **Props.** When a row leans in and wants to fall, hold it up from inside with
   a pencil standing on the table. Count your props.
5. **Close the top.** The last five short straws meet at the top corner.
6. **Take the props out**, one at a time. Push gently on the dome.
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
A dome only carries itself once it is closed: until then each ring leans on the
one below and the leaning has to be held. Once the top closes, every triangle
pushes on its neighbours all the way round and down, and the props can come
out. The real frame goes up the same way, in courses -- the first course of
{{ring.first}} panels, the second of {{ring.second}}, then the {{ring.cap}}
round the top -- or, in the author's own method, is joined in the air on a web
of rope between living trees. Your straw dome is {{mb.straw_diameter_cm}} cm
across and {{mb.straw_height_cm}} cm tall.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
Your straw dome stands with every prop removed, and you can say why it needed
props before the top was closed and none after.
:::
