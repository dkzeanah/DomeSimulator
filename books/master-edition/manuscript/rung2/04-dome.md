This is the biggest project on this rung, and it is the dome itself: the same
{{dome.panels}} triangles as the real cabin, in the same two shapes, put
together in the same pattern, at one-{{mb.paper_scale}}th of the size. The
template is at the end of this chapter, on two pages.

Look at the picture first. The real dome's edges come in two lengths, coloured
two ways. The long edge and the short edge are in the ratio {{mb.ratio}} to
one -- close enough to look the same, different enough that a triangle built
with the wrong one will not fit.

There are two kinds of triangle:

* **{{mb.paper_equi}} equal-sided triangles**, every side long;
* **{{mb.paper_iso}} short-sided triangles**, with one long side and two
  shorter ones.

The short-sided triangles always come in groups of five, with their short
sides together and their points meeting at one corner, like the petals of a
flower. Each group of five is a **cap**. There are {{mb.paper_caps}} caps: one
on top, and five round it. The ten equal-sided triangles fill the gaps between
the caps.

When you cut out a cap you will find something interesting. Five triangle
points laid flat side by side do not quite go all the way round: they add up
to {{mb.paper_five_deg}} degrees, not the 360 degrees of a full turn. There is
a thin slice missing -- {{mb.paper_gap_deg}} degrees of it. When you close that
gap with tape, the cap stops lying flat and bulges up into a shallow point.
**That missing slice is what makes the dome curve.** Every round thing made of
flat pieces gets its roundness exactly that way.

:::box do "Make it"
**You need:** the two template pages (print them at **100 per cent / actual
size** -- the check bar must measure 50 mm), scissors, clear tape, and a ruler.
For a dome {{mb.paper_double_cm}} cm across instead of {{mb.paper_diameter_cm}}
cm, enlarge both pages to 200 per cent.

1. Cut out the six caps along their solid outlines. Crease the dashed lines.
2. Close each cap: bring the two edges of its gap together and tape them. Each
   cap now bulges up like a very flat tent.
3. Cut out the ten equal-sided triangles.
4. Put one cap on the table, point up. That is the top. Tape an equal-sided
   triangle to each of its five bottom edges.
5. Between each pair of those triangles is a gap. Fit a cap into each gap,
   point up, and tape its two upper edges to the triangles on either side.
6. Between each pair of those five caps is one last gap at the bottom. Fit an
   equal-sided triangle into each, point up, one side along the table, and tape
   it to the caps beside it.
7. Stand it up. The bottom edge is a ring of ten long sides. Press gently on
   the top.
:::

:::box why "Why it works"
The dome is a two-frequency geodesic hemisphere: an icosahedron with each edge
halved and the new corners pushed out onto a sphere, then cut at the equator.
Its {{mb.corners}} corners are of three kinds -- {{mb.five_way}} where five
triangles meet (the tops of your caps), {{mb.six_way}} inside corners where six
meet, and {{mb.rim_corners}} round the bottom ring -- and its {{mb.edges}} edges
of two lengths: {{mb.long_edges}} long and {{mb.short_edges}} short. The real
cabin is the same object {{dome.diameter_ft}} feet across. Notice one more
thing: your paper triangles each have their own three edges, and the tape
between them is a seam. The real dome is built exactly that way, which is the
subject of the next chapter.
:::

:::box done "You've got it when"
Your paper dome stands by itself, and you can point to a five-way corner, a
six-way corner and the ten-sided ring at the bottom.
:::
