The dome was standing. But if you stood inside it, you could look up through
every triangle and see the sky. The wind blew straight through. And when it
rained -- splash.

A house needs a coat.

Water is sneaky. It runs downhill, it creeps into cracks, and if it gets inside
wood and stays there, it makes the wood soft and rotten. A house can be strong,
and straight, and beautiful, and still be ruined by a little water that got in
and never got out. So the builder thought about water more carefully than
anything else.

The answer was one coat that keeps out every drop of rain. Just one. Like a
raincoat, or a shower cap pulled down over the whole dome. Everything else
goes *under* the coat, where it stays dry.

And under the raincoat? Warm, soft layers -- like the quilts on a bed. The
builder's quilts can be sewn from old clothes, the ones too worn out to wear.
Each layer makes the house a bit warmer. When winter comes, add another
quilt. The next winter, add another. A house that gets warmer every winter,
like a person putting on one more hat.

There is one more sneaky kind of water: the kind that comes from inside. When
it is warm in the house and cold outside, your breath makes tiny drops on the
cold walls, just like on a cold window. So the builder left the house a way to
breathe, and a place for that water to drip out.

:::box do "Try it together"
Take two pieces of paper and a tray. On the first, lay three strips of paper
side by side with their edges just touching, and tape them. On the second, lay
three strips *overlapping* like roof shingles, the top one over the middle one
over the bottom one. Tilt each like a roof and sprinkle water from the top.
Which one lets water through to the tray?
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
The skin, not the frame, is the part of a dome that has to be right, because
water is what ruins wooden buildings. This design uses exactly one watertight
layer -- a cap over the whole shell -- with insulation underneath it. The
insulation is quilted from recycled clothing: about {{tt_quilt.shirts_per_layer}}
t-shirts a layer, each layer adding R-{{tt_quilt.r_per_layer}}, added one winter
at a time. Moisture from inside is handled on purpose, by letting the shell
breathe and draining water at the rim.
:::
