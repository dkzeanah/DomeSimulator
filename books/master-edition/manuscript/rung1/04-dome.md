If triangles are so strong, how do you make a whole house out of them?

Start with a ball.

Take twenty triangles, all exactly the same, and fit them together edge to
edge. They fold up into a shape like a big jewel, or a twenty-sided game die.
It is almost a ball -- but it is a bit too pointy. You could not live in a
jewel.

So the builder did something clever. Each big triangle was cut into four
smaller triangles, like cutting a sandwich into four. Then every new corner was
pushed out, just a little, until it touched the skin of a perfect ball.

Now the shape is much rounder. It is made of eighty small triangles, and it
looks like a ball made of glass panes.

And then the builder cut the ball in half -- right around its middle, like an
egg cup.

Half a ball sits flat on the ground. It has a floor. It has a roof that comes
all the way down to be its walls. That is a house: a **dome**.

This dome has {{dome.panels}} triangles. Count them in the picture if you
like. And here is the best part: there are only *two* kinds.

* {{dome.aaa}} of them have all three sides the same length.
* {{dome.bab}} of them have one long side and two sides that are a little
  shorter.

So in the whole house there are only two lengths of stick: a long one and a
slightly shorter one. If you can cut two lengths, you can build the whole thing.

:::box do "Try it together"
Find something in your house made of flat shapes put together into a round
one: a soccer ball, a lampshade, a wooden toy. Count the shapes. Are any of them
the same?

If you have a soccer ball, look for the five-sided patches. There are always
twelve of them, on every soccer ball in the world. Rung six tells you why.
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
The shape is a two-frequency ("2V") geodesic hemisphere. It begins as an
icosahedron, the twenty-faced solid; every edge is halved, the midpoints are
pushed out onto the sphere, and the sphere is cut at its equator. The result
has {{dome.panels}} panels, {{dome.vertices}} corners and only two edge lengths,
in the ratio {{mb.ratio}} to one. The reference dome is {{dome.diameter_ft}}
feet across and {{dome.height_ft}} feet tall at the centre.
:::
