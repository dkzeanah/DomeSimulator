The wedges were not perfect. Some were a little bent. Some had a knot, or a
bump, or one side a little fatter than the other. A tree is not a factory.

A worried builder might say: *How can I build a house out of sticks that are
not perfect?*

The answer is a shape. The **triangle**.

Here is how to see it. Make a square out of four sticks, with a loose pin at
each corner. Now push the top corner sideways. What happens? The square leans
over. It squashes into a diamond, and then it falls flat. Nothing broke -- the
sticks are still the same length -- but the square could not hold its shape.

Now make a triangle out of three sticks, with a loose pin at each corner. Push
it. Push it harder.

It will not lean.

A triangle cannot change its shape unless one of its sticks gets longer or
shorter. And a stick does not want to get longer or shorter. So the triangle
holds.

That is why bridges, and swings, and roofs are full of triangles. And that is
why the builder's house is made of nothing *but* triangles. When every part of
a building is a triangle, the building holds its shape all by itself -- even if
the sticks are a little bent, or a little bumpy.

The builder liked to say it like this: *your head is not perfectly even on
both sides either, and it still protects your brain just fine.*

The sticks are allowed to be a little bit wrong. The triangles make them
strong.

:::box do "Try it together"
Cut seven strips of cardboard or stiff paper, all about the same length, and
make a hole near each end. Join four into a square and three into a triangle,
with paper fasteners (or a pencil poked through) at the corners.

Push each one sideways. Which one wobbles? Now add one more strip across the
square, corner to corner. What happened to the wobble?
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
A square frame is a mechanism: its corners turn and it racks over. A triangle
is fixed by the lengths of its three sides, so a frame made only of triangles
carries loads along its members -- push and pull, end to end -- rather than by
bending them. That is what lets a split log with a bow, a knot or an uneven
section be a structural member here. The two parts that do have to be right are
the skin, because water gets in, and the ground, because everything sits on
it. Later rungs return to both.
:::

:::box wonder "Wonder question"
How many triangles can you find holding something up, in your house or on your
street?
:::
