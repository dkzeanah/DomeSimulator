Have you ever sat at a table with one short leg? Every time you lean on it, it
wobbles. Clunk. Clunk.

A dome is a little like that table. All of its weight comes down through its
bottom ring: a circle of {{dome.base_sides}} corners sitting on the ground. If
the ground under one corner is lower than the others, or soft, or sinks when
it rains, the whole dome leans a little -- and a dome that leans a little at
the bottom leans more at the top.

So the builder said: *the frame is allowed to be a little bit wrong. The ground
is not.*

The ground under the ring has to be level, all the way round. And it has to be
strong enough not to sink. There are lots of ways to make good ground: a ring
of flat stones, a bed of packed gravel, a wooden deck on short posts, or a
concrete floor. The builder checks the ring by measuring straight across it,
corner to corner, in several directions. If every measurement is the same,
the ring is round. If one is different, the ring is telling you where it is
wrong, while it is still easy to fix.

And here is an idea that is bigger than one house. What if one family owned a
good flat piece of ground with water and electricity ready, and another family
brought their dome and set it down there? The ground stays; the dome can move
on someday. The builder calls that ground a **pad**.

:::box do "Try it together"
Make a ring of ten paper cups or toy blocks on a pillow, and set a plate on top.
Now do the same on a flat table. Push gently on each plate. Which one rocks?

Then measure across your table ring with a string in three different
directions. Are the three the same?
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
The base ring is the one place in the method where error does not get absorbed
-- it accumulates, because every course above stands on whatever circle the
ring actually is. That is why the ring is checked across opposite corners when
it is set and again as the first courses go on. Footings range from gravel to
a slab; the options and their costs are on rung three. A pad is serviced ground
built once and shared or rented, so the dome on it can be moved.
:::
