Building a house is a team job. And on every good team, everybody has the right
job.

Some jobs are grown-up jobs. The chainsaw is a grown-up job: it is sharp, and
fast, and very loud, and it does not care whose leg is in the way. Cutting
down a tree is a grown-up job too, and even grown-ups who have done it a
hundred times are careful every single time. Climbing ladders, lifting heavy
triangles, working with electricity and water pipes -- all grown-up jobs.

When a grown-up uses the chainsaw, they wear special clothes. A hard hat with a
screen over the face. Ear protectors, because the saw is loud enough to hurt
your ears. Thick gloves. Strong boots. And special trousers called **chaps**,
full of tough threads that can stop a chain if it ever touches them.

And there is one rule the builder never, ever breaks: **nobody uses the saw
alone.** There is always somebody else there -- standing well back, watching,
ready to help.

So what can a helper do? Lots!

A helper can count the sticks and sort them into piles by length. A helper
can mark each stick with a crayon so everybody knows which pile it belongs to.
A helper can hold the end of the measuring tape. A helper can draw the plans,
time the work and write down the numbers. A helper can build the paper models
that show everybody what the real house will look like. A helper can help oil
the wood -- with gloves on. And a helper can ask the best questions of all.

The most important job on any team is the one that keeps everybody safe.

:::box do "Try it together"
Make two lists on one piece of paper. On one side, write "Grown-up jobs". On
the other, "Helper jobs". Go through the twelve chapters of this rung and put
every job you can think of on one list or the other. Is there anything you are
not sure about? Ask!
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
Children stay well outside any felling zone -- at least two tree-lengths from a
tree being cut -- and away from a running saw. Chainsaw work needs a helmet with
a visor, hearing protection, gloves, boots and chainsaw chaps, and a second
person present every time. Oily rags from finishing can catch fire by
themselves: they go straight into a metal tin of water. Before anyone sleeps
in a building made this way, an engineer and the local building department
must have seen it. Rung three turns all of this into a written plan.
:::
