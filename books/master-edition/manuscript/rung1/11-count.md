How long does it take to build a house?

For most houses, the answer is: a long time, a lot of people and a lot of money.
The builder of this book wanted to know exactly how long *this* house would
take, so they counted.

They counted the hours of cutting wedges at the log: about {{hr.split}} hours.
They counted cutting the ends of the sticks, and building the triangles on the
jig, and lifting them up into a dome. And then -- because the first time you
build anything, things go wrong -- they added extra hours just for mistakes.

All together the frame came to about {{clock.total}} hours. A grown-up's
working week is {{clock.week}} hours. So the frame of the house fits inside one
week of work. That is why this book is called *The 40 Hour Cabin*.

But the builder was careful to say what the forty hours do *not* include:
cutting down the trees, putting the coat on, the floor, the door, the windows.
Those come after, and they take longer. All of it together is about
{{money.labour_hours}} hours of building.

The builder was also careful about something else. Some of those numbers were
*measured* -- timed with a clock while the work was really done. Others were
*guesses*, good guesses, but guesses. In this book, every number says which kind
it is. Because a number that pretends to be measured when it was guessed is a
kind of fib, and you cannot build a house on fibs.

:::box do "Try it together"
Get a clock or a timer. Time how long it takes to cut out and tape together
{{mb.timed}} paper triangles. Now work out how long forty would take: forty is
{{mb.timed_times}} lots of {{mb.timed}}. Was your guess before you started close
to the real answer?
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
The forty-hour clock covers the frame, from wedges to standing:
{{clock.split_h}} hours of splitting at the measured rate of
{{hr.split_session}} wedges per {{hr.session}}-hour session, then cutting,
panels and raising as estimates, plus the owner's own error allowance of
{{clock.error_h}} hours. Felling, the skin, the floor and the openings are not
on that clock; the whole build is about {{money.labour_hours}} hours. Measured
and estimated figures are labelled as such everywhere in this book.
:::
