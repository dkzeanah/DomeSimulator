Here is a riddle. What is round, made of triangles, and can be a bedroom, a
workshop, a greenhouse, a sauna, a garage, a studio, a gym and a cold room for
keeping food?

The answer: the same dome.

Every triangle in the dome is an opening. What you put *in* the opening decides
what the building is. Put windows in, and it is full of light. Put a door in,
and you can walk through. Put clear panels in the top, and plants can grow
inside. Put a little chimney through one, and you can have a wood stove. Put
in solid, warm panels, and it is a cosy bedroom.

So the builder thinks of the bare frame like a seed, or like a baby cell in
your body that has not decided yet whether it will be skin, or bone, or brain.
Scientists call a cell like that a **stem cell**. The frame is the stem cell.
The panels decide what it grows into.

And if you change your mind? Take a panel out and put a different one in. The
greenhouse can become a bedroom. The bedroom can become a workshop. The frame
does not mind. It stays exactly the same.

There is one more piece the builder treats as special: a column right in the
middle of the floor that carries water, power and drains. It is the most
expensive part, so it is built to unbolt and move with the family to the next
dome, the way you would take your favourite chair when you move house.

:::box do "Try it together"
Draw a big circle on a piece of paper. That is your dome's floor. Decide what
your dome is for, and draw what goes inside it. Then turn the paper over, draw
another circle, and make the same dome into something completely different.
:::

:::box why "For the grown-up"
The "stem cell" is the project's name for the bare frame, skin, core and one
blank panel, with the building's purpose deferred to the panels hung in its
forty openings. The catalogue prices {{seed.count}} fit-outs on the same body,
from {{seed.cheapest_usd}} dollars for the bare article to {{seed.dearest_usd}}
dollars for the most fitted. Many of them are utility buildings -- storage, a
workshop, a greenhouse, a studio -- which is usually where a family's first
dome earns its keep.
:::
