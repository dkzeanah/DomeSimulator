Before the frame goes up, understand why it will stand: why a rough wedge is a
good strut, what it gives up as a beam, and where the frame forgives and where it
does not. These chapters are the argument the whole method rests on.

:::box do "Before you start"
Rung two's wobble test, built and pushed. Rung three's worksheet on where your
care goes, filled in.
:::

:::box done "Done when"
You can explain to somebody else why the wedge loses to a two-by-four as a beam
and wins as a strut, and why that is the right comparison for a dome.
:::

:::box check "For checkers"
Volume B compares the wedge with a two-by-four on area, stiffness and bending
strength, and prints the column the wedge loses. Is the comparison fair, and is
its conclusion -- that a dome loads its members mostly along their length --
stated with the right caution?
:::
