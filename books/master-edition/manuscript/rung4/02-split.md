The sections become wedges: each one cradled, ripped through the pith three
times over with the chainsaw, and the eight wedges stacked bark-up on spacers to
dry. This is the measured part of the clock, so time yourself and keep the log.

:::box do "Before you start"
A ripping chain, filed; bar oil and mixed fuel; a cradle that stops the round
from rolling; spacer sticks and a dry place for the stack. The saw's setup and
safety page in Volume B has been read by everybody who will be at the log.
:::

:::box done "Done when"
The pile holds every member the frame needs plus spares, graded in four passes --
bend, knot, bearing end, pith -- and your own rate, in wedges per session, is
written down.
:::

:::box check "For checkers"
Is the claim that a small consumer saw can rip six-foot green sections, at the
measured rate, believable to someone who runs saws for a living?
:::
