Cut the joints: the one compound cut at the butt, the head left deliberately long,
the flush cut that makes the panel true, and the key in every seam. This is where
the tolerance of the whole method lives.

:::box do "Before you start"
The cut list from rung three, worksheet five. A saw setup for each distinct butt
cut, proved on one offcut before the class is cut. Key stock for both profiles.
:::

:::box done "Done when"
Every member is cut to its class, marked, and stacked by class, and a test butt
cut sits flat on its neighbour's face with no rocking.
:::

:::box check "For checkers"
The volumes describe the butt cut, the head overfit and the keys in several
places. Do they agree with each other -- on the overfit allowance, on whether
the build uses one key profile or two, and on when the keys go in?
:::
