# To Whoever Is Holding This Copy

Thank you for reading this.

What you are holding is the whole of this project in one place: a way to frame a
small round building from split logs and one chainsaw, told six times over at
six depths, with both of the books I am writing about it bound in at the back. It
is not finished. It is a proof -- the copy I want checked by people I trust before
anybody pays for any of it.

## Why it is built like this

The idea for this edition is simple. A parent should be able to read the first
part of it aloud to a child at bedtime, and that child should be able to climb
the rest -- to understand it, plan it, build it, design their own and push past
what anybody has measured yet -- as far as they want to go, in whatever order
they want to go. Buckminster Fuller wanted housing cheap enough that a mother was
not so burdened by it that she could not get on with raising her child. A book
that a mother can read to her son, and that the son can grow up into, is my
attempt at the same thing from the other end.

So the same twelve ideas are told on every rung: simply at the bottom, completely
at the top, and every chapter points to the same idea one rung down and one rung
up. Rungs one to three are written for this edition. Rungs four to six send you
into the two volumes, in order, for the full build, the full catalogue and the
experiments still to run.

## Built by a veteran, for veterans

I came home from the Navy to the same question I had at eighteen. On one night in
{{mb.vets_when}}, {{mb.vets_pit}} veterans had no home of their own, and
{{mb.vets_out}} of them were unsheltered. This method suits the way a lot of us
already work: a small, squared-away space, a system you maintain on a schedule,
parts you swap in the field, and a crew that builds together. I want it to be
built by veterans, in villages of them, beside the programs that already exist
to house us -- {{mb.vets_programs}}. There is a reading path for that on the
next pages.

## What I am asking

Read whatever you have time for -- the paths on the next pages suggest a route for
an hour, an evening or a weekend -- and answer four questions as you go.

1. **Is it clear?** Where did you get lost, bored or confused?
2. **Is it right?** Mark every number, claim or instruction you think is wrong.
3. **Is it safe?** Mark anything you would not want a first-time builder to do
   exactly as written.
4. **Would you build it -- or back it?** And if not, what would change your mind?

Mark the page itself if you are reading on paper: **✗** for wrong, **?** for
unclear, **!** for unsafe and **★** for something to keep. There is a feedback
sheet at the back, and a page that gathers every question I most want a second
opinion on.

## What I already know

The two books describe two different domes, because they turn "six feet" into a
size in two different ways. A page near the back sets them side by side; please
check it rather than finding it the hard way. Some pictures in Volume B are marked
as illustrations still to come. And both books are still being revised: this copy
was built on {{mb.built}} from their manuscripts as they stood that day, and every
number in it was computed that day by the same software that solves the dome.

Be hard on it. The numbers that do not help the argument are the ones I most
want found.
