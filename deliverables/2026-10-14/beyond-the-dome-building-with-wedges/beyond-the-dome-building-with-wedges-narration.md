## 02. Why wedges

Time: 00:00:22.933 - 00:00:48.849

The log does the geometry.

A log split through its centre, halved, halved again and halved once more, gives 8 wedges of exactly 45.0 degrees, and nobody measures an angle. The saw takes only its kerf: 89 percent of a 12 inch log is kept as members. And because every flat face runs through the heart, wedges move least as they dry: pine shrinks 7.4 percent around its rings and 4.8 across them, and a radial face sits square across the rings.

## 04. Square on demand

Time: 00:01:10.219 - 00:01:45.297

Two wedges, slant to slant.

Now take two. Turn one end for end and lay it against the other, slant to slant. What you hold looks like a two by four pushed sideways, with corners of 67.5 and 112.5 degrees. Plane it square to its long face and the board inside it is the radius times the cosine of the point angle, by the radius times the sine. For an eighth, those are the same number. The pair planes to a square, 3.87 inches on a side. It costs wood: it keeps 53 percent of the log, against 89 for the wedges as they are. So you square only what needs it. Square, on demand.

On-screen math:

- theta = 45.0 deg: cos = sin = 0.707
- square: 3.87 in
- kept: 53% vs 89% as wedges
