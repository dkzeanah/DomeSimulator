# The 40 Hour Cabin — Kickstarter Reward Tiers

Every pledge, add-on and stretch goal, with the numbers the campaign's
own costing produces (`kickstarter.py` — a tier's *contribution* is what
reaches the goal after the reward is made and shipped, and the campaign
says so on the page).

**The naming rule:** every tier is named for the *thing* it buys and the
*person* it makes you, never for the price. Prices are real; the names
carry the intangible half of the payment.

---

## The ladder

### $1 — The First Stone
**What it is.** Nothing but the name. Your name — or a name you give us —
on the backer wall in the next edition of the book, on the campaign page
forever, and in the credits of the film series. The wall is a page, not a
database: the first hundred names will be read, not counted.
**Why it exists.** Because the person with one dollar and the person with
nineteen thousand are building the same house, and the campaign should
say so in its cheapest tier.
**Contribution.** $1, minus nothing.

### $35 — The Drawings and the Numbers *(early bird $29, first 200)*
**What it is.** The plan set: every table in the book, the cut list for
any diameter, and the solver that made them. A download, priced at what
it costs to serve it — the two dollars is card fees and hosting.
**Why you want it.** This is the tier for the person who wants to check
the arithmetic before they believe it. The campaign's whole reputation
rests on people doing exactly that.
**Contribution.** $33 to the goal.

### $95 — The Quilter's Kit *(early bird $79, first 300)*
**What it is.** Pattern, thread, binding, and the layer specification —
how big, how thick, how the edge closes. Sew a layer for your own dome,
or sew one for somebody else's and get paid for it.
**Why you want it.** This is the tier that makes the campaign more than
a store: it is a job offer you can hold in one hand. Insulation in this
building is a waste stream and somebody's evening — and the campaign
pays for evenings.
**Contribution.** $37.

### $180 — One Bay's Hardware Set
**What it is.** Four threaded inserts, the flange screws, the spline
gasket and the seam key for one triangle. The smallest piece of the
building that is a real object rather than a drawing.
**Why you want it.** Hold the shape in your hand. It weighs almost
nothing, it costs what a dinner costs, and it is the exact joint that
lets rough, split timber frame a dome without a machined connector.
**Contribution.** $68.

### $640 — The Pad Host's Pack
**What it is.** The platform drawings at five foundation types, the
service-port spec, the rim latch pattern, and the host's costing sheet.
**Why you want it.** For somebody with land who wants a dome to be able
to land on it. You are not buying a building here; you are buying the
handshake that lets someone else's building arrive.
**Contribution.** $245.

### $1,450 — The Shower Cap
**What it is.** The rain-slick outer cap, made to the reference
diameter, hemmed with grommets and strapping. The only watertight layer
in the building — and the one you cannot sew at home.
**Why you want it.** Because of the rule the whole method rests on: a
dome is a head, and it wears hats, and exactly one of the hats has to
keep the rain out. This is that hat.
**Contribution.** $470.

### $2,400 — The Utility Hub
**What it is.** The centre column, assembled and tested: pad port, water
manifold, drain stack, sub-panel, riser and seal cap. The part an owner
cannot make, and the part the goal exists to get right — three
iterations are budgeted to get it right.
**Why you want it.** It is the spine of the house. Everything the
building needs passes through it, and buying it is buying the one piece
the fortnight does not cover.
**Contribution.** $780.

### $11,400 — Dome Kit, Bring Your Own Trees
**What it is.** Everything except the frame. You fell, split and cut the
members from your own land — which is the whole argument — and we send
the rest.
**Why you want it.** This tier is the book, turned into a box. The most
expensive part of your house is already standing in your woods, and it
is already paid for.
**Contribution.** $3,300.

### $19,800 — Dome Kit, Frame Included
**What it is.** Everything, including the timber, for somebody with no
trees. The expensive version, honestly priced: you are paying for
somebody else's 45 per cent recovery.
**Why you want it.** The same building, no land required, no saw
required, no fortnight required — and the price printed here with the
margin named, because a campaign that will not say its margin is hiding
one.
**Contribution.** $4,900.

## The sponsor tiers — the intangible payment, made concrete

### $5,000 — Sponsor a Quilter's Year
Fund a year of the network: machines, shipping, and the first paid
layers. Your name on the quilt-network page and on the tag of the first
layer your pledge pays for. The layer keeps your name forever — every
winter it is in a wall, your name is in the wall.

### $10,000 — A Name on the Test Platform
Your name on the pad plate of the standing test dome — the dome the
campaign leaves out in a year of weather with sensors in its walls. What
your money bought is a building that will be measured and published
about. That is a longer monument than most plaques.

### $25,000 — Found the Shop
Your name on one of the machines the goal buys — the router, the
plasma table, or the laser. The machine will cut panels for domes for
years, and its nameplate will say who put it there. First backer at this
tier receives the finished frame's first panel set, signed.

## The backer wall (every tier, no extra charge)

Every backer's name — or the name they dedicate their pledge to — on
the campaign page, in the next edition of the book, and in the film
credits. The wall is the campaign's promise that the intangible half of
every pledge will be kept as carefully as the physical half is shipped.

## Fulfilment, said plainly

Plans and downloads go out the week the campaign funds. Kits, caps and
hubs ship in backer order as tooling lands, on the published timeline —
and the shipping itself is a goal line, budgeted at cost, because a
campaign that funds its tooling out of its shipping budget delivers
neither.
