Take one design and move a single slider: the radius. Everything else is held exactly where it was. Twenty feet across, thirty-three feet across, fifty-two feet across. Two hundred and ninety-five square feet up to two thousand and ninety-six. And the part list on the bench in front of you never changes. The counts are identical across all three. The same struts, the same hubs, the same panels. And the hub bill is identical to the same dollars at twenty feet as it is at fifty-two. What does move is the stick and the skin, because those are priced by the meter and the square meter. Seven times the floor off the same list of parts. The hardware is a range of sizes, shipped with two sizes up in mind as the base level. That is what makes one good hardware set worth paying for: it is the part of the house that does not change when the house does, so it is the part you buy once and keep.
