## 01. Built in the air

Time: 00:00:00.000 - 00:00:38.700

Forty triangles, hung from a web.

Most domes are built from the ground up, a ring at a time, with scaffolding inside. This one is built in the air. A web of cable strung between four living trees, and every triangle of the dome hauled up by a battery winch, swung into its place, and left hanging there on three cords. Then joined, gathered to one point at the top, skinned, and perhaps never let down. The ground, the foundation and the gasket can wait. Here is how, with every load worked out, and the numbers that argue back.

- web at 8 m

## 17. Lashed, not screwed

Time: 00:09:58.459 - 00:10:48.029

Cord, strut to strut, under a hard shell.

And the seams need not be screwed. A dome works mostly in compression: its triangles lean on each other. A joint only has to stop them sliding apart, and cord can do that. Lash each of the 55 seams in 3 places, 8 turns round both struts, and it takes 165 lashings and about 1,101 metres of cord. Cord stretches and creeps, so lashings want re-tightening, and they suit best a dome whose shell, fibreglass, say, takes the pulling. Under a hard shell, the lashings only hold the shape.

## 22. A silicone skin

Time: 00:14:02.178 - 00:15:05.898

Cotton, soaked in silicone dissolved in naphtha.

The covering. Sailcloth would do, at about 25 dollars a square metre. The owner's way is cheaper: dissolve one hundred percent silicone in naphtha, soak cotton in it, and let the naphtha evaporate. The silicone stays in the fibres, a waterproof, chemically quiet skin. Two layers over 51 square metres of dome, with laps, take about 55 tubes of silicone, 65 litres of naphtha and 112 square metres of cotton: about 1,486 dollars, against 2,809 in sailcloth, and 55 kilograms. Silicone sticks to silicone, so layers stack into one skin; almost nothing else will stick to it later. Naphtha is very flammable: soak outdoors, far from any flame.

On-screen math:

- area 51.1 m2 + 10% = 56.2 m2 x 2 layers
- silicone 16.9 kg = 16.2 L = 55 tubes
- naphtha 4:1 = 65 L (estimate)

## 30. What the trees ask in return

## 31. Never under the load
