# The Shop the Money Buys — pitch script

*A Kickstarter film about tooling. Landscape and phone cuts; every number
spoken here is computed by the repository's own models and is marked in
`deliverables/pitch/the-shop-the-money-buys.md` against its source.*

**Running time:** about four and a half minutes at a documentary pace.
**Speaker:** the builder, on camera, in the shop, not in a studio.
**Rule for the whole film:** no claim that is not either measured on screen or
named as an allowance. Where the campaign's own budget is quoted, it is quoted
as the campaign's.

---

## 1. Cold open — the sheet (0:00–0:25)

**Picture.** A single sheet of steel on a rack: 10 feet by 5, the size a
fabricator buys. Camera walks its length. No music yet.

**Voice.** "This is ten feet of steel by five. It cost less than the phone
you're watching this on. In about forty minutes, a machine I built is going to
cut the whole bottom half of a building out of it — forty panels, no template,
no measuring."

**On screen.** `SHEET: 120 x 60 in` · `PLATE: 3 mm` · `PANELS: 40`

---

## 2. The turn — what the money was for (0:25–1:05)

**Picture.** The campaign page on a monitor, the goal's lines scrolling. Cut to
the shop: an empty bay where the machine will go.

**Voice.** "When we set the campaign goal, we did the honest thing and costed it
line by line. Eleven thousand eight hundred dollars for a CNC plasma table.
Fourteen thousand five hundred for a CNC router. Those were real quotes for real
machines, and they were the two biggest lines in the ask after tooling."

**On screen.** `GOAL: $110,000` · `CNC PLASMA: $11,800` · `CNC ROUTER: $14,500`

**Voice.** "Then I read the plans, bought the parts, and found out what those
machines cost when you build them on the same frame. This film is that
difference, and what it pays for instead."

---

## 3. The numbers, spoken plainly (1:05–2:10)

**Picture.** The parts laid out on the bench as they are named: rack, pinion,
rails, block, a pinion gear turning in a hand. Then the bill, on screen, line by
line.

**Voice.** "The drive is rack and pinion, the way the plans describe it. Mod
one-point-five rack, half a metre a length. Ten feet of travel needs eight
lengths a side on the long axis and five on the gantry. The pinion is
fifteen-tooth, twenty-two and a half millimetres pitch diameter, so one turn of
the motor moves the axis just under seventy-one millimetres — twenty-two point
six four steps per millimetre on the controller. Get that number wrong and every
part you cut is the wrong size, which is why it's the first thing the plans tell
you to set."

**On screen.** `RACK: mod 1.5 · 500 mm` · `PINION: 15T · Ø22.5 mm` ·
`70.69 mm/rev` · `22.64 steps/mm`

**Voice.** "The rails are HGR20, in kits of two lengths and four blocks. Ten
feet of travel wants five kits on the long axis, joined and aligned, plus one
for the head. The rest is motors, drivers, a supply, a torch height controller,
steel tube, plate for the carriage, slats, and a 50-amp plasma cutter with a
pilot arc."

**On screen.** the bill, line by line, with the total landing:
`PLASMA TABLE, BUILT: $5,300`

**Voice.** "Five thousand three hundred dollars, all in, with every part named.
Not eleven eight."

**Picture.** The same bill, then the router lines appearing under it.

**Voice.** "And because a router is the same machine with a different head — the
same frame, the same rack, the same rails — a two-point-two kilowatt spindle, a
ballscrew Z, a spoilboard and dust extraction come to another thousand and
fifty."

**On screen.** `ROUTER CONVERSION: $1,050` · `BOTH MACHINES: $6,350`

**Voice.** "Six thousand three hundred and fifty dollars for both machines. The
campaign budgeted twenty-six thousand three hundred for them separately. That
difference is not a boast — it's what happens when a shop builds its own tools.
And it's the reason the ask's biggest line is tooling: every dollar of it turns
back into machines."

---

## 4. The machine, cutting (2:10–3:05)

**Picture.** The table running. Slats, water pan, the torch trailing a bright
line. Cut to the sheet being loaded, the floating head touching off, the arc
starting. Real sound: the air, the arc, the steppers. No narration for fifteen
seconds — let it cut.

**Voice.** "Forty parts come off this sheet in one run. The nesting is paired —
two triangles at a time — because two triangles sharing an edge make a
parallelogram, and parallelograms tile a sheet instead of leaving slivers of
scrap between them."

**Picture.** The nest filling the screen, drawn as the model draws it.

**On screen.** `SHEET: 120 x 60 in` · `PANELS: 40` · `ROWS: 4` ·
`PLATE IN A PART: 77%`

**Voice.** "Two sizes of panel: nineteen-inch long members and seventeen-inch
short ones. Four rows, five pairs a row, seventy-seven per cent of the sheet
inside a part. The offcut is one strip down the edge, and it becomes the stove's
door and the tub's rim."

---

## 5. What the sheet becomes (3:05–3:55)

**Picture.** Four objects in the shop, in this order: a bowl-shaped vessel
upside down with someone sitting in it; a wood burner with a fire in it; a
shelter with the lid bolted on; a chair. Each shot short.

**Voice.** "Five feet across, two and a half feet deep, two hundred and sixty-six
gallons if you fill it. That's a tub, turned over it's a changing room, welded
with a door and a flue it's a wood burner — and because a sphere has no corners
to burn through, the firebox lasts."

**On screen.** `VESSEL: 5.14 ft across` · `266 gal` · `3 mm: 186 lb of panels`

**Voice.** "Two hundred and sixty-six gallons, out of one sheet, on a machine
that cost five thousand three hundred dollars. Now scale the same idea up."

**Picture.** A 3V plate shell being tacked, or the drawing of one.

**On screen.** `ONE-PIECE PANEL: up to 69.3 in` · `3V DOME: 18.7 ft` ·
`274 sq ft of floor`

**Voice.** "A panel sixty-nine inches on a side is an eighteen-foot dome in
welded plate, with two hundred and seventy-four square feet of floor. Bigger
than that, you cut the panel in segments and weld it on the bench — staggered,
so no two joints line up. That's how every big plate dome is actually built."

---

## 6. The honest part (3:55–4:20)

**Picture.** The builder at the bench, welding. Gloves, spatter, the real speed
of it.

**Voice.** "What the money does *not* buy if you build your own machine is a
warranty, a support line, and somebody else's evenings of troubleshooting. This
table took me weeks of nights. The guide it came from is published, the parts
are on the list in the book, and every price in that list is either what I paid
or marked as an allowance. If you'd rather buy the machine, buy the machine —
the campaign's lines are honest quotes for that. I built it because the shop
is the thing I'm actually trying to have."

---

## 7. Close — the ask (4:20–4:40)

**Picture.** The table cutting, the sheet becoming a shell, the shell going on a
pad between two trees. Hold on the trees.

**Voice.** "The backing money buys tooling, and this film is part of what tooling
means here: a table that cuts a building's worth of panels out of a single
sheet, a router that makes the jigs for the wooden version, and a year of weather
on a standing dome so we can publish the measurement whichever way it comes out.
Build the machine, cut the shell, put it between the trees. Everything you need
to do all three is in the book."

**On screen.** `THE 40 HOUR CABIN: 3 TREES` · `the book, the plans, the solver` ·
`back it, and the shop gets built`

---

## Production notes

* **Numbers on screen** come from the book's own tokens: `shop.build_usd`,
  `shop.router_usd`, `shop.both_usd`, `shop.goal_plasma_usd`,
  `shop.goal_router_usd`, `shop.x_racks`, `shop.y_racks`, `shop.mm_per_rev`,
  `shop.steps_per_mm`, `plate.panels`, `plate.rows`, `plate.util_pct`,
  `plate.long_in`, `plate.short_in`, `plate.diameter_ft`, `plate.gal`,
  `plate.part_lb`, `plate.v3_side_in`, `plate.v3_diameter_ft`,
  `plate.v3_floor_sqft`, `plate.weld_ft`, `plate.weld_hours`.
* **Cut list for the edit:** the sheet (cold open), the campaign page, the parts
  on the bench, the bill animating to its total, the machine cutting with real
  sound, the nest filling frame, four objects, welding at real speed, the trees.
* **Phone cut:** drop section 3's bill walk to two lines (plasma total, router
  total), keep sections 1, 4, 5 and 7 whole; the nest and the vessel are the
  two images that carry the pitch on a phone.
* **What the film must not do:** claim the built table is equivalent to a bought
  one. It is a different purchase with a different risk, and the script says so
  out loud.
