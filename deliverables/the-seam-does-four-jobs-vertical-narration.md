## 03. Rain in at the ridge

Time: 00:00:52.040 - 00:01:21.832

185 gallons an inch.

## 04. The air barrier

Time: 00:01:21.832 - 00:02:04.080

4 pascals, and a leak stops being a leak.

Second job, and it is the one that changes how you think about leaks. Run the fan so the inside sits about 4 pascals above outside, and the net flow at every gap is outward. A gap in an inward-leaking building admits weather. The same gap in an outward-flowing one does not, because the air is going the wrong way for anything to ride in on. It is 18 cubic feet a minute over 309 feet of seam -- a bathroom extractor. And it only works while it runs, which is why it is a second line behind the cap rather than a replacement for it.

## 05. Drying the wood

Time: 00:02:04.080 - 00:02:35.168

Air along the faces, or through the silica.

Third job. A perforated liner washes air down both sawn faces of every member. That is the surface most at risk in a timber frame and the hardest to reach, and it now has moving air on it permanently. When the weather is too wet for moving air to dry anything, a three-way gate routes the same flow through a silica cartridge instead. The cartridge pulls out of a hatch and goes in an oven.

## 06. Condensing on purpose

Time: 00:02:35.168 - 00:03:28.120

73 gallons a year, and here is what that is worth.

Fourth job, and this is the one the film has to be honest about. Thermoelectric plates on the cold side of the channel, dropping moisture out of the air as liquid straight into the gutter the rain already uses. The appeal is obvious: the plate sits exactly where the moisture is highest and the drain is right there. Now the number. 360 watts of plates make 73 gallons in a year. That is 0.4 inches of rain. At 2.9 kilowatt hours a litre it is several times worse than a compressor, and a compressor is already not how anybody sensible makes water on a roof. So they are a dehumidifier that happens to yield liquid. They are not a water supply. The water supply is the roof.

## 07. What it costs

Time: 00:03:28.120 - 00:04:09.264

$7,549 of module on a dome that lists at $12,036.

And the price, because a system that is half the cost of the building it is fitted to has to argue for itself. The module is $7,549 across the whole dome. The cap, the liner, the gutter and the drain are per foot of seam and grow with the building; the gates, the cartridges and the plates are per seam and grow more slowly. If you fit one part of it, fit the gutter. Water off the roof is the best return here, it needs no power, and it is the only piece that pays for itself in a season.
