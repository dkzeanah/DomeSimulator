## 02. Three directions, three numbers

Time: 00:00:38.538 - 00:01:18.615

Wood shrinks by different amounts depending on which way you measure it.

Green pine doesn't shrink at all until it dries below about 30 percent moisture. From there to bone dry, the handbook gives 7.4 percent round the growth rings, 4.8 percent across them, and almost nothing along the grain. A building settles near twelve percent, which is 60 percent of the way down. So a frame shrinks about 4.4 percent round the rings and 2.9 across. The pictures draw that movement five times life size, so you can see it. Every number on screen is the true one.

- along the grain 0.1% to 0.2%
- none above 30%; stamped S-DRY at 19%
- dried to 12%: (30 - 12) / 30 = 0.60 of it
- e_T = 4.44%   e_R = 2.88%   (drawn 5x)

## 03. One rule decides the shape

Time: 00:01:18.615 - 00:01:58.308

Round the rings shrinks more than across them, and that one difference bends straight lines.

Picture every point on the end of the log moving in toward the pith by the across-the-rings number, and round the pith by the round-the-rings number. Lines that run straight out from the pith stay straight. The rings stay round. Every other straight line bends. And a ring that goes all the way round can't do what it wants. It comes up 5.8 degrees short, so the log end tears open along a radius. That's the check you see on every log end.

On-screen math:

- r' = r (1 - e_R)
- theta' = theta (1 - e_T) / (1 - e_R)
- k = 0.9556 / 0.9712 = 0.98394
- full ring: 360 (1 - k) = 5.78 deg short

## 05. Flatsawn: the cup

## 06. Rings on a slant: the lean

## 07. Boxed heart: the check

Time: 00:03:55.077 - 00:04:38.231

A board with the pith inside holds whole rings, and a whole ring can't shrink the way it wants.

The middle stud holds the pith, so it holds complete rings, and every one of them wants to shrink more round its length than across it. A closed loop can't. So the wood tears along a radius: a V of 5.8 degrees, about seven hundredths of an inch wide where it reaches the face. Where it opens depends on the weakest spot in that board, so you can't know in advance. A 4x4 post sawn from a log this size holds the pith too. That's why posts split.

## 11. Dry first, key last

Time: 00:06:30.385 - 00:07:23.154

Predictable isn't the same as zero, and this project once had the direction backwards.

In the dome, two wedges meet at a seam with a V between their sawn faces, cut green at 22.5 degrees. A correction first. The drying chapter of The 40-Hour Cabin said this V opens as the frame dries. It doesn't. The V is the sector angle less the fold, the fold belongs to the dome, and only the sector shrinks. So the V closes by the same 0.72 degrees, to 21.8, and each face swings in four hundredths of an inch at the room side. A key fitted green is too wide there. Because every wedge moves by the same amount, the fix is a rule instead of a guess: dry the frame, then make the keys.

## 12. What a wedge still does

Time: 00:07:23.154 - 00:08:04.385

The numbers that don't help the argument.

A wedge still shrinks: about 4.4 percent round the rings and 2.9 across, the same as any pine. It's thicker than a 2x, so it dries about five times slower. This film used a textbook log: round, with its rings centred on the pith, no knots, no reaction wood. And it only looked at the end of the piece. Along its length, the youngest wood at a wedge's point shrinks a little more than the rest, so a wedge can bow toward its point. Every wedge the same way, but we haven't measured how much.

- modelled: a round log, centred rings
- not modelled: knots, reaction wood
- not measured: bow along the length
