The limit is the shell area: at this size the layouts run from about five to ten kilowatts of nameplate capacity, and a larger shell has more area to work with. But here is the number that first made this model wrong. A dome shell carries far more panel than a household inside it consumes. Several times more. The first version of this model valued every generated kilowatt hour at the retail price, which quietly assumed the tenant was buying power they would never have bought. So it does not any more. What displaces the tenant's own draw is worth retail. Everything past it is worth export, which is about a third of that. Correcting it took this pad's solar income down by well over half. The ring is still worth fitting. But the real case for cladding a dome in cells is a park that can sell the surplus, not a tenant who cannot use it.
