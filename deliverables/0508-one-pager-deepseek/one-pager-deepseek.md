# The Code-House — One Page

**The 40 Hour Cabin: housing, re-implemented as software.**
*Three trees, one saw, forty hours. A house that ships.*

---

## The premise

Every house is a program written in wood, and most of them are legacy
code: a million lines of unversioned, undocumented, unrepeatable
carpentry, recompiled from scratch on every lot.

This project is the refactor.

It asks one question, the way a good engineer asks it: *what is the
minimum number of operations between a standing tree and a standing
house?* The answer it found is eight. Fell. Buck. Three splits. Dry.
Jig. Cut. The house is the compiler.

## Software principles, applied to shelter

| principle | in the codebase | in the house |
|---|---|---|
| **Single source of truth** | every number derives from the solver; nothing is typed | the cut list, the seam schedule, the book — one solved model, zero drift |
| **DRY** | one function, reused | two member lengths, 120 members, one jig |
| **Minimal dependencies** | a lean toolchain | one $199 chainsaw; no mill, no crew, no loan |
| **Modularity** | objects with interfaces | frame, skin, floor, ground — hot-swappable, replaceable in any order |
| **Separation of concerns** | one layer per job | exactly one watertight layer; everything else breathes |
| **Idempotent processes** | a build step you can re-run | one compound cut, made once, off a jig — repeatable 120 times |
| **Version control** | the repo | the book names its own bugs; corrections ship inside the chapters, never silently |
| **CI / tests** | the arithmetic is a test suite that refuses to print a lie | a year of wall sensors, published whichever way the data comes out |
| **Open source** | the solver is public | the method is public; the moat is the tooling, the network, and the trust |
| **The rewrite** | delete the prototype | the frankendome was the MVP; the wedge is the refactor that shipped |

## The numbers, in one breath

A 365-square-foot cabin. **$692** for the frame — fuel, oil, fasteners.
**37.5 hours** of hands-on work. **3 trees**, 120 members, 40 panels.
A log that keeps 45% of itself as boards keeps **87.4%** as wedges.
The complete dome builds at $10,030 and prices at $12,036, with the
margin named on the page. The campaign raises **$110,000** in twelve
named lines — tooling, sensors, an engineer, the first paid quilters,
the shipping. Every dollar becomes a machine or a measurement.

## Who it is for

**The single mother with an evening.** In this system the insulation is
a quilt — sewn from recycled clothing at a kitchen table, after
bedtime, *paid per layer*. The barrier to entry is a sewing machine.
The work is real, the wage is real, the name goes on the tag.

**The veteran with discipline and no rank to build in.** Written by a
Navy veteran, for the 32,882 veterans the federal count found homeless
on a single night. The build is a crew method: one panel at a time,
one person on a ladder, no crane, no mill — learned, not received.
Stretch goals give a frame to a veterans' housing program as a build
event their residents crew.

**The investor who wants a factory, not a fantasy.** The campaign is
the pre-sale that buys the tooling; behind it sits a small
manufacturing business with a named 20% margin, a pad-host network,
and a published book that funds the work while the hardware scales.

## The ask

Fund the machines and the measurements. In exchange: a housing method
that is checkable, forkable, teachable, and cheap — the **starter repo
of shelter**.

It is not a fancy house. It is humble, starting infrastructure: a
house that has versions, that can be debugged, that a stranger can
rebuild from the published source. A house a codebase could be proud
of.
