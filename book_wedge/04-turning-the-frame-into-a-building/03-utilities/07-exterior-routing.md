# Exterior routing

Once a line is outside the shell, it runs down the outside, and that is a
deliberate choice rather than an admission of defeat.

WHAT IT COSTS

$6.40 a foot for conduit, clips and
weathering.

WHY NOT INSIDE

Because inside means through the insulation, through the vapour control, and
through a bay somebody will want to open later.

And because the thing a service usually connects to -- a solar panel, a tank,
a second building -- is outside anyway. A line that goes out at the apex and
down the outside crosses the envelope once. A line routed internally crosses
it twice and does the second crossing somewhere less convenient.

HOW IT LOOKS

Like conduit on the outside of a building, which is to say, industrial.

Some people mind. The honest answer is that the alternative is worse and the
conduit can be painted the colour of the shell, which is what the reference
build does.

THE PANELS IT FEEDS

Utility panels -- the reference build calls them polyps -- bolt to the
outside of the dome and take their service from this routing. A wall port at
$88 and the service connection at
$95.

Each one is a bathroom, a kitchen, a plant room or a battery bank hung on the
outside of the shell, fed from the top, occupying none of the floor.

That is what the exterior routing is for. It is not tidying-up; it is the
delivery mechanism for everything the building becomes later.
