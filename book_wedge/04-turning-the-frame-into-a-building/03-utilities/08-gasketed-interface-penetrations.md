# Gasketed interface penetrations

A penetration is a hole with a promise attached, and the promise is what
costs money.

THE THREE THIS BUILDING HAS

The floor port, where everything arrives.

The apex sleeve, where everything leaves.

The wall ports, where utility panels connect.

That is it. Three kinds of hole in the entire envelope, all of them designed,
none of them cut later.

WHY GASKETED AND NOT SEALED

A sealed penetration is permanent. A gasketed one comes apart.

Every one of these three is somewhere the building expects to be opened: the
floor port when the dome moves, the apex cap when a service is added, the
wall port when a panel is swapped. Sealant at those points has to be cut out
and remade every time, which means it will not be, and the thing will be
open.

A gasket compresses when the fitting is done up and springs back when it is
undone. It can be done a hundred times and the hundredth is as good as the
first.

WHAT MAKES A GASKET WORK

Compression, evenly. That means a fitting that clamps rather than one that
is screwed down at one edge.

A face to compress against that is flat and clean. On a curved shell that
means a flange, and the flange is the expensive part.

And a groove for the gasket to sit in, so it cannot roll out under
compression. A gasket squeezed between two flat faces will find its way out.

THE FAILURE TO KNOW ABOUT

Gaskets take a set. A gasket left compressed for years does not spring back
as far, and a joint that has been open and shut a dozen times wants a new
one.

They cost a few dollars. Keep spares, and change them when the building is
open anyway.
