# Electrical entry

Electricity comes into this building at one point, low, and goes up.

WHERE

The floor port, in the middle of the pad, under the dome.

Not through a wall. A dome does not have a wall in the sense a service entry
wants -- every surface is curved, sloping and part of the weather envelope,
and a penetration through it is a penetration through the roof.

The middle of the floor is flat, sheltered, and already where everything else
arrives.

WHAT IT IS

A sub-panel with breakers and an outlet ring, priced at
$310 in the reference build. That is the
equipment, not the supply.

The supply is somebody else's number: a spur from a meter, a generator, a
battery bank, or the shared hub the campaign film prices at a quarter of a
four-way panel. Which of those you have changes the cost by thousands and
changes nothing about the dome.

THE RULE THAT MATTERS

Everything electrical goes up the column, and the column is metal and
grounded.

That gives you one chase, one route, and one place to look. The alternative
-- cable run round the inside of a curved shell -- is how you end up drilling
into something you should not have.

SEPARATION

Water rises in the same column. They are in the same object and they are not
in the same chase: the electrical side is its own conduit with its own
clearance, and the wet side drains downward past it.

Three feet of clearance between the electrical and anything wet is the
reference build's rule, and it is the one number in this chapter nobody
should shave.
