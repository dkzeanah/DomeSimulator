# Plumbing

Waste is the part of a small building people plan last and regret first.

THE DRAIN STACK

A drain stack and floor-port tie-in, $165.

Everything wet in the dome drains to the middle of the floor and out through
the pad. That is the opposite of a conventional building, where waste runs to
a wall and out sideways -- and it is the right way round here, because the
middle of the floor is the only place the geometry guarantees is reachable.

FALL

Waste moves because it is running downhill, and in a building on a platform
there is not much downhill available.

A quarter-inch of fall per foot is the number, and the shower tray in the
reference build's wet area is built to exactly that over a three-foot run.
Under a quarter-inch the water moves and the solids do not, which is the
failure people describe as "the drain keeps blocking" and is actually "the
drain was never going to work".

WHERE IT GOES

Off the pad and into whatever the site has: a sewer connection, a septic
field, a holding tank, or a greywater system with a composting toilet
handling the rest.

That last combination is the one that suits this building best, because it
halves the waste problem and removes the one part of it that needs treating.

THE WET AREA

Water and electricity in the same small round room is the thing to design
rather than hope about.

The reference build's answer: a sloped tray that redirects to the drain, two
barriers round the shower rather than one -- a double glass wall or a double
curtain -- and three feet of clearance between anything wet and anything
electrical.

Two barriers is not belt and braces. The inner one takes the spray and the
outer one catches what gets past, which is the same two-line strategy the
shell uses against rain.
