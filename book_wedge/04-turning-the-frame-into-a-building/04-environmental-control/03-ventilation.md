# Ventilation

A small, tight, well-insulated building needs deliberate ventilation, and a
round one needs it more than most.

WHY MORE

A dome has no corners and no natural cross-draught. Air in a sphere wants to
circulate in a loop rather than pass through, and a room with one opening
does not ventilate at all -- it just breathes slowly at that opening.

Two openings, at different heights and on opposite sides, is the minimum that
produces actual airflow.

THE STACK EFFECT IS YOUR FRIEND HERE

Warm air rises, and a dome's highest point is dead centre and nine feet up.

An opening at the apex and an opening low at the perimeter gives you a
chimney: warm air leaves at the top, cool air is drawn in at the bottom, and
it happens without a fan for as long as there is a temperature difference.

That is free ventilation and it comes from the shape.

THE APEX IS ALREADY A HOLE

The seal cap is the obvious place for a controllable vent, and it costs
almost nothing to make it one.

THE SEAM DUCT, AGAIN

Reversible airflow through the seam network is the reference build's
ambition: blow to keep the frame dry, draw to collect what lands on the
shell.

Unproven, as the last section said. But the ports and the fans are priced,
the channel exists whether it is used or not, and a building that has the
ducting in it can experiment later.

THE RULE

Ventilation is not the same as draughts, and insulating a building without
ventilating it is how you get mould.

Tight envelope, deliberate openings, controllable. In that order.
