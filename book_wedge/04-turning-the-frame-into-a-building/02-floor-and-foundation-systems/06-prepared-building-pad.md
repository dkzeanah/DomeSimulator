# Prepared building pad

The pad is what goes under every other option here, and it is the one people
skip.

WHAT IT IS

Strip the topsoil. Cut and fill to level. Compact. Lay graded stone.
Compact again. Drain away from the building.

WHAT IT COSTS

$443 for a 22.4-foot pad.

That is the cheapest line in this whole chapter by a factor of five, and it
is the one that decides whether any of the others work.

WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE THING ON TOP

Every foundation on this list transmits its load into the ground. If the
ground moves, the foundation moves, and it does not matter what the
foundation is made of.

Uncompacted fill settles. It settles for years, it settles unevenly, and a
dome on an uneven base ring racks -- which shows up as doors that stop
closing and panels that will not sit in their bays.

Concrete does not fix bad ground. It spreads the load over more of it, which
helps, and then cracks where the ground moved anyway.

DRAINAGE IS THE OTHER HALF

Water under a building is what turns settled ground into moving ground.

Fall away from the pad in every direction. A swale uphill if the site has
one. Gutters, if there is anything to catch.

None of it is expensive and all of it is boring, which is exactly why it gets
left out.

THE HONEST SUMMARY OF THE CHAPTER

Cheapest to dearest, on the same 22.4-foot pad:

* gravel base only: $443 -- not a floor, and nobody lives on it
* full concrete slab: $2,468 -- and it *is* the floor
* framed deck on piers, sealed: $2,916 -- the staple
* concrete ring, timber middle: $3,039
* framed deck, ply and epoxy: $4,263 -- a floor you can mop

Two things in that list are worth stopping on. The slab is not the expensive
option people assume, because it is the only one that does not then need a
floor built on it. And the ring, which sounds like it ought to be the clever
economy, costs more than the slab it half-replaces.

Neither of those is the answer the argument wanted. They are what the takeoff
says.
