# Fiberglass

Glass fibre in resin is the material the reference build's hard shell is made
of, and it is worth understanding because it sets the cost ceiling for the
whole building.

WHAT IT IS

Cloth -- woven or stitched glass -- wetted out with a resin that cures hard.
Layers build thickness. A core between layers makes it stiff without making it
heavy.

HOW IT IS PRICED

By weight, not by area. Ounces of glass per square foot, times a
resin-to-glass ratio.

That is the industry's own method and it is the thing most amateur costings
get wrong. A rule of thumb in resin-per-square-foot will be right for one
schedule and wrong for every other.

WATCH THE UNITS. Woven and stitched fabrics are sold by ounces per square
*yard*. Chopped strand mat is sold by ounces per square *foot*. Getting that
backwards understates a laminate by a third.

WHAT IT COSTS ON THIS DOME

Over 1,325 square feet of laminate, four named systems:

* sheathed ply in epoxy: $5,395
* boatyard polyester: $6,045
* premium marine polyester: $6,668
* vinyl ester: $9,144

That is the most expensive line in the building by a wide margin -- more than
the frame, the core, the services and the fit-out together.

WHY IT IS STILL WORTH DESCRIBING

Because it is a sixty-year object.

A gelcoated hull outlives every other option in this chapter several times
over, it is genuinely waterproof rather than nearly waterproof, and it is a
trade with real suppliers, real published prices and sixty years of people
knowing how to do it.

THE ARGUMENT AGAINST, WHICH THE NEXT CHAPTER MAKES PROPERLY

It is made once, at one size, and it cannot grow.

The cavity behind it holds four quilted layers and then it is full. After that,
insulating further means losing floor area or building a second shell outside
the first, and neither happens.

So the hull quietly caps how good the building is ever allowed to get. That is
the problem the shower cap exists to solve.
