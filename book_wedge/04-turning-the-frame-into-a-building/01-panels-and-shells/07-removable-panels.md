# Removable panels

A panel you can take off is a different object from a panel you screwed on,
and the difference shows up in year three rather than on the day.

WHY REMOVABLE

Because the cavity behind it is where the insulation goes, and the insulation
is bought a layer at a time over years.

Because a panel gets damaged and replacing one should not mean dismantling
six.

Because a bay's *function* changes -- a blank becomes a window, a window
becomes an exhaust, a solid panel becomes a solar bay -- and the whole
fit-out argument depends on that being an afternoon's work.

HOW THE REFERENCE BUILD DOES IT

The panel sits on the *outside* face of the frame and is pulled into it by
screws landing in threaded inserts set in the wedges.

Four inserts per bay, 160 for the dome.

That arrangement matters more than it sounds. The older version had the panel
compression-fitted into the bay from outside, held by friction -- and most of
a dome's bays are overhead, so a friction fit overhead spends the life of the
building arguing with gravity.

Screwed in, gravity works with the fixing. And the panel comes off with a
driver rather than a pry bar.

THE SECOND JOB THE INSERTS DO

They are a grid of structural anchor points on a known pitch.

Three inserts together hold about 540 pounds, which means a shelf, a bunk, a
hanging rail or a kitchen run can span two or three triangles and bolt to
them.

So a bay does not have to be *used* to be useful. It is a barrier with fixings
in it, and that is worth more than the panel.

THE COST

Inserts and flange screws come to a couple of hundred dollars across the dome,
and about four hours of build time to set them.

That is the price of every future change to the building being a driver job.
