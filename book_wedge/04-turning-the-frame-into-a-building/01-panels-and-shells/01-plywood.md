# Plywood

Plywood is the default and it has one problem on this building, which is worth
getting out of the way first.

NO PANEL FITS A SHEET

The narrowest panel in a 2V hemisphere on a six-foot member is 52.520 inches
across its shortest altitude. The widest AAA panel is 62.354.

A sheet of plywood is 48 inches wide.

There is no rotation, no nesting and no clever layout that gets a panel of
this dome out of a standard sheet. The narrowest one is four and a half inches
too wide.

That is not a small inconvenience. It decides how the entire skin gets built,
and it is the sort of thing that is obvious in a car park and invisible on a
drawing.

WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT

Three options.

Buy oversized sheet. Some suppliers carry 5x10 or 4x10; a 60-inch sheet takes
a BAB panel and a 5-foot sheet takes both. It is a special order and it costs
more per square foot.

Sheet across the frame. Run ordinary sheets over several panels at once,
following the curve in facets, and cut at the frame lines. You get joints in
the middle of panels instead of at their edges, which is structurally
irrelevant and visually obvious.

Build the panel up from boards. Narrower stock, laid edge to edge inside the
bay. More labour, no special order, and it looks like a timber building rather
than a sheet-goods building.

THE REFERENCE BUILD

Sheets across the frame, taped at the joints, because it is the option that
does not require anybody to order anything unusual.

GRADE AND GLUE

If it is inside the envelope and dry, interior-grade is fine.

If it is outside, or if it is part of the removable shell that lives in the
weather, it wants exterior glue at minimum and marine ply if you can afford
it. The failure mode of the wrong glue is delamination, and delamination on a
curved surface is not repairable.
