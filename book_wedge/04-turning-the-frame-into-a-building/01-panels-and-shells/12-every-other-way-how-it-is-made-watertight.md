# Every other way: how it is made watertight

One watertight layer or a laminated hull, and what the quilted layers under the cap add. The reference build is the cap, because two impermeable skins with insulation between them is a trap.

EVERY OTHER WAY OF DOING IT

                                                    cost     weight
    Shower cap, bare                          $    3,265              wood panels, a breather and one rain-slick cap   <- the reference build
    Shower cap, 1 quilted layer               $    3,333              R-2
    Shower cap, 3 quilted layers              $    3,471              R-5
    Shower cap, 7 quilted layers              $    3,748              R-11
    Laminated hull, sheathed ply              $    5,395      222 lb   2 plies, $2.14/sq ft of materials
    Laminated hull, boatyard polyester        $    6,045      631 lb   3 plies, $2.63/sq ft of materials
    Laminated hull, premium marine polyester  $    6,668      631 lb   3 plies, $3.10/sq ft of materials
    Laminated hull, vinyl ester hull          $    9,144      747 lb   4 plies, $4.97/sq ft of materials

The dearest of these is 2.8 times the cheapest. That is the size of the decision, and it is why the book states which one it assumes rather than leaving it to a reader to infer.

This book assumes shower cap, bare. Nothing else in it depends on that: change the row and the rest of the method is unchanged.

WHERE THESE NUMBERS COME FROM

soft_shell and hull_laminate, applied to the reference build's own quantities. Nothing on this page is a rate quoted from outside the project, and nothing on it was typed: regenerate the whole table with `py -3.12 -m wedge_book.alternatives`.

There is no straw-bale row and no earthbag row, because this project cannot cost one. A table with a plausible number in it that came from nowhere is worse than a table with a gap.
