# Door frames

A door is the opening you will use ten times a day for twenty years, and it is
worth more care than any other hole in the building.

WHERE IT GOES

At the base ring, taking one or two triangles.

The base triangles are the only ones where the wall is close to upright, and a
door in a leaning wall is a door you duck through. The dome is 9.708 feet at
the apex and the wall is already tilting in noticeably by the time you are
head height at the perimeter.

That is a real constraint and it is the first thing people are disappointed by.
A nineteen-foot 2V hemisphere does not have a full-height vertical wall
anywhere.

THE EXTENSION ANSWER

The usual solution is not to fight it. Build the door in a small extension --
a porch, a vestibule, a box on the side -- with vertical walls of its own,
joined to the dome at the opening.

That gives you a proper door in a proper frame, a place to take boots off, and
an airlock, which matters more than it sounds in a building with this little
thermal mass.

It also moves the weather detail away from the curved surface, which is the
hardest place to make a door seal.

THE FRAME ITSELF

Build it as a box, independent of the shell, and fix the box to the reinforced
opening.

That way the frame is square even though the hole is triangular, the door is a
normal door, and the joint between the two is a piece of flashing and a gasket
rather than a piece of carpentry nobody has done before.

Do not try to hang a door directly off the dome members. They are at odd
angles, they are split timber with irregular faces, and the door will not stay
square.

THE THRESHOLD

The floor is a platform above ground and the door sill is at platform level.

There will be a step. Plan where it goes and make it a proper step rather than
discovering it at the end, and if the building needs to be accessible, this is
the detail that decides whether it is.
