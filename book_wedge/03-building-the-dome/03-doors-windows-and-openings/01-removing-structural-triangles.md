# Removing structural triangles

Every triangle in a dome is carrying something. Take one out and its load goes
to the ones around it.

That is the whole problem with openings, and it is worth stating before any of
the solutions.

WHAT A TRIANGLE ACTUALLY DOES

A geodesic shell works because it is triangulated. A triangle cannot change
shape without changing the length of a side, and the sides are timber, so it
does not change shape.

Remove one and you have left a quadrilateral or worse in the shell -- a shape
that *can* change without stretching anything. It can rack.

The dome does not fall down. The rest of the triangulation is still there and
it is redundant. But the shell around the hole is now doing a different job
than it was designed for, and the members immediately either side of the
opening are the ones doing it.

HOW MANY YOU CAN TAKE

On a 2V hemisphere with forty triangles, taking one is unremarkable. Taking
two adjacent ones is fine with a frame round the hole. Taking three in a row
at the base ring -- which is what turns a dome into a garage -- wants
deliberate reinforcement and is the largest opening this book will describe
without sending you to an engineer.

WHERE TO TAKE THEM

Low, and at the base ring, in preference to anywhere else.

A base triangle has one edge on the ground, which is already an edge with
nothing on the far side of it. Removing one interrupts less than removing a
triangle from the middle of the shell, where five other panels are relying on
it in five directions.

The apex is the worst place. Five triangles meet there and they are all
holding each other.

THE PART THAT IS EASY TO FORGET

An opening is not only a structural hole. It is a hole in the envelope, a hole
in the insulation, a place water can get in, and an edge that has to be
finished.

The structure is usually the easiest of those four to solve.
