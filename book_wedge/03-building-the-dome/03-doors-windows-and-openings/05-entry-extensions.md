# Entry extensions

An entry extension is a small structure joined to the dome at an opening, and
it solves several problems at once.

WHAT IT SOLVES

A vertical door, in a building with no vertical walls.

An airlock, in a building with very little thermal mass, where opening the
door in January dumps the room's heat in ten seconds.

Somewhere to leave boots, coats and firewood, in a round room with no corners
and no hallway.

And a place to put the weather detail, off the curved surface.

WHAT IT IS

A box. Stud-framed, or built from the same wedge members if you want the
consistency, with a flat roof or a small pitch, joined to the dome around a
reinforced opening.

It does not need to be big. Four feet deep is enough for a door, a step and a
pair of boots.

THE JOINT

This is the only difficult part and it is the same difficulty as any roof
penetration: a flat thing meeting a curved thing.

Two approaches.

Flash it. Treat the joint like a chimney flashing -- an apron under the shell
covering above, sealed at the top, open at the bottom so anything that gets
behind it drains out.

Or gasket it. Build the extension with a collar cut to the dome's curve, and
compress a gasket between the two. This is tidier, more expensive, and depends
on the collar being accurate.

The reference build's shell is removable, which makes the second option
awkward -- anything gasketed to the shell has to come off with it. So: flash
to the frame, not to the shell, and let the shell overlap.

THE THING WORTH SAYING

An extension makes the dome look less like a dome.

Some people mind that a great deal. If you are one of them, the answer is a
door straight into the shell and a life of ducking, and that is a legitimate
choice as long as it is a choice rather than a surprise.
