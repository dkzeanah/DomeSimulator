# Foundation interfaces

The frame has to sit on something, and what it sits on has to do three things:
hold it level, keep it off the ground, and hold it down.

LEVEL

Every base vertex at the same height. Not approximately -- a dome is a closed
shape and a base that is out of level puts a twist into the whole frame.

If the ground is not level, the foundation makes up the difference, not the
frame. Do not shim individual members to fix a sloping site.

OFF THE GROUND

Timber in contact with soil rots. Split timber with exposed grain rots faster.

The base members should be clear of the ground with air under them, sitting on
piers, blocks or a deck rather than bearing on soil.

The reference build stands the frame on a framed platform, which puts the base
ring six hundred-odd millimetres clear of the ground with ventilated space
underneath.

HELD DOWN

This is the one people forget.

A dome is light and it is a very good aerofoil. The load case that matters on
a dome is not weight pushing down; it is wind lifting up.

Every base vertex needs a hold-down to the foundation -- a strap, an anchor, a
bolt into the deck. Ten of them.

The reference build uses a strap, ratchet and ground anchor at each of the ten
base vertices, because the soft shell it uses has no stiffness of its own and
the frame carries the whole uplift.

THE INTERFACE ITSELF

Keep it simple and keep it reachable.

A bracket that bolts to the deck and takes the base member with two fasteners
is enough, and it can be inspected and tightened with the building standing.

Anything cast in, buried, or inaccessible is a joint you will never check
again, and this is the joint that stops the building leaving.
