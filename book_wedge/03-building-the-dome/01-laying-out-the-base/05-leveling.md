# Leveling

Level the foundation, not the frame.

The frame is a rigid closed shape. If you try to level it by adjusting members
you are distorting the geometry, and the distortion goes somewhere -- usually
into the apex, which will not close.

HOW LEVEL IS LEVEL ENOUGH

Every base vertex within an eighth of an inch of the others.

That is tighter than it sounds and looser than perfectionism. An eighth over
nineteen feet is a very flat platform, and it is achievable with ordinary
tools.

TOOLS THAT WORK

A water level -- two clear tubes and a length of hose -- is accurate over this
span, costs almost nothing, and does not care about line of sight.

A rotary laser is faster if you have one.

A spirit level on a straight edge works for short spans and accumulates error
over long ones. For a nineteen-foot circle, do not chain spirit-level readings
round the perimeter.

THE METHOD

Establish one datum point. Every other point is measured to that datum, not to
its neighbour.

That is the same rule as the members: measure from the master, not from the
last one. Chaining measurements accumulates error; referencing a datum does
not.

Go round twice. The second pass catches the points that moved while you were
working on the others, and there will be some.

CHECK AFTER LOADING

A platform that is level empty may not be level with a dome on it, especially
on soft ground or new fill.

Check again once the base ring is down and before you go up. Settlement that
happens at that stage is easy to shim out. Settlement discovered at the apex
is not.
