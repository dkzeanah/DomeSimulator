# Apex closure

The last panel is the test of everything.

WHAT HAPPENS AT THE TOP

Five AAA triangles meet at the apex vertex. When the fifth one goes in, the
dome is closed and self-supporting.

Until then it is an arch. After it, it is a dome.

WHY THE LAST PANEL IS DIFFICULT

Because it has no slack.

Every other panel went into a space with one open side. The last one has to go
into a hole that is already bounded on all three sides, and it only fits if
everything below it is right.

That is what makes it such a good test and such a bad surprise.

IF IT DOES NOT FIT

Do not modify the panel.

A panel trimmed to fit the hole is a panel that is now the wrong size, and the
error is still in the building -- it has just moved into the shape of the
frame rather than the gap.

Measure the hole. Compare it to what the geometry says it should be. The
difference tells you how much error has accumulated and, usually, which
direction it came from.

Then loosen the top course, redistribute, and try again. An accumulated error
spread over five joints is invisible. Concentrated in one, it is a gap you can
put your hand through.

WHEN IT DOES FIT

Fasten it, then take the central post out, and stand underneath.

The dome is now holding itself up with nothing in the middle. That is the
moment the method either convinces you or does not, and on a frame that has
gone together properly it is genuinely surprising how solid it feels.

THE PENETRATION

On the reference build the apex is not solid. It carries a sleeve -- the one
hole in the weather surface, with a gasketed cap over it, through which
services leave the building.

If you are building that version, the sleeve goes in with the last panel and
not after. Fitting it later means working at the top of a finished dome, which
is the thing the whole design is arranged to avoid.
