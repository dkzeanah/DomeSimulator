# Alignment correction

Something will be out. This is what to do about it.

FIND OUT WHETHER IT IS ONE THING OR EVERYTHING

Measure three panels from different parts of the dome.

If one is different and two agree, you have a bad panel. Replace it.

If all three differ from the geometry by the same amount in the same
direction, you have a systematic error -- a jig setting, a stop block, a
fence -- and replacing panels will not help because the next one will be wrong
too.

That distinction is the whole of diagnosis and it takes five minutes.

CORRECTING A SINGLE PANEL

Take it out and rebuild it. Do not shim it, pack it, or trim it in place.

A dome is a closed geometry and a panel that is the wrong size is the wrong
size in three directions at once. You cannot fix it with a shim; you can only
move where it is wrong to.

CORRECTING ACCUMULATED ERROR

Spread it.

If a course is half an inch out over ten joints, loosen all ten and let each
one take a twentieth of an inch. Nobody will ever find it.

If you concentrate it in one joint, everybody will find it, starting with you
every time you walk past.

WHEN TO ACCEPT IT

A dome built to a sixteenth is built better than most houses.

If a vertex is a sixteenth off its mark and everything closes, leave it. Wood
moves more than that with the seasons.

If the apex closed, the base ring is level and round, and no joint is held
open, the frame is good. Stop measuring and put the skin on.

THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING

Every error you find in the frame is an error you found before it was covered
up.

That is worth something. The dome is never going to be easier to correct than
it is right now, with nothing on it and every joint reachable from both sides.
