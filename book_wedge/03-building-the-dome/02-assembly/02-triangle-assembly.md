# Triangle assembly

A panel is three members and three pinwheel joints, built flat on a bench.

Build them all before you raise anything.

WHY FLAT AND WHY FIRST

A joint made on a bench, at waist height, with the workpiece held in a jig, is
a better joint than the same joint made overhead on a ladder.

Every joint in this building can be made on a bench. That is not true of most
construction methods and it is one of the genuine advantages here.

So: forty panels, all made flat, all checked, and then an assembly job that is
purely about putting finished things in the right places.

THE SEQUENCE FOR ONE PANEL

Lay the three members in the jig in the pinwheel arrangement. Each one butts
into the side of the next, all going the same way round.

Check the handedness. All forty panels must pinwheel the same direction --
clockwise seen from outside on the reference build. A panel built the other
way is a mirror image and it will not fit anywhere.

Clamp, pilot, fasten. Two or three fasteners per joint.

Take it out of the jig and check it on a flat floor: sits on all three
corners, no rock, sides match the chords.

Label it and stack it.

THE STACK

Stack panels flat, not on edge, and separate the AAAs from the BABs.

Ten of one and thirty of the other. Put them where you can get the one you
want without moving the other twenty-nine, because you will be doing that
forty times.

TIME

About an hour each for the first few. Twenty minutes each once you have the
rhythm.

Forty panels is somewhere between fifteen and twenty-five hours of bench work,
and it is the most pleasant part of the whole build: repetitive, at a
comfortable height, indoors if you want, and entirely checkable.
