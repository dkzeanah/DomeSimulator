# Raising sections

Raising is getting finished panels from the stack into the air and holding
them there until they are fastened.

WHAT IT TAKES

For a nineteen-foot dome: two people and a central prop.

A BAB panel out of six-inch split timber is not light but it is not a
two-person lift either -- one person can carry one. Two people are needed
because one holds while the other fastens.

THE CENTRAL PROP

A post at the centre with an adjustable platform on it.

Each course rests on that platform while it is being fastened. When the course
closes, it is self-supporting and the platform goes up for the next one.

Simple, cheap, and it turns "hold this panel in exactly the right place while
I drive four screws" into "put this panel down on the platform".

ORDER WITHIN A COURSE

Go round the ring in one direction, setting panels loosely.

Do not fasten permanently until the course closes. Same rule as the first
course, for the same reason: an accumulated error should be distributed round
the ring rather than dumped into the last joint.

WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not raise a partly-built course and leave it overnight.

Wind gets into an open dome and an unclosed course is the least stable thing
on the site. Either close the course or brace it properly before you stop.

THE DAY IT TAKES

The raising, for a dome this size, is a day or two with two people -- provided
every panel is already made.

That ratio is the point of the method. Weeks of comfortable bench work, then a
couple of days of assembly. Most of the building happens at waist height.
