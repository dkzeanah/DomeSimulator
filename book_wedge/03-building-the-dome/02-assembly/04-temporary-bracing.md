# Temporary bracing

Temporary bracing holds the building in the shape it will hold itself in once
it is finished.

Everything here comes out at the end, and every piece of it should be planned
to come out.

WHAT NEEDS BRACING

Each course until it closes.

The whole frame until the apex goes in. A dome without its top ring is an
incomplete arch and it pushes outward at the base.

Any opening. If you are leaving a door or window out of the frame, the panels
either side of the gap have lost a neighbour and need holding.

THE CENTRAL POST

Does most of the work. It carries the courses while they are open and it gives
you something to measure from.

Keep it plumb and keep it in the centre. It is also your reference for whether
the dome is going up straight.

RADIAL BRACES

Timber from the central post out to individual vertices, where a vertex needs
holding at a specific radius.

Use these sparingly. A brace that forces a vertex into position is hiding an
error rather than fixing it -- if a vertex does not want to be where the
geometry says, find out why.

MARK THEM

Use a different colour screw for every temporary fastener.

At the end of the build you have to remove every temporary and leave every
permanent one, and there will be several hundred fasteners in the frame. If
they all look the same you will either leave temporaries in or take permanents
out.

A box of bright screws costs a few pounds and removes an entire category of
end-of-build confusion.

WHEN IT COMES OUT

Top down, after the apex closes and not before.

The dome becomes self-supporting when the last panel goes in. Taking bracing
out from the bottom while the top is open removes the props from under the
part that is still pushing.
