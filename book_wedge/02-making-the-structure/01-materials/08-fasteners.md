# Fasteners

The frame has 120 joints and every one of them needs fasteners that will still
be doing their job in twenty years.

WHAT THE FASTENERS ACTUALLY DO

They do not hold the dome up. The wood does that, in compression, through the
butt faces.

The fasteners hold the joint closed so the wood can work. That is a smaller
job than it sounds and it changes what you need.

CORROSION

This is the one that decides longevity.

Green timber is wet and many species are acidic. Plain steel in wet acidic
wood corrodes, and corroding steel stains the wood black around it and loses
section.

Use stainless, or a properly coated exterior screw. Not zinc-plated indoor
screws, which will be rust within a couple of damp seasons.

Stainless is expensive at 120 joints and it is the right expense. Work out what
it costs to replace every fastener in a standing dome and the premium stops
looking large.

QUANTITY

Two or three per joint, 120 joints, so somewhere between 240 and 360
structural fasteners for the frame.

Plus pilot holes for every one of them.

THE INSERT OPTION

A threaded insert set into the receiving member, with a bolt threading into
it, gives a joint you can undo and remake indefinitely because the thread
lives in metal rather than wood.

Two per joint is 240 inserts. It is more money and more work at build time and
it is the difference between a dome you can take apart once and a dome you can
take apart repeatedly.

BUY FOR THE DOME AFTER NEXT

If there is any chance you will build a bigger one, buy the bolts long now.

A bolt cut about a third longer than this dome needs still reaches through the
thicker members of the next two sizes up. The extra length costs pennies and
saves re-buying every fastener in the building.
