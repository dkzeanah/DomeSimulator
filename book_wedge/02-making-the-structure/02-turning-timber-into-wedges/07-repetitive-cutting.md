# Repetitive cutting

The forty-first identical cut is where accuracy goes to die.

Not because the saw changed. Because you did.

WHAT ACTUALLY DEGRADES

Attention first. By the thirtieth piece you are no longer checking that the
stick is against the fence; you are assuming it is.

Then technique. You get faster, and faster means less pressure on the fence,
and less pressure means the piece can lift.

Then the setup, slowly, from vibration and sawdust and the clamp relaxing.

None of those announce themselves.

THE COUNTERMEASURES

Work in batches of ten, not sixty. After each ten, check one against the
master, blow out the stop, and take thirty seconds.

That is not a break for you. It is a checkpoint for the work, and it caps how
many pieces a drifting setup can ruin at ten.

Count out loud or make a mark. You will lose count otherwise, and a batch of
nine or eleven is how the A and B counts end up uneven.

Stop when you are tired. The last hour of a long session produces most of the
errors, and a piece cut wrong at eight in the evening costs more than the hour
you would have saved.

THE THING THAT HELPS MOST

Do not think of it as 120 cuts.

Think of it as twelve batches of ten, each with a check at the end. Twelve is
a number you can hold. 120 is a number you stop counting.

That is a psychological trick and it works, which is the only test that
matters.
