# Layout

Layout is deciding, before any saw runs, which part of the tree becomes which
part of the building.

Do it badly and you will run out of good wood halfway through and start using
the parts you set aside for a reason.

START AT THE BUTT

The bottom of the trunk is the best wood in the tree. Straightest grain,
fewest knots, largest diameter.

Your longest members should come from there. On the reference build that is
the sixty A members at six feet each.

The top of the trunk is smaller, knottier, and tapers faster. That is where
the short members and the offcuts come from.

This sounds obvious. It is routinely got wrong, because people buck the log
into equal lengths first and then decide what each piece is for -- by which
point the decision has already been made for them.

Decide first. Then cut.

HOW MUCH TREE

The reference build needs about one and a half trees of usable stock for 120
members: 664 feet of member at a six-inch depth and four and a half inch
average width.

"One and a half trees" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. It assumes a
trunk you can get six-foot clear lengths out of, straight enough to split
predictably, and about twelve inches across.

A twelve-inch trunk split into eight gives sectors that are the right size for
this dome. A smaller trunk gives you smaller sectors and a thinner wall. A
bigger one gives you sectors you will have to trim, which is wasted work and
wasted wood.

MARK BEFORE YOU BUCK

Chalk the whole log while it is still one piece.

Mark where each member will come from, mark the direction of the taper, and
mark anything you are avoiding -- a knot cluster, a bend, a shake.

Once the log is in pieces you have lost the information about which end was
up, and which end was up matters for the taper.

THE ONE THAT CATCHES EVERYONE

A tree is not a cylinder. It is a cone.

A six-foot length taken from a twelve-inch butt is twelve inches at one end
and perhaps eleven at the other. Split that into eight and every sector is
wider at one end than the other.

You cannot avoid that. You can only decide which end of the member the fat end
goes, and be consistent about it across all 120 pieces. Pick a rule now --
fat end toward the vertex, say -- and write it on the wall.
