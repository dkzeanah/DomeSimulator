# External brackets

An external bracket is fastened on the outside of the joint, visible and
reachable.

THE ARGUMENT FOR THEM

You can see them and you can get at them.

Every fastener that carries load should be one you can inspect, tighten and
replace without dismantling anything. An external bracket is the most honest
version of that.

On a building you intend to take apart and move -- which this one is -- that
matters more than on a permanent house. A dome that comes down in a weekend
needs every structural fastener reachable with a driver.

THE ARGUMENT AGAINST

They are on the outside, which means they are in the weather and they are in
the way.

Weather: any bracket outside the envelope needs to be corrosion-rated for the
climate, and the fastener holes through the timber are water paths unless they
are sealed or shielded.

In the way: the outside of the frame is where the skin goes. A bracket
standing proud of the member is a lump the panel has to accommodate, and forty
panels each accommodating three lumps is a lot of fiddling.

THE COMPROMISE THE REFERENCE BUILD MAKES

Fasteners on the inside face, where they are reachable from within the room
and out of the weather entirely.

The inside of a dome is accessible everywhere -- there is no roof space and no
wall cavity. So the inside face is both the sheltered position and the
convenient one, which is unusual and worth taking advantage of.

The outside stays clean for the skin to land on.
