# Replaceable connections

Design every joint on the assumption that somebody will undo it.

This is the section that separates a dome you build from a dome you can live
with for twenty years.

WHY ANYTHING GETS UNDONE

Wood moves. Green timber especially -- it dries, it shrinks across the grain,
and joints that were tight in month one are loose in month eighteen. That is
not a failure; it is what wood does, and it means every joint gets tightened
at least once.

Things get damaged. A member splits, a panel gets hit, water finds a way in.
Replacing one member should not require dismantling a quarter of the building.

Buildings change. A door moves, a window is added, a partition goes in.

WHAT MAKES A CONNECTION REPLACEABLE

Three things, and they are all decisions you make before assembly.

Access. The fastener can be reached with a driver, with the building standing
and the skin on. If it cannot, it is not replaceable no matter what it is made
of.

A thread that does not live in the wood. A screw straight into timber can be
removed and refitted a few times before the hole is chewed. A threaded insert
can be done indefinitely, because the thread is in metal. That is the whole
argument for inserts and it is worth the extra cost per joint.

Standard parts. If the fastener is something you can buy in ten years, the
joint is replaceable. If it is a custom bracket from a supplier who may not
exist, it is not.

THE ORDER OF DISASSEMBLY

A dome comes apart in the reverse of the order it went together, and it is
worth knowing that before you need it.

Top ring first, then down. If you take out a middle ring first, the rings
above it have nothing to stand on.

That is also why the fasteners go on the inside face: at every stage of taking
the dome apart, the joints you need to reach are reachable from inside the
shrinking building.

THE ONE-LINE TEST

Ask of every joint: if this member split tomorrow, what would it take to
replace it?

If the answer is more than an hour and a driver, change the joint now while it
is still a drawing.
