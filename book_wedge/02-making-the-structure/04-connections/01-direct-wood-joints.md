# Direct wood joints

The pinwheel joint is wood against wood. No plate, no hub, no bracket.

Three members meet at each corner of a triangle. Instead of all three running
to a single point, each one butts into the side of the next, and they chase
each other round the corner. That is the pinwheel.

WHAT IT BUYS YOU

No end of any member is mitred. Every cut is a plain angle across a stick.

That is the entire reason this method exists, and it is worth being clear
about what it replaces: in a hub dome, the angles live in the hub, and the hub
is either an expensive casting or a piece of steel somebody has to fabricate
accurately. In a hubless dome without the pinwheel, the angles live in
compound mitres cut on the ends of every stick, which is a cut most people
cannot make repeatably.

The pinwheel moves the angle out of the joint and into the *arrangement*. The
sticks are simple; the pattern is clever.

WHAT IT COSTS

Eighty percent more timber.

A dome that shares one strut between two triangles uses 65 members and 369
feet of stock. The pinwheel gives every triangle its own three sticks: 120
members, 664 feet.

That is not a small tax and this book is not going to pretend it is. You are
buying simple cuts with wood.

Whether that is a good trade depends entirely on where your wood comes from.
If you are buying dimensional lumber by the foot, it is an expensive trade. If
you are splitting your own trees, it is close to free, and the chapter on
harvest explains why.

WHERE THE LOAD GOES

Each member is in compression along its length, and it delivers that
compression into the side of its neighbour through a flat face.

Wood is strong in compression across the grain at these loads, and the contact
area is large -- the full width of the member. This is a much better loading
condition than a fastener in shear, which is what most bracket systems rely
on.

The fasteners in a pinwheel joint are not really carrying the building. They
are holding the members against each other so that the wood can do the work.
That distinction matters when you choose them.
