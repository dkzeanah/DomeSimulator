# Temporary assembly joints

A dome does not stand up until it is nearly finished.

Partway through assembly you have a shape that is not yet closed, not yet
stable, and entirely dependent on whatever is propping it. Temporary joints
are how you survive that.

WHAT NEEDS PROPPING

The base ring, until it closes. Ten panels standing on edge in a circle want
to fall inward, and until the tenth one goes in there is nothing stopping
them.

Each subsequent ring, until it closes. Same reason.

The whole dome, until the top ring goes in. A dome without its apex is an arch
with a hole in it, and arches push outward at the base.

THE SIMPLEST RIG

A post in the middle with a temporary platform on it, at the height of each
ring as you build it.

You build the base ring around the post, resting on nothing. You build the
next ring resting on the platform. You raise the platform. Repeat.

That is how domes have been put up by one person for sixty years and there is
no reason to improve on it.

TEMPORARY FASTENERS

Use a screw that comes out easily and a different colour of screw from the
permanent ones.

Different colour matters more than it sounds. At the end of the build you need
to remove every temporary fastener and leave every permanent one, and there
will be several hundred fasteners in the building. If they all look the same
you will either leave temporaries in or take permanents out, and both are bad
in different ways.

THE ORDER TO REMOVE THEM

From the top down, once the dome is closed.

The dome becomes self-supporting when the last panel goes in, not before.
Removing bracing from the bottom while the top is still open takes the props
out from under the thing that is still pushing outward.
