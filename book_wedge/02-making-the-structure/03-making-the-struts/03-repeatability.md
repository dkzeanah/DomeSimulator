# Repeatability

You are making 120 members. The question is not whether you can make one
correctly. It is whether the hundred and twentieth is the same as the first.

WHERE DRIFT COMES FROM

Three places, in order of how much damage they do.

A fence that moves. If the stop that sets your length shifts by a
thirty-second over forty cuts, the last stick is an eighth short and nothing
tells you.

A blade that wanders. A saw with play in the pivot cuts a slightly different
angle depending on how hard you push it.

You. Cut fifty of anything and your technique changes -- you get faster, you
push differently, you stop checking.

WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT

Do not measure from the last piece. Ever.

If you use the stick you just cut to set the length of the next one, every
error is inherited and added to. Forty sticks later you have accumulated forty
small mistakes into one large one. Always measure from the fence, or from a
master.

Keep a master. Cut one member, check it properly, and hang it on the wall. It
is not for building with. It is the thing you compare against every tenth
piece, and it is how you catch a fence that has crept without noticing when it
started.

Check at intervals, not at the end. Every tenth member, put it against the
master. If it matches, you have lost nothing. If it does not, you have nine
bad sticks to redo rather than fifty-nine.

THE HONEST BIT

You will still get some wrong.

The point of all of this is not to make a hundred and twenty perfect members.
It is to make sure that when one is wrong you find out while it is still one.
