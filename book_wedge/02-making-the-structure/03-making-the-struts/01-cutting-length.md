# Cutting length

The chord is not the cut.

This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in dome building and it
catches people who have done everything else right.

The chord is the distance between two vertex points. Those points are
mathematical -- they float in space at the corners of the triangles, and on
the reference build they are 72.000 inches apart for an A member.

The stick you cut is shorter than that, because both of its ends get taken
back to make room for the members it meets.

THE NUMBERS ON THE REFERENCE BUILD

A members: 72.000 inch chord, and the usable stock comes off the saw between
68.977 and 70.182 inches.

B members: 63.670 inch chord, stock between 60.492 and 62.681 inches.

Look at the range. It is not one number. A members are not all the same
length, even though they are all the same chord.

WHY THE RANGE EXISTS

Because the amount taken off an end depends on what that end runs into.

In the pinwheel joint every member butts into the side of its neighbour rather
than meeting it point to point. How far it has to be set back depends on how
thick the neighbour is at that spot and what angle it arrives at. Those are
not the same at every corner of the dome.

So an A member near the apex is trimmed slightly differently from an A member
in the middle band, and the difference is up to an inch and a fifth.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR CUT LIST

You cannot cut sixty identical A members and sixty identical B members and
expect them to go together.

You need the actual per-member list, and the tool generates it. Run the
fabrication package and you get every member with its own length, its own
identifier, and the panel it belongs to.

THE ONE THING TO DO ANYWAY

Cut long.

Every member is easier to shorten than to lengthen, and the first panel you
build is the one that tells you whether your numbers are right. Cut the first
three members half an inch over, fit them, measure what you actually needed,
and then commit the other 117.

Half an inch of sacrificial length on three sticks is the cheapest insurance
in the whole build.
