# 24. Every Other Way of Doing It

This book recommends one way of doing each thing. You are owed the others.

Not a paragraph saying alternatives exist — the alternatives, named, with what
each one costs and weighs **on this building**, and a mark against the one the
reference build uses.

## How to read these tables

A list of materials with prices per square metre is not a decision. A decision
is *"on a {{dome.diameter_ft}}-foot dome with {{dome.panel_sqft}} square feet
of shell, cedar shakes are this much and this heavy, and asphalt is that"* —
and that is arithmetic this project can do, because it costs domes for a
living.

Every option comes from a tool that already models it. Nothing is invented for
the book. The full tables are in the reference section at the back; these
pages are the shape of the choice.

## The frame

![The same frame, priced and weighed eight ways.](plate-eight-materials.png)

Eight materials, the same {{dome.members}} members. The reference build is the
whole tree trunk, and it is the cheapest by a long way — which is the entire
economic argument of this method in one row.

![Round tube to quarter wedge, all at the same width.](plate-eight-sections.png)

Eight cross sections at the same nominal width. Read the wedge row carefully:
the Creator's quarter wedge and this book's member are both split log, sized
differently — one is a quarter of a log scaled to the strut's width, the other
an eighth of a {{cut.trunk}}-inch one. Same family, different stick.

## The envelope

![Everything the tool can put between the struts.](plate-sixteen-panels.png)

Sixteen panel types, from canvas to solar, over {{dome.panel_sqft}} square
feet. The span from cheapest to dearest is more than a hundred to one, which
is why this is the decision that actually sets what your dome costs.

![What goes over the sheathing.](plate-nine-claddings.png)

Nine claddings. A green roof is twenty-four times the weight of house wrap,
and the frame has to carry whichever one you pick.

## What is deliberately missing

There is no straw-bale row and no earthbag row.

Not because they are bad — because this project cannot cost one, and a table
with a plausible number in it that came from nowhere is worse than a table
with a gap.

The same applies to anything else you find missing. If it is not here, it is
because nothing in this repository models it, and the honest thing is to say
so rather than to estimate.

## The four you cannot change later

Everything above can be decided after the dome is standing. Four things
cannot, and they are worth a page of their own in the back:

* which way the wedge's point faces
* how many sectors the log splits into
* how big the tree was
* which way the triangle pinwheels

Each of those changes the frame itself, and each has a figure in this book
showing what it does to the seam. Chapter {{ch.member}} is where they are
argued; the tables at the back are where they are priced.
