# 29. Hang It Between Two Trees

This is the most speculative corner of the method, and it exists because the
floor of Chapter {{ch.floor}} clamps to a mast rather than being framed into
the base ring.

Once the floor hangs off something in the middle, the ground stops being
structural. And once the ground stops being structural, you can take it away.

![The same mast and floor, hung on three cables to saddles on two trees.](floating-dome.png)

## What changes

Everything the earlier chapters assumed about the ground quietly reverses.

The base ring stops being what holds the building up; the mast is. The floor
stops being something the dome stands on and becomes something the dome
**carries** — clamped to the mast, hanging under the shell, level because the
mast is level.

The utility column keeps its job. Services come up a flexible drop instead of
a rigid rise, which is a fitting rather than a redesign.

## The parts

A steel core through the utility column, timber-clad, floor to apex, with a
lifting ring under the seal cap. Three galvanised cables from that ring to
three saddles on two trees. A brake winch and a block.

The rig is ${{money.float_rig}}. With the mast and the dome's own floor the
whole upgrade is ${{money.float_total}}, against a frame weighing
{{money.frame_weight}} pounds in green pine.

**Saddles, not holes.** Nothing is drilled into the trees. A saddle spreads
the load around the trunk and comes off without leaving a wound, which matters
because a tree with a bolt in it is a tree with a problem in ten years.

## Why you would

Flood, mostly. Getting a floor above water is the whole reason in some places
and no amount of foundation solves it.

After that: slope without earthworks, airflow underneath that keeps timber
dry, and the space below, which becomes storage or shade.

## The sentence that has to go with it

**That is a design possibility, not an engineered structure.**

The loads on the trees, the cables, the saddles and the mast need an engineer
before anybody stands under it. The model prices the parts; it does not rate
them, and the difference between those two things is the whole of somebody's
safety.

Trees are also not static. They move in wind, they grow, they shed limbs, and
two of them move independently of each other. A rig that is correct on a still
day in June is a rig that has not yet been tested.

Nothing in this chapter has been built. It is here because the floor design
makes it possible and because a book that only prints the parts of a method
that are finished is not documenting a method, it is advertising one.
