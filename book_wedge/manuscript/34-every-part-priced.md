# 34. Every Part, Priced

Six bills. Every line carries the constant it came from, so any of them can
be argued with by editing one number rather than by doubting the total.

## What is separate from what, and why

**The dome** is ${{money.price}}, and it is priced in Chapter {{ch.core}}
and the chapters around it. Nothing on this page changes it.

**The pad** is the host's bill, not the buyer's — Chapter {{ch.pad}}. It is
here because somebody has to build it and because a book that prices only
half of an arrangement is not pricing the arrangement.

**Everything else on this page is an upgrade.** A dome works without the seam
module, without the solar bench and without the water bench; it sits on a
pad's pedestal and drinks from the pad's stub. These are what you fit when you
want it to work with nothing plugged into it.

Added up, the systems on this page come to ${{sys.total}}.

## The utility column, complete

Including the steel, which is the part most bills leave out.

    {{sys.column_table}}

Two lines there are doing more than they look.

**The steel core** runs the full height, floor port to apex ring, and it is
what the floor clamps to and what the lifting rig hangs from — Chapter
{{ch.floating}}. It is a pipe. It is also the single component that makes a
floating dome possible, and it costs ${{money.mast}} in a ${{money.price}}
building.

**The radial floor spokes** are the {{dome.base_sides}} steel arms from the
hub out to the base ring. They are what make the floor a component rather
than framing, which is Chapter {{ch.hotswap}}'s third feature.

## The power bench

    {{sys.power_table}}

A {{cut.splits}}-way split log frame does not need electricity, but the
condensing plates and the barrier fan of Chapter {{ch.seam_module}} do, and so
does everybody who lives anywhere.

The array is sized at 800 W and the bank at 10 kWh, which is the owner's
stated arrangement rather than a calculation from a load list. The metering
is in the bill deliberately: a system you cannot read is a system you cannot
size next time.

## The water bench

    {{sys.water_table}}

This is what turns {{dome.seam_ft}} feet of seam gutter into something you
can drink.

The first-flush diverter is the cheapest item and the one that matters most.
It throws away the first gallons off a dry roof — the ones carrying whatever
has landed on the cap since the last rain — and it is the difference between
roof water and drinking water.

The UV lamp is the other one. A filter makes water clear; a lamp makes it
safe. Neither does the other's job.

## Joining hardware, stainless

    {{sys.hardware_table}}

Edge to edge, into threaded inserts rather than into end grain — Chapter
{{ch.hotswap}}.

Stainless throughout is not a luxury here. Chapter {{ch.floor}} notes that wet
timber and plain steel together are a battery; on a frame whose whole argument
is that it lasts decades and gets taken apart, a corroded fastener is the one
failure that turns a demountable building into a permanent one.

### The V bracket

The flat V bracket is the connector this method settled on: two flaps meeting
at the panel's angle, **{{brk.holes}} holes in each flap**, folded from
stainless on a bench brake.

The flaps run out to as much as **half the member's length** —
{{brk.flap}} inches on a long member — which puts about {{brk.spacing}}
inches between holes. That spacing is the point of the thing. Screws
clustered at the end of a stick are all working the same short grain; spread
along it, each one is into fresh wood, and the joint stops depending on any
single fixing.

At ${{brk.each}} each, {{dome.members}} of them is not the expensive part of
anything.

## The pad's own materials

    {{sys.pad_table}}

The host's bill, counted board by board rather than rated per square foot —
which is why it can be checked against a merchant's quote.

## What this page does not include

Labour, which is Chapter {{ch.core}}'s territory and is
{{money.labour_hours}} hours on the standard article.

Tools. A chainsaw, a froe, a circular saw, a drill and a bench brake, none of
which are consumed by one dome.

And freight, permits, and the ground itself, all of which are local and none
of which this book can know.
