# 32. What Each World Shows You

One picture from every three-dimensional environment that ships with this
book, what it is for, and what to do once you are in it.

## The raw-wedge solver

![The reference build, opened eight inches.](standard-dome-exploded.png)

**What it is.** The dome this whole book is about, solved from one number:
the long member. {{dome.members}} members, {{dome.panels}} panels,
{{dome.seams}} seams, every angle derived.

**What to do in it.** Press the explode key. The panels slide apart along
their own face normals — nothing in the geometry moves, it is the same solve
drawn with a gap — and for the first time you can see into a seam and watch
the key sitting in it.

Then turn one decision at a time. Change the wedge orientation and watch the
key's size change. Change the split count from {{cut.splits}} to twelve and
watch the seam channel shrink to something that will not take a hose.

**What it is not.** It has no doorway, no foundation and no utility column,
and it builds 2V hemispheres only. Those live in the other worlds.

## The fabrication jig

![A panel closed on the jig.](the-jig.png)

**What it is.** The fixture of Chapter {{ch.jig}}, in twelve steps, each one
saying what it *enforces* rather than just what it is.

**What to do in it.** Step through all twelve before you cut a board. The
corner stations are the ones worth flying to — the corner is where this
method either makes sense or does not, and seeing the butt land on a radial
face rather than on an end is the moment it clicks.

Then set it for the other panel family and step through again. The fixture
does not change. That is the claim, and you can check it.

## The Dome Creator

![Twelve designs, drawn by the tool that builds them.](plate-twelve-domes.png)

**What it is.** Every dome this project can describe that is *not* this one.
Frequencies 1V to 4V, eight frame materials, eight strut sections, sixteen
panel types, nine claddings, seven foundations — and a bill of materials for
whatever you just assembled.

**What to do in it.** Build the dome you were going to build before you read
this book, price it, then build this one and compare. That comparison is more
persuasive than any argument in these pages, and it is yours rather than mine.

## The seed world

![Power and water up the middle and out through the apex.](plate-core-socket.png)

**What it is.** The product version: the utility column, the gasketed cap at
the apex, the shower cap, the quilted layers, the mast and the floor.

**What to do in it.** Look at the column. It is the one object that touches
the ground, the wall and the sky, and almost every decision in Part 5 and
Part 7 of this book is downstream of it existing.

Then add layers to the shell and watch the cap grow. Chapter {{ch.hats}} is
arithmetic on a page; here it is a thing changing size.

## The pad and the park

![The platform, built one course at a time.](plate-the-pad.png)

**What it is.** The ground of Chapter {{ch.pad}}: a pad built course by
course, and a network of them with the shared services between.

**What to do in it.** Watch the colours. Amber is what the host owns, cyan is
what the tenant owns, and the clean split of Chapter {{ch.network}} is
something you can see rather than something you have to be told.

## The cost console

![The geometry and the price in one program.](tool-cost-console.png)

**What it is.** The cost model, open over the solved dome. Every line carries
its own source constant, and changing an assumption moves every figure
downstream of it immediately.

**What to do in it.** Find a price you disagree with — there will be one —
and change it. The whole quote re-derives. That is the right way to argue with
this book: not by doubting the total but by editing the input you think is
wrong and seeing what it was worth.

**The number to look at first.** ${{money.price}} for the standard article,
built to ${{money.cost}}, at ${{money.per_sqft}} a square foot of floor. The
maker's markup is stated as a markup on cost rather than a margin on price,
because those are different numbers and the one a reader can check with a
calculator is the first.

## Where to start

If you have an afternoon: the solver, exploded, then the jig's twelve steps.

If you have a tree: the cost console, with your own trunk diameter typed into
it.

If you have ground: the park.
