# 26. Twelve Buildings, One Tool

The previous chapter priced the frame and the envelope. This one is
everything else the Dome Creator will put on a shell — and the point of it is
that all twelve of these are the same program, so the comparison between them
is real rather than rhetorical.

![Every design in the catalogue, priced.](plate-economics.png)

## What the catalogue contains

Twelve finished buildings, from a timber workshop to a concrete monocoque, a
glass studio loft, a rebar garden dome, a treehouse canopy and a steel arc
hangar.

They are not illustrations of an idea. Each one is a configuration of the
same four dials — frequency, frame material, strut section, panel type — plus
cladding, foundation and finish, and each one comes with its own bill of
materials.

Set the dials differently and you get a thirteenth.

![Frequency, and where the dome gets cut.](plate-frequency.png) The catalogue is a set of
saved positions, not a product range.

![What frequency actually costs.](plate-frequency-cost.png)

## The decisions nobody prices

Three of them, and they change how a dome *lives* rather than how it stands.

![Sixteen finishes.](plate-colours.png)

**Finish.** Sixteen of them over the same shell. Cheap to change, and the
only decision in this entire book that is purely aesthetic — which is worth
saying, because almost every other choice here has a number attached and this
one honestly does not.

![Four ways to divide a round floor.](plate-floor-divisions.png)

**Floor division.** This is the question everybody asks second, right after
"does it leak", and it is the one round buildings are genuinely worse at.

A round floor does not take rectangular rooms. The four divisions the tool
offers — radial, a central core, a chord wall, and open — are four answers to
that, and the honest summary is that a dome wants **one big room and a
periphery**, and fighting that produces awkward rooms with curved outside
walls.

The periphery is also where headroom runs out. Chapter {{ch.size}}'s
{{dome.floor_sqft}} square feet of floor is not {{dome.floor_sqft}} square
feet of usable room, and a knee wall is the cheapest fix there is.

![The part nobody films.](plate-fitout.png)

**Fit-out.** Benches, storage, services, the things that make it a workshop
rather than a shell. It is most of the cost of a finished building and none
of the glamour, which is why it is in the tool and not in the photographs.

## How to use this chapter

Do not read it. Open the tool.

The tables at the back have every figure in them, but a table of sixteen panel
types is a worse instrument than sixteen domes side by side with the prices on
them. Chapter {{ch.worlds}} says how to start it, and the one thing worth
doing first is building the dome you were going to build before you read this
book, pricing it, and then building this one.

That comparison is yours rather than mine, and it is the only one that will
change your mind.
