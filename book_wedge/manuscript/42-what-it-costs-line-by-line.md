Quantities are measured off the dome's solved geometry and cannot be argued with.
Prices and rates are typed in by a person, each with a unit and a reason, and they
are assumptions, not market research:

    {{econ.seed_declared}}

At the other end of the scale: the {{skin.nest_d}}-inch dome from one sheet of

## The factory's view

<!-- concept: world_chatgpt/ms_line -->
<!-- concept: world_chatgpt/cg_costs -->
<!-- concept: master/ms_math_labour -->

The software that wrote this book also prices domes the way a factory would: a
fifteen-station line in trailer-plant order, every element with a material cost and
an install time. Its consumer price separates three things that a sticker merges --
the bill of materials, the labour at a burdened wage with its overhead, and the
margin a comparable product carries. A cheaper shell does not erase labour, and a
fast build does not make glass cheap. And what that line costs the people on it is
Chapter {{ch.body}}, in one sentence:

    {{econ.master_labour}}
