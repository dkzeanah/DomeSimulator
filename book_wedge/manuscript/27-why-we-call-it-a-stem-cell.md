# 27. Why We Call It a Stem Cell

A stem cell is a cell that has not decided what to be yet.

It has the full machinery — a nucleus, a membrane, everything a working cell
needs — and no specialisation. Put it in one place and it becomes liver. Put
it somewhere else and it becomes bone. The body is the same body; the
environment does the differentiating.

That is the product line in this book, and the word is not decoration.

![One body, many things it can become.](plate-stem-cell.png)

## One body, undifferentiated

The standard article is a dome: {{dome.members}} members, {{dome.panels}}
panels, a shell, a floor, and a utility column running from the floor port to
the apex. It lists at ${{money.price}}.

It is not a house. It is not a workshop. It is not a studio, a gym, a sauna or
a guest room. It is the **body** of all of those, and what it becomes depends
on what you put in it and what you plug into the column.

There are {{seed.count}} of them in the catalogue — Chapter {{ch.seeds}} — and
every one is this same body. The frame line of the quote does not change
between them. Not by a dollar.

## The socket at the top

![The roof comes apart too.](plate-slices.png)

What makes differentiation possible is that there is exactly **one** place
where things connect.

The utility column is the only object in the building that touches the ground,
the wall and the sky. It carries {{core.services}} services in
{{core.parts}} documented parts, and it makes exactly one penetration in the
weather surface — the apex sleeve, with a gasketed seal cap over it that is
meant to stay shut for years and come off in ten minutes.

Everything a fit-out needs comes off that column. Power at a sub-panel inside
it. Water at a four-port manifold. Drain down a stack inside the same chase.
Air, eventually, through the seam channel of Chapter {{ch.pinwheel}}.

**Nothing is plumbed into a wall.** The wall is a shell that resolves loads
and keeps weather out, and it has no services in it to disturb. That is what
makes a fit-out a thing you do rather than a thing you commission.

## And a skin that snaps on

The outside works the same way. Services that want to be outside the footprint
— a water heater, a cooling unit, a shower — hang off the shell on threaded
inserts rather than cutting a new hole. The design calls them polyps and the
name is the same joke as the stem cell: an organism growing an appendage
rather than a builder cutting an opening.

Every one of them is reversible. Take it off and there is a panel with four
inserts in it.

![Everything else snaps onto the outside.](plate-polyps.png)

## Why this is a different kind of product

A normal building is specialised on the day it is designed. A workshop has a
workshop's floor, a workshop's power, a workshop's door. Turning it into a
guest room is a renovation.

This one is specialised on the day it is **fitted**, and un-specialised by
taking the fit-out out again. The most expensive parts — the frame, the
shell, the column — never learn what the building is for.

That is the argument, and the next chapter is the economic consequence of it,
which is the part that actually decides whether any of this is worth doing.
