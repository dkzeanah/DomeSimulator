# 28. Fifteen Buildings From One Body

The catalogue a homestead wants second, and third, and fifth.

![The catalogue, priced.](plate-seeds-priced.png)

## The list

Every one of these is the same frame, the same shell and the same utility
core. What differs is the fit-out — and the fit-out is the only line in the
quote that moves.

    {{seed.table}}

{{seed.count}} structures. The bare body is the {{seed.cheapest}} at
${{seed.cheapest_usd}}; the most specialised is the {{seed.dearest}} at
${{seed.dearest_usd}}.

That spread — about two to one across the whole range — is the differentiation
cost. Everything below it is the body, and the body is the same body.

## What the spread tells you

**Water is what makes a building expensive.** Sort that list and the ones with
plumbing in them are at the top. A sauna, a jacuzzi and a food seed are all
wet buildings, and the ones that are merely rooms are cheap.

**Power is not.** The core already carries a sub-panel and a spare breaker, so
a building that wants more sockets wants almost nothing.

**Structure is never the variable.** No building on that list changes the
frame. Chapter {{ch.core}}'s argument, made visible: the expensive parts never
learn what the building is for.

## The ladder

![How far down the ladder goes.](plate-ladder.png)

Every one of these can be bought in stages, and the ladder matters more than
the list does.

The rungs are, roughly: the bare body; the body with a floor; with a cap and
one quilted layer; with the fit-out; with the polyps. You can stop at any rung
and live there, and each one is a real building rather than a half-built one.

What makes that possible is Chapter {{ch.hotswap}} — the four features that
keep the skin and the floor swappable. Without them the ladder is just a
half-finished building with a tarpaulin on it.

## One building, fifteen stations

![One building, fifteen stations.](plate-line.png)

There is a manufacturing view of this, and it is worth a page because it is
where the argument stops being about one person with a chainsaw.

The nine processes of Chapter {{ch.nine}} do not change between these
{{seed.count}} structures. Neither does the jig, the cut list, or the core.
What changes is which station a particular dome stops at on its way through.

That is a **production line**, and the thing that makes it one is not
machinery — it is that the parts list and the verb list are both short and
neither grows with the catalogue.

A workshop that builds one dome a month is building the same nine operations
whether this month's is a sauna or a garage. That is the difference between a
product line and a range of products.

## What this catalogue is not

It is not a range anybody has built. The {{seed.cheapest}} is the one this
project has solved end to end; the rest are configurations of the same model
with their fit-outs costed, and they are **priced rather than proven**.

The difference matters. A food seed's price is real arithmetic over real line
items. Whether a food seed is a good building — whether the ventilation is
right, whether the floor drains where it should — is not something a cost
model can tell you, and this book will not pretend otherwise.

Treat the list as what it is: evidence that the body generalises, and a set of
starting points rather than a set of finished designs.
