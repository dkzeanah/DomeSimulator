# 20. A Pad, Not a Plot

The ground is the one part of this building you cannot take with you.

Everything else — {{dome.members}} members, forty panels, a column, a cap, a
floor — comes apart and moves. The ground does not, and it is usually the most
expensive thing in the whole arrangement.

So stop buying it.

![The landowner builds the pad. It stays when the dome leaves.](plate-pad-is-not-yours.png)

## The split

**The host owns the ground and everything permanently attached to it.** The
pad, the service port coming up through it, the water and power to that port,
the access track, the under-floor storage if there is any.

**The tenant owns the building.** The frame, the panels, the shell, the
column, the floor, the cap.

That line is not arbitrary. It falls exactly where "can this be carried away"
falls, which means it never has to be argued about. Nothing straddles it.

![Two people, and what each of them owns.](plate-two-owners.png)

## Why it is a clean line

The usual version of this arrangement — renting land and putting something on
it — is messy because the something is half-attached. Whose slab is it?
Whose septic? Who pays when the deck rots?

Here the pad is a single object with a price. In this model it is
${{money.pad}}, built once, by the host, to a specification the tenant can
check before arriving. The dome is another object with a price,
${{money.price}}, and it arrives on a trailer.

When the arrangement ends, the tenant takes a building and the host keeps a
platform. Both of those are worth something to their owner and neither is
worth arguing about.

![The line that never moves.](plate-line-never-moves.png)

## What the host is really selling

Not land. A **prepared, serviced, level place to put a building**, which is a
much more specific thing and much more valuable than an acre of field.

Preparing that place is the expensive, skilled, permitting-heavy part of
building anywhere. It involves earth, concrete, a trench, and somebody who
knows where the frost line is. Doing it once and having it used for decades
is a far better use of that effort than doing it once per owner.

## And what the tenant is buying

A foundation, without a mortgage.

The pad is cheaper than the foundation they would otherwise pour, because it
is amortised over more than one occupancy. It is available now rather than
after a land purchase. And — the part nobody expects to value until they have
it — it is *leavable*.

## The number that decides it

The pad is ${{money.pad}}. The floor a dome brings with it is a few thousand
more. Against a piece of land, which in most places is a five-figure number
before anything is built on it and a thirty-year commitment after.

That ratio is the entire argument for the next chapter, which works it
through from both sides and says what would have to be true for it to hold.
