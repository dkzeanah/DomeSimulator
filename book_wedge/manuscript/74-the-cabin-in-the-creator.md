# 74. The Cabin in the Creator

Everything up to here has been about the frame: the tree, the split, the
member, the pinwheel, the forty hours. The frame is the part that has to be
right. It is not the part you look at. Over the next five chapters the frame
stays exactly as it is, and everything that goes on it -- the skin, the
cladding, what it stands on, what goes on its floor -- changes, every way the
software in this book can change it, with a price on every version.

The software is the Dome Creator, which chapter {{ch.creator}} introduced: a
customizer whose menus multiply to {{sty.shells}} different shells. Those
chapters toured its own twelve designs, which are bigger buildings than this
one. Here it builds this one. Given the cabin's frequency and the cabin's
size, it rebuilds the same dome: {{sty.diameter}} feet across, a sphere of
{{sty.radius_m}} metres, {{sty.struts}} struts and {{sty.panels}} panels, the
count chapter after chapter of this book has been cutting.

![The cabin, bare, in the Creator.](plate-sty-bare.png)

## The same dome, in another tool

The Creator prices a building the only honest way software can: it measures
quantities off the model it just built -- the length and volume of every
strut, the area of every panel, the footprint of the foundation -- and
multiplies each by a unit price somebody typed into its catalogue. Chapter
{{ch.styprices}} prints that whole catalogue. Nothing it says is a quote. A
price is a declared input; a quantity is a measurement; the total is the one
times the other, and every row in these chapters is a fresh rebuild of the
cabin, not an adjustment to a total.

So the starting point is the cabin, bare: a frame of {{sty.base_frame_mat}}
in {{sty.base_shape}}s, {{sty.base_panel}} panels, {{sty.base_layer}} over
them and a {{sty.base_foundation}} under it, the finish of the Creator's own
split-log design, with nothing on the floor.

## The bare cabin, line by line

The Creator prices that cabin at **${{sty.bare_total}}**:

- the frame, ${{sty.bare_frame}}, weighing {{sty.frame_lb}} pounds;
- the panels, ${{sty.bare_panels}};
- the cladding, ${{sty.bare_cladding}};
- the foundation, ${{sty.bare_foundation}}.

The whole shell weighs {{sty.bare_lb}} pounds, and the Creator's construction
record puts it at {{sty.bare_hours}} hours of work. Hold onto those numbers.
Every other cabin in this Part is this one with something changed.

## Where the two tools disagree

They are the same dome, and they do not report the same figures, and it is
worth saying exactly where and why before trusting either.

**The floor.** The Creator measures {{sty.floor}} square feet: the full circle
of the dome's radius. The wedge method measures {{dome.floor_sqft}}: the floor
inside the base ring, where the members' own thickness has already taken its
share. The panels agree -- {{sty.shell}} square feet of shell in the Creator,
{{dome.panel_sqft}} in the wedge method -- because a panel is a panel in both.

**The frame.** The Creator's frame line is ${{sty.bare_frame}}. The wedge
method's own frame, from chapter {{ch.bom}}, costs ${{money.frame}}. The
Creator frames in {{sty.base_shape}}s at a fixed width and prices timber by
the kilogram; the wedge method splits eighth-log wedges out of trees it
counts, prices those trees, and does not charge for timber twice. The wedge
method's figure is the one this book stands on. The Creator's is printed
beside it so the two tools can be compared honestly, and so a reader who
changes the frame material in the next chapters is comparing like with like.

**The hours.** The Creator's {{sty.bare_hours}} hours cover the whole shell:
foundation, frame, panels, cladding. The wedge method's {{hr.total}} hours are
the frame alone, with an allowance for mistakes. Neither is wrong. They are
answers to different questions, and the next chapters use the Creator's,
because the next chapters are about everything but the frame.

From here on, the frame stays, and the rest moves.
