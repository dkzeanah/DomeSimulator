# 67. The Standard Article, and What the Money Is For

<!-- concept: seed_pitch/open -->
<!-- concept: pitch_hero/open -->
<!-- concept: pitch_hero/frame -->

This is a house you can take apart. {{dome.diameter_ft}} feet across,
{{dome.floor_sqft}} square feet of floor, and every piece of it fits in a
pickup -- a tiny house, with the part of a home that is hard to make done, and
the part that is yours to decide left to you.

The frame is already standing on your land: two trees, split into
{{dome.members}} wedges, no hubs, no jig to buy, three saw settings for the
whole building. Everything this book has said about the stick, the panel and
the skin comes together here as one thing a person could order, and this
chapter prices it -- then prices the two campaigns that would make it more
than once.

What makes the shell cheap is Chapter {{ch.hats}}'s idea: a dome is a head and
it wears hats, and only the outermost one is waterproof. Under it go quilted
layers of recycled clothing -- about {{tt_quilt.shirts_per_layer}} t-shirts a
layer, sewn at somebody's kitchen table and tagged with the name of whoever
sewed it. Every layer added makes the next cap a size bigger, so there is no
limit: a rigid shell fills up and stops, and this one does not.

![The insulation is a thousand t-shirts.](plate-thousand-shirts.png)

<!-- concept: pitch_hero/hull -->

And nothing here is finished, on purpose. A laminated hull instead of the cap
(${{money.hard_price}} for the dome with one), a steel mast through the
column, a floor bought later, a rig that hangs the whole building between two
trees (${{money.float_total}} for the three). Buy them when you want them.

## Four ways to build the same dome

<!-- concept: kick/bom -->
<!-- concept: kick/ladder -->

The first campaign priced the same reference dome four ways, every part bought
new -- nothing salvaged, nothing harvested, pressure-treated lumber off the
rack, sheathing, foam, epoxy, cloth, screws, and a basic kitchen and bathroom:

    {{kick.w_builds}}

${{kick.standard_usd}} for the middle of the range, about
${{kick.standard_per_sqft}} a square foot finished. Bare frame and a hat on
top, ${{kick.bare_usd}}; pressure-treated and glassed all over,
${{kick.full_usd}}. The cheapest of them is under the price of a used car --
and cut your own timber, and the largest line on the sheet goes to zero.

:::box against
**These are not the same number as the invoice above, and both are right.**
The campaign's ladder is materials at the till for a dome its buyer builds.
The standard article is a dome somebody else builds, with labour, overhead, a
warranty reserve and a margin on it. Quote the first as the price of a house
and you have quietly left out everybody's wages.
:::

## Running it on nothing

<!-- concept: seed_pitch/solar -->

If the envelope is that cheap to run, the off-grid arithmetic gets easy -- and
it is not sized against a guess. The draw is this dome's own heating and
cooling year out of the performance model, plus the lights and the fan it
ships with:

    {{kick.w_offgrid}}

Panels on the sunward bays or up a mast beside the dome cover most of a day,
and the bank carries it through a couple of days with no sun. One more honest
note: square cells waste the corners of a triangular bay. Triangular cells are
the obvious fix, and they are not something anybody can order yet.

![Running it on nothing.](plate-off-grid.png)

## What the money is for

<!-- concept: kick/factory -->
<!-- concept: kick/ask -->

Two campaigns, and both asks are lists rather than round numbers. The first
was for the means to build domes faster than by hand:

    {{kick.w_equipment}}

${{kick.capital_usd}}. ${{kick.equipment_usd}} of it is equipment that still
exists on day three hundred -- a used flatbed, a trailer, a used telehandler
because a dome goes up in panels and panels are heavy, a portable sawmill so
the timber line really does go to zero -- and the rest is material for the
first units, so the thing can start paying for itself. Vague asks deserve to
fail, so it was not vague.

<!-- concept: seed_pitch/goal -->

The second was for the measurements and the tooling that turn a solved
geometry into something makeable twice:

    {{kick.w_goal}}

The top of that list is a test platform and one dome on it, left up through a
full year with sensors in every layer of the wall. That turns the moisture
question -- the one this book's whole Part on the seam worries at -- from
an argument into a measurement, and it gets published whichever way it comes
out. None of it is salary and none of it is marketing.

![What the goal buys.](plate-the-goal.png)

## Three kinds of people

<!-- concept: pitch_hero/three -->
<!-- concept: pitch_hero/close -->

It only works if three different people turn up: somebody who sews, somebody
with land who does not want to be a landlord, and somebody who wants a
building. Chapter {{ch.why_me}} is about why.

And every figure in this chapter came out of a model you can run yourself --
including the ones that argue against it. Run them, and say which price is
wrong.
