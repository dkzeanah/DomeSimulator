# 50. Who This Is For

<!-- concept: byod/my_story -->

When I was nineteen I could borrow five thousand dollars against my name for
college. I blew that chance, joined the military, and came back at thirty-two
for revenge. Constructive revenge, as it turns out, involving domes.

I did not fail because I was stupid. I failed because where I lived was not
stable. I did not have the support I needed, and I could not keep my mind on
what mattered, and it cost me more than I can easily add up.

So I ask a specific question when I design this, and it is not "what would a
buyer want." It is: **what would a small home I owned have done for my
eighteen-year-old self?** A floor that was mine, on a pad that supplied what a
small house could not, that I could improve as I went and take with me when I
left. I think it would have been revolutionary. That is the person I keep in
mind.

![What this would have meant to me.](plate-my-story.png)

## Built with the little guy in mind

<!-- concept: byod/little_guy -->
<!-- concept: byod_deepseek/little_guy -->

Rich people will find a way to have what they want. They always have. I want
to make a difference for the little guy: single parents, people with very
little money, families just starting, bachelors, anyone trying to get stable.

My mother was a poor single parent. She worked so much that I went to the
before-school program *and* the after-school program. I wish she had had an
economical home that worked for her, instead of one more thing eating her
life. If there are people with the means to fund something like this, women
especially, that is who I would love to reach. Help build the kind of
stability my mother deserved.

I would like to become successful enough to give domes away. Maybe by then I
am making dome furniture too. That is an ambition, not a plan. The plan is the
first step: a home people can afford to start with and improve.

## Three things at once

<!-- concept: seed_pitch/three -->
<!-- concept: byod/network -->

This was never a plan to sell domes. It is three things that need each other.

**Quilters.** A dome's insulation is a quilted layer of recycled clothing --
about {{tt_quilt.shirts_per_layer}} t-shirts a layer, adding
R-{{tt_quilt.r_per_layer}} each, the only building insulation I know of that a
person can make at a kitchen table. So the network wants a register of people
who will sew them, paid and listed. Not a charity drive.

**Pad hosts.** People with land and no vocation as a landlord, who will keep a
deck and a service connection and not a roof, a boiler or somebody's kitchen.

**Dome owners.** People who want a building, with trees of their own or
without.

A dome with nobody to quilt for it is a cold dome. A dome with nowhere to
stand is a kit in a garage. A pad with no dome on it is a deck. And one host
with one pad is a favour; what makes it worth building is the second host,
and the third, and the four hundredth -- choice of place and price for the
owner, a pool of residents for the host that is not limited to whoever is in
town, and a resale market for the houses. A house you can sell is an asset. A
house nobody will buy is a very expensive tent. I want to build demand for the
real thing, not assume it is already there.

![Each one needs the other two.](plate-three-things.png)

I see my own job becoming pad design and getting hosts started: a repeatable

plan.

For the people who help: founder status, a printed dome keychain or a window
decal, a signed copy of this book -- and, higher up, sitting down with me to
imagine your own dome, build it digitally and make a plan for it, whether
you are a future host, a future owner or somebody collecting possibilities for
later. Those are the categories; a campaign sets the actual tiers.

## The strategy, stated plainly

<!-- concept: hype/phases -->
<!-- concept: hype/hr -->

The Frankendome film put it in three phases. Phase one: build geodesic
frames. Phase two: spread them across civilisation like a plague of
structurally efficient triangles. Phase three: become filthy rich selling
upgrades.

And to be completely transparent about the endgame: somewhere there is a man
on his seventh holiday of the year, watching the projected yield on a
portfolio of rental houses, and I would like that line to do something sudden
and downward on account of this. That is the strategy. It is entirely
transparent and only slightly relatable, and I have made my peace with it.

## Bring your own home

<!-- concept: byod/close -->
<!-- concept: dome_park/close -->
<!-- concept: byod/share -->

A hotel sells you a night. A short let sells you a fortnight. A lease sells
you a year and charges you to leave early. None of them sells the thing most
people actually want, which is to live somewhere for a while without handing
over the value of everything they do to the place while they are there.

Bring your own home. Pay the host for the ground and the services. Improve
your house as much as you like, because it is your house, and take all of it
with you when you go.

The source, the model's inputs and the experiments are open -- actually open,
not open as a marketing word. The drawings, the prices and the things that
failed are all there to check, and every number in this Part came off them,
including the ones that argue against it.

If this book was worth your time, hand it to one person who can carry the idea
further than I can: somebody who could build it, host it, or needs it.
