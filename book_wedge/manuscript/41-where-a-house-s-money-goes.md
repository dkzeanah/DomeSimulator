## Where its time goes

<!-- concept: harvest/house_time -->

A new house takes about seven and a half months from the first shovel to finished,
by the Census count, and much longer when its owner builds it. One builder's
week-by-week schedule shows where that time goes, and most of it is inside: the
ground and foundation are a small share, framing a smaller one, and insulation,
drywall, floors and trim the largest. The frame -- the part this book is about -- is
one of the shortest stages of building a house.

## Labor is not most of it

<!-- concept: harvest/labor_share -->
<!-- concept: harvest/trailer -->

So is labour most of what you pay for? Not in a new house. Take the building's
labour as the survey's share and it is ${{house.labor}}: **{{house.labor_pct}}
percent of the price**. Lumber at its record high moves the number further toward
materials, not away.

A manufactured home is built the other way. Of a ${{trailer.price}} one, the
dealer's share is ${{trailer.dealer}}, the factory's materials ${{trailer.materials}},
its payroll ${{trailer.payroll}} and its overhead and profit ${{trailer.other}}.
Factory labour is only **{{trailer.labor_pct}} percent** of the price. Building
indoors on a line is what makes it cheap -- the same lesson as Chapter {{ch.body}}.

## Where the saving comes from

<!-- concept: harvest/stacking -->

Stack it up and the saving on the floor has four sources, and the shape is the
smallest of them:

    bought whole                              ${{stack.buy}}
    the builder's and seller's share you keep ${{stack.margin}}
    labour you do yourself                    ${{stack.labor}}
    lumber from your own trees                ${{stack.trees}}
    the shape itself                          ${{stack.shape}}

Most of the saving is building your own, which anyone who builds their own house
keeps, whatever its shape. The dome's own contribution is real, and it is the
smallest line.

## What this does not show

<!-- concept: harvest/limits -->

And the half of the sum that does not flatter the dome. **{{stack.unchanged_pct}}
percent of what a house costs to build does not care about its shape at all**: fees,
windows, cabinets, appliances, floors, the driveway. Only the frame's hours were
measured; the rest of the labour is weeks more of somebody's time, and wiring,
plumbing and gas usually need a licensed trade and an inspection. And a triangle is
harder to finish inside than a flat wall.
