# Raised domes

A raised dome is one lifted clear of the ground on posts, piers or -- in this
method's more speculative corner -- cables.

WHY

Flood. Getting the floor above water is the whole reason in some places.

Slope. A raised platform gets level out of ground that is not, without moving
earth.

Airflow underneath, which keeps a timber building dry.

The view, and the space beneath, which becomes storage, parking or shade.

WHAT IT DEMANDS

The platform becomes the structure. The dome above it is a shell that
resolves its own loads; the posts and beams below are a frame that racks and
must be braced in both directions.

Wind matters more. A raised building has more exposed area, and the
overturning moment is the wind load times the height -- so going up
compounds.

Access is a stair, and the stair is not an afterthought.

THE VERSION THIS METHOD REACHES FOR

Hung, not stood.

The reference build prices a mast through the utility column, a floor that
clamps to that mast, an apex lifting ring and three cables to saddles on two
trees. The rig is $1,490, the mast and the dome's own floor are
$622 and $1,899, and the whole floating upgrade is
$4,011 -- against a frame weighing 2,478 pounds.

Saddles, not holes: nothing is drilled into the trees.

THE SENTENCE THAT HAS TO GO WITH IT

That is a design possibility, not an engineered structure.

The loads on the trees, the cables and the mast need an engineer before
anybody stands under it. The model prices the parts; it does not rate them,
and the difference between those two things is the whole of somebody's
safety.
