# 2V

The 2V is the reference build and the shape the rest of this book assumes.

WHAT IT IS

Each face of the icosahedron divided into four, giving 80 triangles on the
sphere and 40 faces on the hemisphere --
10 of one kind and 30 of the other.

Two strut lengths, A and B, rather than one.

WHY IT IS THE SWEET SPOT

Two lengths is manageable. You can sort two piles in your head; five you
cannot, and above that people start colour-coding and still get it wrong.

It is round enough. The faceting is visible but not dominant.

It is big enough to live in. The reference build is a
19.42-foot sphere cut at the equator --
296 square feet inside the circle, or
277 inside the ten-sided figure the base ring
actually makes.

And the parts count is buildable by hand: 26 vertices,
120 members, 55 interior seams and
10 base edges.

THE NUMBERS THAT DEFINE IT

Radius 116.4984 inches. The A chord is 72.000 inches
and the B chord 63.6703.

Note what that does not say. The chord is the distance between two vertices;
the *stock* you cut is neither. An A member is
70.182 inches of timber against a
72-inch chord, because a pinwheel member runs past the
vertex rather than stopping at it.

Confusing the chord with the cut is the most expensive mistake available in
this method, and it is available on the very first cut.

Dihedral angles fall between 157.5 and
162.0 degrees -- a spread of under five degrees
across the whole building, which is why one gasket thickness works
everywhere. That is topology, so it holds at any diameter.

309 feet of interior seam, reaching every vertex.

THE THING IT DOES NOT SOLVE

No panel of a 2V at this size fits a four-foot sheet.

The AAA faces are 62.354 inches across their narrowest
altitude and the BAB faces 52.516. A sheet is 48. The
narrowest panel in the building is four and a half inches too wide, in every
rotation.

That is entirely a consequence of picking this frequency at this diameter. A
different diameter moves it, and this one was picked for a six-foot member
rather than for a sheet of plywood.
