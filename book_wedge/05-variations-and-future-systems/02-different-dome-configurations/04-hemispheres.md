# Hemispheres

A hemisphere is a sphere cut at its equator, and it is the default for good
reasons and one bad one.

THE GOOD REASONS

It sits flat. The cut is a plane, the base is a circle, and the building
lands on level ground without anything below it.

It is the most enclosed volume per unit of surface of anything you can build.
That is where the 41 percent envelope saving over a box comes from.

The base ring is a compression ring. Cut at the equator, the ring is in pure
hoop stress, which is the most efficient thing a ring can be doing. Cut above
or below and there is a vertical component the ring has to resolve.

The maths is clean. Strut lengths and angles for hemispheres are published;
a cut anywhere else has to be solved.

THE BAD REASON

Habit. Most domes are hemispheres because most domes are hemispheres.

WHAT A HEMISPHERE IS ACTUALLY LIKE TO BE IN

The reference build is 19.42 feet across and 9.708 feet to
the apex. The floor inside the base ring is
277 square feet.

That sounds generous and in the middle it is. At the perimeter the wall is
leaning in hard: a hemisphere's surface is 45 degrees from vertical at
half its height, so by the time you are at head height near the edge, the
usable floor is well inside the footprint.

So the floor area a hemisphere gives you and the floor area you can stand
upright on are different numbers, and the second is the one that matters.

It is the first thing people are disappointed by, and it is the reason the
next three sections exist.
