# Hollow members

A hollow member is a box: four thin walls enclosing air, in the same outside
shape as the solid one.

THE ARGUMENT FOR

Stiffness lives near the surface. In bending, the material at the middle of a
section is doing very little, and taking it out costs less strength than it
saves weight.

At 15.3 square inches of solid section, a hollow
equivalent might use half the material for most of the stiffness.

The frame weighs 2,478 pounds in green pine. Halving that changes what two
people can lift, which changes how the dome gets built.

THE ARGUMENT AGAINST, WHICH IS DECISIVE HERE

You cannot make one with a saw.

A hollow triangular member is four pieces, ripped accurately, glued or
fastened along four long joints, in a jig, by somebody with a shop. That is
the exact work the wedge method exists to avoid.

And the whole harvest advantage disappears. The method's economy comes from
using 88 percent of a trunk instead of the 45 percent a mill gets. A hollow
member is made from milled boards, so it starts from the 45.

WHERE IT WOULD MAKE SENSE

A manufactured dome, in a factory, where the jig is a fixed cost spread over
hundreds of frames and the weight saving compounds through shipping.

Not for somebody with a chainsaw and two trees.

THE MIDDLE OPTION NOBODY TRIES

Solid wedges with the middle bored out along the length. It keeps the sawn
faces, keeps the continuous grain at the surface, and removes some of the
weight.

It is fiddly, it needs a long auger, and nobody has measured what it does.
Listed here as an idea rather than a recommendation.
