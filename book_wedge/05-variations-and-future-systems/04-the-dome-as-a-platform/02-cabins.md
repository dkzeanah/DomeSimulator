# Cabins

A cabin is the hardest thing on this list and the one most people want, so
it is worth being straight about what it is like.

WHAT YOU GET

277 square feet, one round room,
9.7 feet at the middle.

The reference build's standard article lists at $18,834.

WHAT IS HARD ABOUT IT

The perimeter. Furniture is rectangular and the wall is not, so a bed against
the wall has a triangle of dead space behind it and a wardrobe will not stand
upright at the edge.

Subdivision. Putting a wall inside a round room wastes more of it than the
same wall in a rectangular one, and a bathroom in a corner of a dome takes a
disproportionate bite.

Sound. A curved ceiling focuses sound at the centre of the room, which is
noticeable and occasionally unpleasant.

Doors. There is no vertical wall for one, which is why the entry extension
exists.

WHAT SOLVES MOST OF IT

A knee wall. Three feet of vertical between the pad and the dome fixes the
furniture, fixes the door and grows the usable floor by more than its own
area. The reference build does not have one, and for somebody living in it
full time it is probably the right first change.

Built-in furniture, following the curve. This is genuinely better than
free-standing and it is what people who have lived in domes end up doing.

The utility panels. A bathroom hung on the *outside* of the shell takes none
of the floor, which is the reference build's answer to the subdivision
problem.

THE HONEST SUMMARY

A dome cabin is a lovely single volume and an awkward set of rooms. Build it
if you want the first. Do not expect it to become the second without a fight.
