# Prepared dome pads

A prepared pad is a serviced platform waiting for a building, and it turns a
piece of land into something that earns.

WHAT IS IN ONE

A compacted base. A platform -- $2,916 for the
framed version, $2,468 for a slab.

A service port in the middle with water, waste and power brought to it.

Latching points at the rim for a shell, and a footprint sized for a known
dome.

WHY A HOST WOULD BUILD ONE

Because the platform is the expensive, immovable, permission-requiring part,
and the dome is not.

A landowner who builds pads is renting the part of the building that has to
stay. A tenant who brings a dome owns the part that can leave. Neither is
renting from the other in the way a normal tenancy works, and that changes
the relationship.

The upkeep is the other half of it. A pad host maintains a platform and a
service connection -- not a roof, not a boiler, not somebody's kitchen. The
tenant's building is the tenant's problem.

THE NUMBER THAT DECIDES IT

Foundations run from 8 to 63 percent of the cost of the dome that stands on
them, depending which foundation and which dome.

At the low end the pad is a rounding error and the model is obviously
attractive to the host. At the high end the host has spent most of a building
and owns none of one.

That spread is the whole business case, and it is worth working out for the
specific pad and the specific dome before anybody builds ten of them.

THE HONEST DIFFICULTY

It is an unusual arrangement and the law in most places does not have a word
for it. Whether a dome on a pad is a building, a vehicle, a tenancy or a
chattel varies by jurisdiction, and the answer determines the tax, the
permit and the insurance.

Find that out first, locally, before the second pad.
