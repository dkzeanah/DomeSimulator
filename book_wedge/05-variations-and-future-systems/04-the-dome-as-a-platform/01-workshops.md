# Workshops

A workshop is the best first dome anybody builds, for reasons that have
nothing to do with domes.

WHY

Nobody lives in it, so the things this shape is bad at do not matter. The
leaning perimeter is where the benches go. The lack of a vertical wall is
irrelevant when the storage is shelving bolted to the frame.

And the things it is good at are exactly what a workshop wants: one open
volume, no columns, 277 square feet of clear floor,
and daylight from every direction.

THE FIT-OUT

Benches round the perimeter, under the lean, where the headroom is lowest and
you are sitting or standing at a bench anyway.

Machines in the middle, where the ceiling is 9.7 feet.

Storage on the inserts. Three of them hold about 540 pounds, and there are
160 of them round the building.

WHAT TO SPECIFY DIFFERENTLY

Power, generously. A workshop wants circuits a house does not, and adding
them later means opening the column.

Dust extraction, which needs a route out. The apex is already a penetration
and it is the obvious one.

Light. Glazed bays low, for working light without glare, and a lot of
artificial light because a dome's curved ceiling scatters it beautifully.

Heat that comes on quickly, because a workshop is heated when occupied rather
than continuously, and this building has almost no thermal mass to fight.

THE REASON TO BUILD THIS ONE FIRST

Because everything you get wrong is recoverable, and you will have learned
the method on a building whose standards are forgiving before you build one
whose standards are not.
