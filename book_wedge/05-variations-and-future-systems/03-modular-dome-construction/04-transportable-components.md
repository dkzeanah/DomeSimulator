# Transportable components

Everything in this building is sized by what two people can carry, and that
constraint did more design work than any aesthetic decision.

THE PIECES, AND WHAT THEY WEIGH

A member: a six-foot wedge, comfortably one person.

A panel: 15.6 or 13.1 square feet of sheet.
Two people, awkwardly, overhead.

A shell slice: the hull splits into slices along S-lip seams. This is the one
that caught the film out -- an early cut said two people could carry a slice
and the model said 427 pounds. They cannot. It is a machine lift or more
slices.

The utility column: one object, on a trolley, out of one dome and into the
next.

WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN IT SOUNDS

A component that needs a crane needs a crane on the day it goes in and on
every day it might come out. A building assembled by machine is a building
that cannot be altered by its owner.

The whole deferment argument -- buy it cheap, improve it over years --
depends on every future step being something the owner can do.

THE SHIPPING VIEW

120 members at six feet, forty panels at about five feet, a shell in
slices and a column.

None of it is longer than a pickup bed. That is deliberate and it means the
building can reach a site that a truck cannot.

THE ONE THAT IS NOT TRANSPORTABLE

The pad. $2,916 of platform that stays where it
is when the dome leaves, because it is the host's.

That is the right division. The expensive, immovable, site-specific thing
belongs to the land, and the light, demountable, valuable thing belongs to
the person.
