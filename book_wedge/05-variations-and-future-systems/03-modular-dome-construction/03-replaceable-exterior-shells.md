# Replaceable exterior shells

The shell is a consumable and the frame is not, and separating them is the
single most consequential decision in this method.

THE TWO SHELLS THIS BOOK PRICES

A laminated hull: glass fibre in resin over the whole envelope. Sixty years,
genuinely waterproof, and $8,747
more than the alternative.

A shower cap: wood panels, one membrane, quilted layers added over the years,
and a rain-slick cap strapped over the lot. $3,430 bare.

WHY THE CAP WON

Because a hull is moulded once, at one size, and cannot grow.

Its cavity holds 4 quilted layers and then it is full. After
that, insulating further means losing floor area or building a second shell
outside the first, and neither happens.

A cap is a bag. Every layer under it buys the next cap a size up -- seven
layers makes the cap 15.6 percent bigger and nothing else about the building
changes.

THE FRAME DOES NOT CARE

That is the point. The frame is the same frame under either, and under
whatever comes third.

A shell that fails, or dates, or turns out to have been the wrong choice, is
a replacement rather than a demolition. The building underneath is untouched.

WHAT MAKES IT POSSIBLE

The shell lands on the frame, not on the panels, and it overlaps every seam.
It latches to the pad at the rim. It has one penetration, at the apex, closed
by a gasketed cap.

Take off the latches and the cap and it lifts. Nothing else in the building
is attached to it.

THE HULL IS NOT GONE

It became the upgrade -- the fifty-year option, sold to the buyer who wants
it, at $27,581 against
$18,834.

That is the shape of every decision in this method: ship the cheap thing that
works, and let the expensive thing remain available.
