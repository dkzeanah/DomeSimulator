# 20-foot reference dome

This is the dome the rest of this book prices, the one the simulator solves,
and the one every figure in the campaign film comes from.

THE NUMBERS

    diameter                   19.42 ft
    apex height                 9.71 ft
    A strut, cut length       70.182 in
    B strut, cut length       62.681 in
    floor, inside the ring     277.0 sq ft
    shell surface              549.8 sq ft
    timber in the frame          664 ft

Precisely: a radius of 116.4984 inches, so
19.416 feet across and 9.708 to the apex.

THE PARTS

26 vertices. 40 faces --
10 AAA at 15.59 square feet and
30 BAB at 13.13.

120 members: 60 A at
70.182 inches and 60 B at
62.681, against chords of 72.000 and
63.6703.

55 interior seams, 10 base edges,
309 feet of seam channel.

WHY THIS SIZE

Because the member is six feet.

Everything else follows from that. A six-foot stick is what comes
comfortably out of a twelve-foot log section, it is what one person carries,
and it fits in a pickup. The diameter is the answer to the member, not the
other way round.

WHAT IT COSTS

12,036 dollars for the standard article, shower cap and all.

THE INCONVENIENCE

No panel fits a four-foot sheet. The narrowest face is
52.516 inches across and a sheet is 48.

That is a direct consequence of sizing the dome from the member rather than
from the sheet, and it is the price of the decision. Anybody who would rather
have the sheets than the six-foot member should build a different diameter,
and the tables in the next chapter will give them one.
