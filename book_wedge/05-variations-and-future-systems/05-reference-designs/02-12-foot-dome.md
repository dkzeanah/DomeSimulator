# 12-foot dome

Twelve feet is the smallest dome that is genuinely useful, and the largest
one person can build alone without much trouble.

THE NUMBERS

    diameter                     12.00 ft
    apex height                   6.00 ft
    A strut, cut length         43.375 in
    B strut, cut length         38.739 in
    floor, inside the ring       105.8 sq ft
    shell surface                210.0 sq ft
    timber in the frame            411 ft

WHAT IT IS FOR

A greenhouse. A workshop for one person. A studio. A shed that is nicer than
a shed.

Not somewhere to live. At six feet to the apex the standing room is a circle
in the middle and the rest is bench height.

THE ADVANTAGES OF THIS SIZE

Every member is under four feet, so the stock comes out of small trees or
short offcuts, and the harvest argument works on timber a bigger dome could
not use.

Nothing needs lifting equipment or a second pair of hands.

And panels fit sheets. At this diameter the narrowest face is under four
feet across, so ordinary plywood works with no special order -- which is the
reference build's one real inconvenience, absent.

THE DISADVANTAGES

The perimeter is almost unusable. The wall leans in so sharply that only the
middle third of the floor has headroom.

A knee wall helps disproportionately here. Two feet of vertical on a
twelve-foot dome is a bigger change than three feet on a twenty.
