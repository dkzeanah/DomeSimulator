# Chord lengths

A chord is the straight line between two vertices.

It is not the curve of the sphere. The sphere curves; your timber does not.
Every member in the frame is a straight chord cutting across the inside of a
curved surface, and the dome is round only in the sense that enough straight
lines arranged carefully look round from a distance.

THE TWO CHORDS ON THIS DOME

A 2V dome has two of them. The book calls them A and B, and so does everyone
else.

On the reference build:

* A is 72.000 inches -- six feet, the longest member
* B is 63.670 inches -- five feet three and eleven-sixteenths

There are sixty of each in the frame.

CHORD FACTORS

The lengths above are for one dome at one size. The numbers that travel are
the chord factors: the chord divided by the sphere radius.

* A factor: 0.618034
* B factor: 0.546533

Multiply either by your radius and you have your member length. That is the
whole calculation, and it is the same calculation for every 2V dome that has
ever been built.

The A factor is worth a second look. 0.618034 is the golden ratio minus one,
and it is not a coincidence -- it falls out of the icosahedron the dome is
subdivided from. You do not need to care why. You do need to type it
correctly.

GOING THE OTHER WAY

Most people do not start with a radius. They start with the longest stick they
can get.

Divide it by 0.618034 and you have your radius. From there, multiply by
0.546533 for the B chord and you have your second length.

Seventy-two inches divided by 0.618034 is 116.4984 inches. Times 0.546533 is
63.670 inches. That is the reference build, derived from one decision about
timber.

WHAT THE CHORD IS NOT

The chord is the distance between vertex points -- the mathematical corners,
floating in space.

It is not the length of wood you cut.

Your actual sticks come out shorter than the chord, because the ends get
trimmed back where they meet other members, and how much shorter depends on
how the joint is made. On the reference build the A members come off the saw
between 68.977 and 70.182 inches of usable stock against a 72.000 inch chord.

That gap between the chord and the stick is where most first-dome errors live.
It has its own chapter.
