# Triangle heights

The height of a triangle -- the perpendicular distance from one side to the
opposite corner -- is the number that decides whether your panel fits on a
sheet of anything.

It is also the number nobody calculates until they are standing in the
plywood aisle.

THE NUMBERS

On the reference build:

* an AAA triangle is 72.000 inches on every side, and its height is 62.354
  inches
* a BAB triangle has a 72.000 inch long edge and two 63.670 inch sides, and
  its height above the long edge is 52.520 inches

Both of those are the *minimum* width the triangle can be squeezed into. You
cannot rotate a triangle to make it narrower than its shortest altitude.

WHY THAT NUMBER RUINED A PLAN

A sheet of plywood is 48 inches wide.

62.354 is not 48. 52.520 is not 48 either.

No panel of this dome fits on a four-foot sheet. Not in any rotation, not with
any clever nesting, not if you turn it diagonally. The narrowest panel in the
building is still four and a half inches wider than the widest sheet you can
buy at a normal yard.

That is not a small inconvenience. It decides how the whole skin gets built.
You either buy oversized sheet goods, or you sheet across the frame and cut on
site, or you build the panels up from narrower boards.

The reference build sheets across the frame and tapes the joints, because that
is the option that does not require a special order.

CHECK THIS BEFORE YOU DESIGN ANYTHING

Take your longest member. Multiply by 0.866 for an equilateral panel -- that
is your AAA height. Compare it to whatever you were planning to skin the dome
with.

If the answer is bigger than your sheet, you have just found out something
important, and you have found it out for free rather than in a car park with a
sheet of ply that does not fit in the car.
