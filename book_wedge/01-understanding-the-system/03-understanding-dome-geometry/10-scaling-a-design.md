# Scaling a design

Scaling a 2V dome is one multiplication, and then a list of things that do not
scale with it.

WHAT SCALES

Pick your new longest member. Divide by 0.618034 to get the radius. Multiply
by 0.546533 to get the B member. Everything geometric follows:

* diameter is twice the radius
* height is the radius, for a hemisphere
* every chord is its factor times the radius

Double the member length and you double the radius, the diameter and the
height.

WHAT SCALES FASTER THAN YOU EXPECT

Area goes as the square. Double the dome and the floor goes up four times, and
so does the skin you have to buy.

The reference build is 19.416 feet across with 277 square feet of decagonal
floor and 592 square feet of spherical envelope. Double it to a member of
twelve feet and the floor is over 1,100 square feet. That is not a bigger
dome; that is a different building with a different budget.

WHAT DOES NOT SCALE AT ALL

The counts. Forty triangles, sixty-five edges, twenty-six vertices, fifty-five
interior seams, 120 members. A nine-foot 2V and a forty-foot 2V have exactly
the same parts list, with different numbers written on it.

The angles. Every dihedral is the same at every size -- 18.029 degrees on an A
seam whether the dome is a playhouse or a hall. Angles are a property of the
shape, not the size, and that is why one jig design works across the whole
range.

WHAT SCALES THE WRONG WAY

Your timber.

The chords scale linearly, but the *stock* you need to cut them from does not
behave as politely. A six-foot member comes out of an eight-foot log with
room. A twelve-foot member does not come out of a sixteen-foot log the same
way, because the taper of a real tree means the wedge cross-section changes
along its length, and the far end of a long member is coming from a thinner
part of the trunk.

There is a size above which you stop being able to get your members out of one
tree, and it arrives sooner than the arithmetic suggests.

THE ADVICE

Scale up by going up in frequency, not by stretching the members.

If you want more span, a 3V dome with the same six-foot sticks covers more
ground than a 2V with nine-foot ones, and every stick still comes off the same
saw, out of the same size log, with the same jig. You get a bigger building
without changing anything about how you make the parts.

Stretching the member is the obvious way to get a bigger dome. Going up a
frequency is the one that keeps the method intact.
