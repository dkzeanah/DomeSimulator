# A/B/C strut families

A strut family is a set of members that are all the same length and all do the
same job. Domes are described by how many families they have.

A 2V dome has two: A and B. A 3V has three, usually called A, B and C. A 1V
has one.

WHAT A AND B ACTUALLY ARE ON THIS DOME

A members are the longer ones. Sixty of them, 72.000 inches of chord.

B members are the shorter ones. Sixty of them, 63.670 inches of chord.

They are not scattered at random. They sit in a pattern, and the pattern is
worth learning because it is how you check your work without counting
everything.

THE PATTERN

Look at the top of the dome. There is a ring of five triangles around the
apex, and every edge of every one of them is an A. Those are the AAA
triangles. There are ten of them in the hemisphere.

Everything else is a BAB triangle: one A and two Bs. There are thirty of
those.

Ten plus thirty is forty triangles, which is the whole dome.

So: if you are looking at a triangle and all three sticks are the same length,
you are near the top. If two are short and one is long, you are not. That is a
check you can do from the ground with your eyes.

WHY THE FAMILIES MATTER MORE IN THIS METHOD

In a normal strut-and-hub dome the families are just two lengths. You cut
sixty of each, you keep them in two piles, and the difference between them is
eight inches.

In the wedge method they are more than that, because the two families sit at
different angles to each other and their seams fold by different amounts.

On the reference build:

* an A seam folds 18.029 degrees
* a B seam folds 22.459 degrees

Same dome, same wedge, four and a half degrees of difference depending on
which family you are looking at. That difference is not an error and it does
not average out. It is the geometry telling you that an A seam and a B seam
are different joints.

Mix up an A and a B and you will not just have a stick of the wrong length.
You will have a stick that wants to sit at the wrong angle.

Keep the piles apart. Mark them before they leave the saw.
