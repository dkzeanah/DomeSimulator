**Every house is an argument about what a person is worth.**

The straight wall argues one way — stack them, price them, and let the market decide who sleeps inside. The triangle argues another. The triangle says the load should be *shared*: that no member carries the roof alone, that strength comes from how things are joined and not from how much they cost.

A 2V dome is forty triangles and two lengths of wood. Thirty short. Thirty-five long. Cut them once, cut them true, and those same two numbers raise a shelter on a mountainside, on a lot outside Tulsa, on a field three weeks after the water went down. The geometry does not check your credit. The geometry does not know your zip code. A sphere encloses the most room with the least skin whether you are a developer or a widow — and that indifference is the most democratic thing left in construction.

So we built the whole argument into code.

One floor: no staircase to age out of, no doorway that needs an apology. Seams that drink the rain instead of leaking it. A south face that earns its keep in sunlight. Forty triangles from **two jigs** — which means a shop, which means a line, which means the second house goes up faster than the first, and the hundredth faster still. Not a handmade miracle. A repeatable answer.

We will not tell you it beats everything. We will show you the numbers and let you disagree. Every figure in this repository — every strut, every dollar, every labor-hour, every tree — is **computed, never claimed**. Where the case is strong, we make it. Where it is uncertain, we say so out loud. Where the honest answer is *not yet*, we wrote that down too — because a housing argument that only survives while nobody checks it isn't an argument. It's a sales pitch.

This is for the person who has done everything right and still can't get a key.

**Build one. Then build the next one faster.**
