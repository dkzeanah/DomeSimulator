# 14. Wood keeps changing after the cut

The model's geometry stays still after a calculation. Freshly cut timber continues to exchange moisture with its surroundings. Changes in dimensions, shape, and checking can affect joints that fitted at the first assembly. The field record therefore needs dates and moisture observations, not just a photograph of a tight new corner.

Record the timber species when it is reliably identified, the condition of the stock, how it was stored, and any moisture readings with the method used. Preserve examples of the cross-section at fabrication and at later inspection. When a seam changes, this record helps distinguish an initial cutting error from movement after assembly.

The USDA Wood Handbook organizes moisture relations, mechanical properties, fastenings, grading, and drying as separate but related subjects. Those are the subjects the prototype's material record needs to connect. [USDA Wood Handbook](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook).

Avoid turning an attractive green-wood fit into a prediction of long-term performance. The final fastening and seam design needs to account for the actual timber, exposure, and changes over time. The two-week schedule does not erase those later observations; it gives them a clear initial condition.

Reserve a few documented samples for follow-up. Mark them with the same tree and section IDs used in the member ledger. If a sample changes while the frame is in service, it becomes a small, accessible witness to the larger material process. Its behavior is evidence to interpret, not a substitute for inspecting the actual frame.
