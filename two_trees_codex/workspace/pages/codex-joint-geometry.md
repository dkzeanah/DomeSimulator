# 17. The triangle corner is a physical object

The ideal triangle has vertices where lines meet. Real members have width, depth, and ends that must occupy different space. The project's pinwheel arrangement gives each member a receiving role at one end and a terminating role at the other. One member ends against the side of its neighbor while receiving another member in turn.

Earlier explanatory code describes simplified square-ended pinwheel members. The current raw-wedge simulator carries more detailed corner geometry: the terminating end is solved against its receiving surface, and the other end is clipped to the triangle's envelope. The corresponding cut planes and stock extents should govern the fabrication discussion for this configuration.

This correction matters to the story. Preserving the longitudinal forty-five-degree sector does not imply that every end is a square crosscut. The long radial faces, the panel-to-panel fold, and the member's end cuts are separate geometric questions. A process can preserve the raw sector while still requiring an oblique end cut.

Start with a full-size representative corner using the intended cross-section and orientation. Mark the mathematical reference points and the actual cut surfaces separately. Check the receiving relationship, the available bearing region, the fastening space, and whether either member extends beyond the intended panel envelope.

Move from that corner to one A-A-A panel and one B-A-B panel. Check their reference dimensions and their physical boundaries. A fixture that reproduces one family does not automatically reproduce the other. Before cutting the batch, retain a labeled master example and the configuration that produced it.
