# 4. Define the experiment before starting the clock

Write the finish line in a sentence that someone else could check: produce and assemble the selected dome frame from documented source timber. Define which preparatory work happens before day one. Existing software development, drawings, fixtures, equipment purchases, site preparation, and professional design work may all be real contributions even when the cutting-and-framing clock has not started.

Use four milestone records: material available; accepted members produced; triangular panels checked; frame assembled and inspected against its design. A pile of ripped wedges reaches the first fabrication milestone. A standing frame reaches another. Neither should be counted as a finished home.

Keep an equipment ledger. The planned saw is identified by the author as a Husqvarna 120 Mark III from Lowe's. Record the nameplate and actual configuration rather than copying specifications from another generation of the 120. The manufacturer uses product-number information to distinguish variants; the purchase record resolves the particular machine in this story. [Husqvarna product identification](https://us-support.husqvarna.com/en/petrol-chainsaw/KA-70145).

Also record the other work required to make the saw useful: measuring, securing and supporting work, moving timber, maintaining the tool, marking members, making fixtures, and managing the site. A small saw does not make those operations disappear. It makes the organization around them especially visible.

Before field work, use training appropriate to the operations and the manufacturer's instructions for the exact equipment. Protective equipment, secure work support, separation from helpers, and emergency arrangements belong in the work plan. Felling and handling timber under load require skills that a chapter about dome geometry cannot supply. The Forest Service training material treats saw use as both classroom and field instruction. [USFS saw training guide](https://www.fs.usda.gov/t-d/pubs/htmlpubs/htm06672805/page01.htm).
