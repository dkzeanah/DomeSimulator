# 11. The shape of one eighth

For a circular trunk of radius r, one ideal eighth-sector has an angle of 45 degrees and area πr²/8. Its two radial sides each have length r. The outside is an arc, not a straight third side. Its arc length is πr/4; the straight chord joining the arc's endpoints is 2r sin(22.5°).

For an eight-inch diameter trunk, r is four inches. The ideal sector area is approximately 6.283 square inches. The curved arc is approximately 3.142 inches long, while its straight chord is approximately 3.061 inches. These describe a raw circular sector before kerf, bark removal, or other changes. Drawing a triangle in its place removes some of the wood the idea is trying to preserve.

For a twelve-inch diameter trunk, the ideal sector area grows to approximately 14.137 square inches. A six-foot length of that ideal sector contains about 1,017.9 cubic inches, or 7.07 solid-volume board feet. Board feet here are only a volume unit: 144 cubic inches per board foot. This calculation is not a lumber grade or a market valuation.

A wedge can have substantial area while still presenting difficult bearing surfaces and connections. Its orientation changes how it occupies the panel boundary. Its taper changes the section along its length. Its grain and defects change the material that carries load. The book therefore uses the cross-section to explain the shape and the inventory, while keeping strength evaluation attached to the actual member and design.

The illustration on this page should show a whole log cross-section beside one true sector, then the same sector oriented in the four ways explored by the simulator. Use end-grain views. A long perspective drawing can hide the very profile the reader needs to understand.
