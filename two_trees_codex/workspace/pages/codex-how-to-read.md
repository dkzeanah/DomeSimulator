# Before the first chapter

There are three ways to read this book. Follow the story to understand how the idea arrived. Follow the explanation to understand why the geometry works. Follow the how-to pages to organize measurements, prototypes, material, and assembly. Each chapter has a strand so that a reader can change pace without losing their place.

The title sets the scale of the experiment. Two trees are the intended source of the frame's wedge members. They do not include the ground connection, fasteners, seam components, skin, services, or everything required for a dwelling. The fortnight is a target for making and framing the dome, with its preparation stated openly. Occupancy is a later milestone with its own requirements.

The book uses **A for the long chord** and **B for the short chord**. Other references may reverse these letters. Compare lengths and factors before comparing names. Dimensions are inches unless marked feet; trunk diameter always means a measurement across the trunk, not the radius.

The number printed beside an ideal triangle is not automatically a saw cut. We will follow that distinction from the first sketch to the last stock check. A **blank** is the piece available to fabricate a member. A **member** is the finished wooden part. An **edge** is a line in the mathematical mesh. A **seam** is the interface between neighboring panels. Confusing these words is an easy way to build the right arithmetic into the wrong object.

The geometry in this draft traces to the project files listed in the source appendix. Practical prescriptions for structural connections must be supported by an appropriate design for the actual site and timber. The simulator establishes shape and fit; it does not establish allowable loads. This boundary is part of describing the project accurately.
