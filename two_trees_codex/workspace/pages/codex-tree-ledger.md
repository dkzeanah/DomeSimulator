# 10. Eight sections, sixty-four blanks, sixty accepted members

| Quantity | One tree | Two trees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planned six-foot sections | 8 | 16 |
| Potential blanks at eight per section | 64 | 128 |
| Target allocation | 60 | 120 |
| Potential spare or rejected blanks | 4 | 8 |

The eight potential spares represent 6.25 percent of the gross 128-piece inventory. If eight blanks are rejected, the remaining 120 exactly meet the count and leave no accepted replacements. At a ten-percent rejection rate, the arithmetic no longer supports a complete 120-member frame from this inventory. Report that shortage while there is still time to change the plan.

Eight six-foot sections contain forty-eight feet of net section length. They cannot all be cut from exactly forty-eight feet while also losing material to separating cuts. If seven interior bucking cuts remove a quarter inch each, the minimum span before outer-end trimming is 48 feet plus 1.75 inches, or about 48.146 feet. Additional cuts at the ends and defect removals require more length. This example declares its kerf assumption; it is not a measurement of the author's saw.

Separate bucking kerf from ripping kerf. The first reduces the longitudinal inventory as sections are separated. The second removes wood from their cross-sections as wedges are produced. Both matter, but subtracting one from the other would mix different dimensions.

One organizational option is to reserve one tree's qualified blanks for A members and the other tree's for B members. That makes the title easy to explain, but assignment should follow the actual inventory. A short or defective A candidate may be perfectly useful in a B position if its material properties and finished dimensions meet the design. Keep its original tree and section ID when reassigning it.

The project's earlier timber demonstration also explores twelve-foot sections split and then divided into shorter members. That is a different cut plan. Its yield cannot be copied onto this eight-by-six-foot plan without accounting for usable trunk length and every additional crosscut.
