# 22. Follow an assembly plan that can stay stable

The assembly order must come from a plan for the actual panels, connections, equipment, and site. A complete dome's behavior does not describe an incomplete ring of panels. Temporary support, controlled handling, and the limits of each intermediate stage need their own attention.

Before raising begins, lay out the panel map and verify every label against it. Identify each planned seam, the connection components it requires, and the stage at which it can be checked. Record where temporary support is required and what inspection permits a stage to proceed. These details should be supplied by the build's actual assembly and structural plan.

The earlier Frankendome notes discuss a top-down experiment. That history belongs in the narrative, but it does not choose the safe order for this different frame. The present book should show the sequence actually adopted, with its equipment and supports visible in the photographs.

As the frame grows, compare reference nodes and the base with the model. A forced seam is useful information. Pause to identify whether the cause is the base layout, a panel dimension, an orientation error, or a connection mismatch. Record the diagnosis and correction so the story preserves the discovery rather than editing out the difficulty.

The closure is an important scene only if its conditions are visible. Photograph the final relationship before and after connection, record any adjustment, and retain the final dimensional checks. The reader should be able to understand what “it fit” meant beyond the relief of seeing the last piece in place.
