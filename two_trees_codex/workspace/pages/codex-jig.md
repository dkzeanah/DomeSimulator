# 18. Build the reference into the fixture

A fixture is a way to make the important measurements repeatable. Begin with the selected triangle's reference geometry, then establish stops, registration surfaces, and cut references from the actual physical model. The fixture should make it clear which part of the irregular wedge is being located and which remains free to vary.

The simulator's jig stages are useful illustrations because they separate setup, placement, and trimming. Review them with the chosen model. Do not transfer a picture's apparent dimension onto wood: use the current cut schedule and a verified full-size layout.

Write down the allowance used at each end. The model includes concepts such as head overfit and butt hangoff. Those are deliberately sacrificial pieces of stock, and their values can make a blank longer than the final member. A six-foot finished part with additional holding stock requires more than six feet of raw material.

After the representative panels are assembled, compare them with the reference layout. Check edge references, corner positions, plane of the frame, and the surfaces that will meet adjacent panels. Record the acceptance criteria from the actual design. “Looks triangular” is not a measurement a second builder can reproduce.

The fixture record should contain an overhead photograph, the version of the layout, the panel family, the wedge orientation, the stock cross-section used for the trial, and any adjustments made after the first piece. If a stop moves during the batch, identify which parts were made before and after that change.

The fixture is also a storytelling object. It shows the moment the project becomes a repeatable process. A reader can see how the useful idea was transferred from screen to bench, and which details had to change when the material became real.
