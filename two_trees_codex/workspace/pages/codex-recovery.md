# 13. Measure recovery instead of defending a percentage

The discovery notes propose a high wood recovery and compare it with conventional milling. That is a useful hypothesis. To test it, define the numerator and denominator before looking at the result. Gross solid trunk volume, sawn sector volume, accepted member volume, and installed member volume are different quantities.

For a simplified circular section, the total kerf area of four diametral cut planes can be estimated by 4Dk, where D is diameter and k is kerf width. Compared with the round area πD²/4, the simple retained fraction is approximately 1 − 16k/(πD). The approximation ignores overlaps between cut bands near the center and assumes the intended cut geometry. Actual kerf paths, bark, irregular shape, end losses, and rejected wood change the result.

At D = 12 inches and k = 0.25 inch, this simple estimate retains approximately 89.39 percent before those other losses. At D = 8 inches with the same kerf assumption, it retains approximately 84.08 percent. The change with diameter is enough to show why one attractive percentage should not stand in for an entire tapered tree.

The earlier project timber model computes a tapered trunk by sections and compares its wedge output with its own rectangular packing model. Record that comparison's assumptions: section length, kerf, taper, nominal or actual rectangular dimensions, and acceptance criteria. A result for true two-by-fours is not automatically a result for dressed nominal two-by-fours.

The book's final comparison should use the same starting inventory on both sides. Include labor, equipment, defects, and the material that genuinely replaces a required product. A wedge with more volume than a rectangular stick is not automatically worth that volume multiplied by a store price. It must perform the intended job through an appropriate design.

The author can make the strongest account by keeping the unfriendly rows: pieces rejected, setup that took longer, and usable stock left over. Those rows show where the method can improve and prevent a gross-material argument from pretending to be a finished-building argument.
