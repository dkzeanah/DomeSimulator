# 20. What happens between two panels

At an interior edge, two outward panel normals point in slightly different directions. The small angle between them is the fold: F = acos(N1 · N2) for normalized normals, with the numerical dot product clamped to the valid range. The internal supplementary angle is 180 degrees minus F. Print the definition beside the number because “dihedral” is used in different ways.

The raw sector contributes its own fixed face relationships. In the original point-inward comparison, the ideal residual gap angle is related to 45 degrees minus the panel fold. The actual contact surfaces, offsets, and selected seam mode must still be solved in their physical positions. An attractive sketch does not define a fabrication polygon.

The project's seam studies include rigid keys, hose or gasket concepts, and a shaved-flat alternative. The current raw-trapezoid mode preserves the longitudinal sector faces and uses a truncated key profile. The shaved-flat configuration changes those seam faces and uses a different key. These are distinct construction choices and should have separate captions and schedules.

A rigid shape can help establish a geometric relationship when the surfaces meet as intended. A round hose or gasket mainly fills space and can permit relative rotation unless the connection independently constrains it. Filling a gap, setting an angle, transferring structural forces, and managing water are different jobs. A single interface may need several components to do them.

Make a representative two-panel seam before the larger assembly. Use the actual timber profiles and the proposed connection detail.
