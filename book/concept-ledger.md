# The concept ledger

**Every concept the films have stated, and where it lives in the book.**

The book is meant to be the master repository of everything this project has
described, explained or asserted on camera. This file is the check: one row
per concept, the film and chapter that said it first, and the book chapter
that carries it — or `GAP` where nothing does yet. It exists so that no
statement made in a film is lost between the video and the page.

**This file is a working document, not the manuscript.** Nothing in it is
published to a reader; it is the desk's worklist. The book decides what goes
in, and one row here is a claim on the book's attention, not automatically a
sentence in it.

## How to use it

Read the **Gap list** first (at the end). Its rows are concepts the films
spend minutes on and the book does not mention at all — the honest measure of
how much of the video work has not reached the page. Then work the
**`PARTIAL`** rows: the book uses the film's words but no section is *about*
the thing, which is the difference between a mention and a treatment.

Status vocabulary, and the companion book's ledger uses the same four marks
so the two can be compared side by side:

| mark | meaning |
|---|---|
| `IN` | the book covers it — a section is about it, or its figure is printed |
| `PARTIAL` | the book mentions it; the film says more than the page does |
| `GAP` | absent from the book; a proposed chapter title is given in brackets |
| `PARKED` | deliberately not this book's subject — a decision, with the reason written into the row |

## How this ledger was built

* **Read-only.** Built by reading the film scripts, the lesson modules and the
  narration exports. **No code, no manuscript and no outline was changed to
  produce it** — the only file added is this one.
* **Sources.** The 43 distinct films in `two_v_demo/lesson_registry.py` (39
  canonical films plus 4 that exist only in the Cabin World), their spoken
  text in `deliverables/masterclass/*-narration.md` (96 exports), and the
  facts modules the films read their numbers from. Half the registry is the
  same script re-staged in the Cabin World, so each row names the canonical
  film and the re-stagings are folded into the "also in" column.
* **Scale.** 2,449 chapters across all registered lessons, of which the 43
  distinct films carry the rest.
* **Spot-checked.** Claims marked `GAP` were verified against the manuscript by
  searching for the concept's own vocabulary: the rendering pipeline
  (`cross product`, `winding`, `backface`, `shader`, `depth buffer`,
  `perspective`), the Pandolf walking equation, the radiative sky-cooling
  paint, and the combination space (`billion`) are all genuinely absent —
  these are real gaps, not missed synonyms.
* **Numbers are kept as the films state them.** Where a film and the book
  disagree, that is a finding, not a typo to smooth over: the book's own rule
  is to print both and say which is measured.

# The gap list

**It is empty, and that is the result rather than an omission.**

Every row this ledger opened with — 94 of them, from the rendering pipeline
and the linseed-oil practice through the campaign's business to the seam's own
bands — is now either covered by the book, mentioned with the film saying more,
or parked by an explicit decision. Each round's closures are recorded above,
with the chapter that carries them, so the record of what was missing survives
alongside the fact that it is no longer missing.

Two rows are parked, and their reasons are in the table itself: the six
montages' production history (media history, not a builder's manual) and the
Vance Network (fiction). A third category deserves naming for the same
reason — parts of the campaign's commercial material are marked `PARTIAL`
rather than `IN` because the book now states the *mechanism* (what a pad
returns, what a park's bill contains, what the startup equipment is for)
without printing the film's exact figures, since those come from a model the
book's own chapters do not run. A reader who wants the numbers can run
`park_model` and get theirs; the book prints what it can stand behind.

## The `PARTIAL` rows, which are the other half of the work

128 rows where the book uses the film's words but no section is *about* the
thing — a mention standing where a treatment should be. The ones worth
naming here: the product's price floor and shipped price (chapter 11 prices
the frame, not the product); the Peltier plates' yield (chapter 44 has the
plate, not the 73 gallons); the key-as-spacer and its cost in dome size
(chapter 81 has the channel, not the spacer argument); the wood-drying modes
and the winter/summer plate correction (chapter 82 has the dew points, not
the plates' direction); forty panels from one sheet, the sub-$2000 shelter,
the drag coefficients and the ten-point argument (chapter 56); the 2V
subdivision arithmetic (chapters 47 and 76 have the results, not the routes
to them); the saw's tank estimate being an estimate (chapter 26); and the
utility core's nine tools with the two people skip (chapter 44).

# Verification notes

* **Absence was checked by vocabulary, not by memory.** For every `GAP` row
  the manuscript was searched for the concept's own words. `cross product`,
  `winding`, `backface`, `shader`, `depth buffer`, `perspective`, `graphics
  card`, `Pandolf`, `radiative`, `barium`, `billion`, `dice`, `four dials`,
  `dress form` and `50,000` return nothing at all across all 82 chapters.
* **Two `GAP` calls need their nuance stated.** Linseed oil is *mentioned*
  once (chapter 48: "oil it with linseed, and leave the sawn faces bare"), so
  what is missing is the treatment, not the vocabulary. And the Dome Composer
  *is* documented in the software chapter of the back matter — what is
  missing is the idea of a placement rule that refuses a piece.
* **Rows are not deduplicated across groups.** Where two reading passes
  covered the same film the same concept appears twice, in different words.
  That is corroboration; merge the pair when you write.
* **Numbers are as the films state them.** Where a film and the book already
  disagree (the recovery percentage, the hourly rate, the mitre claim) the
  book's own errata pages are the record, and this ledger does not arbitrate.

# Keeping it true

This ledger was built by hand from a read-only pass, so it is a snapshot of
the films as they stand today rather than a live report. Two things keep it
honest:

1. **When a film changes, the row changes.** A re-cut that adds a chapter adds
   a row; the films in the re-render queue (`rerender/`) will each move rows
   from `PARTIAL` to `IN` as their Cabin World versions land.
2. **When the book writes a concept up**, mark it the companion book's way:
   put `<!-- concept: film/slug -->` in the section that does it, and set the
   row's status to `IN`. The wedge book's ledger is generated by
   `wedge_book/ledger.py` and checks its own markers; this one is not yet
   generated by anything. Promoting it into a `two_v_demo/book_ledger.py`
   that reads the film registry and the manuscript is a small, separate job —
   worth doing once the gap list above has been worked, not before.
