# 37. Panel Drawings

*One flat drawing per distinct panel, full size*

## Panel Drawings

The book's last word on the panel is a drawing, not a paragraph —
one flat, full-size drawing per distinct panel, exported directly
from the simulator, so the drawing on the page and the panel on
the bench are the same object.

The drawings are the check for everything the jig chapters
described. The jig locates members against stops; the drawing is
what the stops were set from. The panels come off the bench; the
drawing is what they are measured against before they are stacked.
And because the drawings export from the same solved model that
produced the cut list and the seam schedule, the whole build has
one source of truth, and it is printed on these pages.

## Reading a panel drawing

What every line on the drawing is, so the page can be read at the
bench and not only admired.

**The outline** is the panel's finished edge — the triangle the
neighbours will meet, gasket allowance included. **The member
lines inside it** are the three sticks: butts located on sides,
heads running to the fence line, the pinwheel's overlaps shown as
the overlaps they are. **The vertex gap** is the empty corner of
Chapter {{ch.pinwheel}} — drawn, not described, so nobody looks
for a hub there. **The seam allowances** are the half-gasket
insets at every edge, the {{jig.gasket_in}}-inch key's share of
each side, which is why the outline is slightly inside the
triangle the members describe.

The drawings print full size: exported as vectors, they scale to
the bench without loss, and a printed drawing laid on the jig's
surface should match the jig's stops line for line. That is their
real job — not illustration but *witness*. When a panel comes off
the bench, it is laid on its drawing; when a jig is first set,
the drawing is what the stops are checked against; and when the
frame finally closes, every panel in it has, somewhere in its
history, been held up against one of these two pages and found to
be the thing the pages describe.

The reference part of the book ends here, with the drawings —
because after the drawings there is nothing left to know about
the panel. The next chapters leave the bench and follow the frame
to the ground it stands on.
