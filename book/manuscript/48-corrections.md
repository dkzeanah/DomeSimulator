# 48. Corrections

*Everything this project has published and had to fix*

> **This chapter corrects something.** Every published claim this project has had to revise.

## Corrections

This chapter is the project's ledger of its own mistakes, and it
exists because of a policy the book states once and follows
everywhere: **a correction is a chapter, never a silent re-cut.**

When a film or a brief or an early draft of this book said
something wrong, the fix was never to quietly edit the original.
The fix was to publish the correction, name the error, print
both versions, and show the arithmetic that decides between
them. A silent re-cut leaves the wrong version in the world and
teaches nothing; a named correction leaves the record honest and
the method for the next error visible. The entries below are the
project's full list — the mitre claim, the recovery percentage,
the hourly rate, the seam count — and the last section is the
invitation: find the next one, and this is where to send it.

## The mitre claim

Named in full, because it was said on camera and the correction
belonged on camera too.

An early film in this project claimed that no cut in the dome is
a mitre. The claim was wrong: the butt cut of Chapter
{{ch.butt_cut}} is a compound angle — a mitre and a bevel
together — and it is made on one end of every member. What is
true, and what the claim should have been, is the narrower
sentence the pinwheel chapter now carries: no member end is ever
mitred *to another member end*, and the one compound cut the
frame does need is made once per stick, off the jig, at a
handful of fixed settings.

The film's own correction chapter — the one that named the
error on camera — is the model this book's correction chapters
follow. The lesson is recorded here because the policy is the
point: the project's credibility was not damaged by the error,
it was built by the correction, and a project that never
corrects anything has simply stopped checking.

## The recovery percentage

Eighty-eight, or {{tree.recovery_pct}}? Both — and the reason
is a tree, not a mistake.

The project's brief and its early films said the split kept 88
per cent of the log. That number was computed for the brief's
reference tree: a {{pine.butt_in}}-inch pine. This book's tree is
smaller — {{tree.butt_diameter_in}} inches at the butt — and the
same split-and-kerf arithmetic on the smaller circle keeps
{{tree.recovery_pct}} per cent. Neither number is wrong; they
are two recoveries for two different trees, and Chapter {{ch.recovery}}
prints both, with the table that shows the difference shrinking
section by section as the taper runs.

The error, if there was one, was quoting a percentage without
naming its tree — and the correction is the discipline that
followed: every recovery figure in this book names its
diameter, or it is not printed.

## The hourly rate

Cross-referenced to Chapter {{ch.money}}, where the arithmetic
lives in full.

The project's brief said the fortnight's work was worth fifty
dollars an hour. The number was reached by doubling a volume
ratio for safety rather than by computing the section, and it
overshot. The computed rates — {{work.rate_nominal_usd}} dollars
an hour against nominal two-by-fours, {{work.rate_dressed_usd}}
against dressed — are the honest ones, and the honest note is
the strut table's: the dressed comparison is the true one, and
it is the *higher* of the two, because a dressed board is
smaller than its name. The correction's shape is the chapter's
shape: the claim, the error, the recomputation, all on the
page, none of it flattering.

## Seams against edges

The book's own error, kept because the book applies the policy
to itself.

An early draft of this book used the edge count where it meant
the seam count — {{frame.edges}} edges, {{frame.seams}} seams —
and the two are different things: an edge is a line in the
geometry, a seam is a joint a builder closes, and the
{{frame.rim_edges}} rim edges are lines that join nothing. The
draft's prose said the wrong one in the places where the
difference matters — the weather chapter, the schedule, the
counts — and the correction was not a silent edit but this
entry, plus the two counts now separated in the frame chapter
where they are first introduced.

The book's rule about its own errors is the reader's guarantee
about all the others: anything this project has published and
had to fix is listed here, and a number anywhere in these pages
can be checked against the software in the back matter, which
is the same check the corrections were found by.

## How to report one

The invitation, because the next error exists and the book wants
it.

If a number in this book disagrees with the geometry, the films,
or the arithmetic you can reproduce from the back matter, the
book wants to know. The corrections policy means the fix will
be a chapter — your finding, named, checked, and added to this
list — not a quiet revision of the page you read. That is the
deal the book offers its readers, and it is the same deal the
project made with itself on camera, the day it corrected the
mitre claim instead of re-cutting the film.

The list above is short. It is short because the project
checks, not because it never errs — and the checking is the
method, applied to the method's own words. Find the next error.
The chapter is waiting for it.
