# 62. The Quilt Network

*The insulation is a quilt of recycled clothing, and the quilt is the one part of the whole project a stranger can build at their own kitchen table -- which makes it the campaign's recruiting half, not its footnote*

## The Quilt Network

Every other part of this book needs a saw, a bench, or a
mortgage. The insulation needs a sewing machine and an evening,
and that difference is the network.

A dome's warm layer — the shell ladder of Chapter
{{ch.shell_ladder}} — is a quilt: fabric patches stuffed with
recycled clothing, cut to the dome's own triangles, stacked one
layer per winter. The technical half of that statement lives in
the shell chapters. This chapter is the social half, and it is
not a footnote: the quilt is the only part of the entire project
that a stranger can take part in from their own kitchen table,
which makes it the campaign's recruiting mechanism, computed
like everything else in this book.

## A layer is an object with a number on it

The arithmetic of one layer, because the quilt's power is that
it is countable.

One quilted layer is a real thing with a number on it: a
definite area of the dome's surface, a definite count of shirts,
a definite R-value, a definite winter. It goes on a named dome,
and it is not a contribution to a pool — it is an object,
addressable, inspectable, whose maker can point at a building
and say *that layer is mine*. The count is the difference
between a movement and a donation drive: a donation dissolves
into a budget line, an object stays an object, and people make
objects.

The layer's economics are the ladder of Chapter
{{ch.hour_at_log}} in miniature: the barrier is a machine and
an evening, not a workshop, a chainsaw or land; the unit is a
layer with a number on it; and the material is the one input
the maker does not have to buy, because textile waste is a
large, boring, well-measured problem and a layer is a
measurable bite out of it.

## Provenance, because the layers come off in order

The third reason the quilt is a network and not a charity:
the dome can name its layers, forever.

The stack wears in order — layer one against the frame, layer
two over it, each winter's addition on top — and it strips in
reverse order. So every layer carries its maker's name and its
date, and the building can state, layer by layer, whose shirts
are in which winter. The provenance is structural, not
sentimental: it comes from the fact that the layers are
separable objects, which is the same removability that makes
the shell ladder an upgrade path. The dome that gets warmer
every winter is, at the same time, a ledger of everyone who
warmed it — and the ledger is the recruiting poster.

## What a stranger's layer does to the economics

The chapter's real claim, stated with the numbers the module
computes.

The quilted layer is the cheapest rung of the shell ladder —
a blanket-quilted layer runs about {{quilt.blanket}} dollars —
and it is the rung whose labour the builder does not have to
perform. A dome's insulation is hundreds of square feet of
fabric work: a week of the builder's own time, or the network's
evenings, spread across people the builder will never meet.
The labour the builder cannot buy at a wage the build can
afford is exactly the labour a network can gift — and the gift
is not charity in the direction it appears, because the maker
gets the one thing the builder cannot give: their name in the
building's winter, provable, permanent, and warm.

The campaign arithmetic that follows — the quilter's kit, the
host pack, the tiers — lives in the stem-cell chapters of Part
Three. This chapter's job is narrower: to establish that the
quilt is not the dome's hobby; it is the dome's economy, and
the network is the economy's engine.

## What is arithmetic here and what is hope

The honest boundary, because the book's rule about numbers
applies to its own campaign.

The shirt counts, the layer areas, the R-values, the prices —
those are arithmetic, computed from declared constants and the
dome's own surface. That people will actually sew them, at the
scale the campaign assumes, is a bet — the book's own modules
label it as such, with the textile-waste figure attributed to
the EPA and the shirt-area figure explicitly marked as
something a person who has actually cut up a hundred shirts
should correct. The network is the project's most hopeful
chapter, and hope is the one material this book refuses to
mislabel: everything here that is a number is a number, and
everything that is a wish is called a wish.
