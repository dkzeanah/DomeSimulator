Create a portrait book-cover illustration, 3:4, for a hands-on building book. The style is cinematic, painterly realism: the warmth of a hand-painted adventure poster with the accuracy of an architectural photograph. It must read clearly at thumbnail size, so use one strong silhouette, high contrast and a simple composition.
The moment. Late afternoon at the end of a working week, in a forest clearing. One person has spent a few days turning a single pine tree into the frame of a small dome cabin, and it is nearly finished. The light is low, golden and raking from the left, with long shadows across the grass. It should feel like quiet pride: the feeling that one person really can build this.
The hero object (centre, middle distance): the half-finished dome frame. A small geodesic dome, exactly half a sphere, about twice as wide as it is tall. It is roughly twenty feet across and ten feet tall, so a standing adult reaches a little over halfway up.

Base and platform. It stands on a low ten-sided timber platform of plain deck boards raised on small pier blocks.
The frame is honest timber. It is made entirely of straight, pale, freshly split pine members, with no metal hubs, no connector plates and no round poles.
Every member is a wedge. Its cross-section is a triangle, like a slice of pie split from a round log. The wide flat face points OUTWARD, forming the outer surface. The sharp point aims INWARD toward the centre of the dome.
How the members meet. Each triangular opening is framed by its own three wedges. Along every line between two openings, two wedges lie side by side, back to back. At each corner the ends lap in a neat pinwheel.
The pattern. The frame is made of flat triangles, never a smooth curve. Short members form five-pointed stars: five stars stand evenly around the lower wall, each centred just below mid-height, with equilateral triangles filling the gaps between them.
It is not finished yet. The lower wall and most of the upper band are complete, but the crown at the very top is still open to the sky. Three or four members are missing there, so the top reads as an unfinished ring against the glowing sky. That gap is the story: one more session and it's done.
Panels. A few triangular openings near the bottom already have flat, pale plywood panels pressed on from outside. The rest are open, so the eye can see through the frame to the trees behind.
The builder (right of centre, standing on the platform). One person, seen from behind and slightly to the side, so the face isn't the focus. They are lifting a single long wedge member up toward the open crown. The wedge's triangular end is clearly visible, and its flat face is turned outward. They wear plain work clothes, leather gloves, safety glasses and ear defenders pushed down around the neck. The figure is small against the dome, which makes the dome feel ambitious but achievable.
The foreground (bottom of the frame, sharp focus): where the frame came from.

The log's end. Lower left, the cut end of a thick pine log lies on the grass, split into eight clean pie-slice wedges that are starting to fan apart. The growth rings are visible, and each wedge's point is the heart of the tree.
The tools. Resting against the log are a froe (a long flat splitting blade with a wooden handle) and a wooden mallet. A chainsaw sits a little further back on the stump, clean and at rest.
The stacks. Beside the log, two neat, tidy stacks of finished wedges: one of slightly longer members, one of slightly shorter ones.
The trail. Scattered fresh wood chips and curls catch the sunlight. A faint trail of chips leads from the log to the dome, linking tree to building.
Background. Tall pines ring the clearing, softly out of focus. The sky above the dome is a warm gradient from amber at the horizon to pale blue at the top, cloudless.
